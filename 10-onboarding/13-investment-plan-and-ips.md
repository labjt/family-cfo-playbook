# 13 — Investment plan and IPS

The investment plan is the first written deliverable of the relationship and the foundation the wealth management plan is later built on. It has two parts — an executive summary written for the client and an investment policy statement that governs how the practice manages money — and it is scoped, peer-reviewed, and revisited every year.

## Purpose

A well-built plan does four jobs: it clarifies and records the client's goals so both sides are working from the same page; it supplies long-term discipline so decisions rest on analysis rather than on reactions to short-term events; it prevents misunderstandings by stating the approach in writing; and it demonstrates the thoroughness of the practice. It is deliberately an *investment* plan, not a financial plan — advanced planning follows through the expert network and is documented in SOP 30.

## Contents

### Part 1 — Executive summary (written for the client; 6–12 pages)

1. **Scope banner** (first page, boxed): who the client is; which households, entities, and trusts this plan covers; what it does not cover and who owns those areas; the mode and scope rung from T05.
2. **Your profile in your words** — the seven areas of the Total Client Profile (values, goals, relationships, assets, advisors, process, interests) summarized from Discovery, goals stated as measurable statements with horizons.
3. **Where you are today** — consolidated picture of assets in scope: entities, accounts, custodians, allocation, concentration, liquidity, cost, tax position at a summary level; held-away assets listed for completeness.
4. **How we work** — the consultative process, the meeting cadence the client chose, the expert network and what it will address next.
5. **Recommendations** — the recommended structure (which accounts/entities, which strategy each, target allocation ranges), how each recommendation maps to a stated goal, what changes from today, transition considerations (taxes, timing, in-kind moves), and what the client must decide.
6. **What it costs** — advisory fee basis and schedule, platform and product costs, any fee-sharing disclosures, in one page.
7. **About [Firm] and the Advisor** — background, investment philosophy in plain language, the team the client will work with (roles, not names, plus the LA by name).
8. **Next steps** — CCM3 date, documents to be executed, the 45-day follow-up, the first progress meeting.

### Part 2 — Investment policy statement (governing document; 8–15 pages)

1. Purpose of the IPS and the parties bound by it.
2. Objectives and constraints per account group: return objective, risk tolerance and capacity, time horizon, liquidity needs, tax considerations, legal and regulatory constraints, unique circumstances (concentrated positions, restricted stock, family entities).
3. Asset allocation policy: strategic ranges, rebalancing rules, permitted and excluded asset classes and vehicles.
4. Investment approach and strategic portfolio process of the practice; roles of the [Firm] platform, the LA, and any third-party managers.
5. Reference material: summaries of index returns and of the hypothetical portfolios the practice recommends — presented as reference, not as a projection of this client's outcome.
6. Monitoring and reporting: what the client receives and when (SOP 24), review cadence, criteria for changing the policy.
7. Per-entity / per-trust addenda where an entity's function differs (T06 addendum table).
8. Signatures and review log.

### Extensions to Bowen's structure

!!! extension "Beyond Bowen"
    **The IPS as a statement of ends.** Write the objectives section so the family could recognize it as *their* decision about what the money is for — not the platform's decision about how to manage it. Goals, horizons, and constraints are the family's; method belongs to the practice.

!!! extension "Beyond Bowen"
    **Multi-entity and trust structure.** One IPS per trust or entity whose function differs (a dynasty trust with a 100-year horizon is not a grandparent's income account). Where a trustee holds investment discretion, the IPS records that the trustee adopts it and cannot delegate ultimate responsibility; where [Trust Co.] is trustee, coordinate through SOP 46 and SOP 71.

!!! extension "Beyond Bowen"
    **Mode A investor-allocation overlay.** Alongside asset allocation, state *who holds what*: higher-growth assets sit with the youngest members and the longest-horizon exempt trusts; lower-growth, income-producing assets sit with the oldest members; intermediate generations take the middle. This is presented as a family decision with counsel involved, never applied unilaterally. Mechanics and preconditions are in SOP 47.

!!! extension "Beyond Bowen"
    **Conversation prompts for the objectives section** (prompts for the family conversation, not prescriptions — portfolio construction belongs to the [Firm] platform): can each holding be explained to the family in fifteen minutes; are fee-heavy or heavily marketed products excluded on principle; does the family want a cadence of one major investment decision every few years rather than constant activity; concentrate to build, diversify to protect — which phase is this family in; what gross return does the family need simply to preserve purchasing power after tax and inflation.

## Standards

- **Voice.** Executive summary in the second person and in the client's vocabulary from Discovery; IPS in the third person and precise. No jargon in Part 1 that is not defined on first use.
- **Truth to source.** Every number reconciles to a statement or tax return in the file; every goal traces to Discovery notes. The AA keeps a reconciliation sheet.
- **Scope discipline.** The scope banner is mandatory; anything outside it is named as outside it, with the owner.
- **No projections as promises.** Reference returns are historical and hypothetical, labelled as such; individual projections, if used, show ranges and assumptions.
- **Length.** Part 1 under twelve pages; Part 2 under fifteen. If it cannot be short, it is not yet clear.
- **Review.** Peer review by a second professional before presentation (sign-off block on T06); [Firm Compliance] review of fees, disclosures, and IPS regulatory items.
- **Format.** T06 outline; [Firm] branding; a one-page summary on top for the notebook (T08).
- **Confidentiality.** Household-level figures never appear in a family-level document without consent recorded on T05/T14.

## Refresh cadence

- **Annual IPS review** (mini-procedure): (1) AA pulls the IPS, the prior year's decisions, and any regulatory changes noted by [Firm Compliance]; (2) LA reviews objectives against the current Total Client Profile and any life events (SOP 22); (3) changes drafted and peer-reviewed; (4) reviewed with the client at the first progress meeting of the year; (5) signed and filed with the review log updated. Trust IPS reviews align to the trustee's annual meeting (SOP 46).
- **Event-driven revision:** liquidity event, major inheritance, sale of a business, change in trustee, change of mode or scope rung, or any material change in goals.
- **Executive summary** is not re-issued annually; it is superseded by the wealth management plan (SOP 30) and the progress-meeting packets.

## Owner

LA owns the document and its presentation; AA drafts and maintains; a peer reviewer (SA or ISB colleague) signs; [Firm Compliance] reviews disclosures.

## Mode A / Mode B

### Mode A — whole family

- One executive summary with a family-level page and a page per household; per-entity and per-trust IPS addenda.
- The investor-allocation overlay is included as a proposal for the family's decision; the family council (where one exists) is the counterparty for family-level policy.
- Family-level policy language (mission, values, long-term growth commitment) may be quoted into the objectives section once the family has adopted it (SOP 43, SOP 45).

### Mode B — individual within a larger team

- Obtain the family's investment policy or the governing trust instrument first; this plan is consistent with it or carves out explicitly, and says which.
- The scope banner states what the family central team owns; the executive summary is delivered to the individual only unless T05 permits more.
- B-full: full plan for the individual's assets. B-partial: plan for the mandated assets plus a coordination note on how it fits the family's policy. B-narrow: IPS for the mandate and a one-page summary only.
