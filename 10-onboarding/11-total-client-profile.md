# 11 — Total Client Profile

The Total Client Profile is the practice's single picture of a client: seven areas captured in Discovery, extended with what a $30M+ family adds (entities, trusts, liquidity events, philanthropy, governance), and kept current at every meeting. The investment plan, the wealth management plan, the expert-network case brief, and the contact plan are all drawn from it.

## Purpose

- Define what a complete profile contains, in what order it is gathered, and how it is recorded.
- Fix the two-level structure used for whole families (Mode A) and the family-context additions used for individuals inside a larger family (Mode B).
- State who may see which parts (confidentiality flags) and when the profile is refreshed.

## Contents

### The seven areas (in this order)

| # | Area | What it captures | Second-order pattern |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Values** | What is truly important to the client about money and life; the "why" behind every goal | Ask what *specifically* about a stated value matters most, and what that would look like in practice |
| 2 | **Goals** | What they want to achieve over the long run — lifestyle, family, enterprise, legacy — with rough timing | For each goal: when, for whom, what "done" looks like |
| 3 | **Relationships** | Who counts: spouse/partner, children and their partners, parents, siblings, close non-family, pets; the family tree as the client describes it | Who depends on whom; who influences decisions; who is not on speaking terms |
| 4 | **Assets** | Everything and where it is held: accounts and custodians, entities, trusts, real estate, operating businesses, concentrated positions, deferred compensation, insurance, debt; income sources | Asked fourth. Who controls each asset; what is held away and with whom |
| 5 | **Advisors** | Every professional relied on — CPA, attorney(s), trustees, other managers, insurance agent, family office — and what has and has not worked with each | What would make them change an advisor; who they trust most |
| 6 | **Process** | How they want to work with the Advisor: channel, frequency, meeting style, who should be present, level of detail, decision style | Honoured for the life of the relationship; recorded verbatim |
| 7 | **Interests** | Hobbies, sports, travel, philanthropy, faith, children's activities — the material of relationship depth | Shared interests noted for future contact (SOP 21) |

### The mind map

- One unlined sheet (or T04 in digital form), the client's name — both names for a couple — at the centre, a branch per area, lines between related items.
- Drawn live, in the client's words, using symbols and short phrases; it is a stimulus for deeper questions, not a form.
- Scanned to the [Document vault] the same day; transcribed into [CRM] fields by the AA within 24 hours.
- Advantages the practice relies on: faster drill-down to what matters, cross-references between areas, client involvement, a one-page review document, and a ready case brief for the expert network.

### UHNW overlay (every profile)

!!! extension "Beyond Bowen"
    Add these nodes under the relevant areas; at $30M+ they are where the planning work lives.

- **Entity and trust map ("trustscape")** — every trust, partnership, LLC, foundation, and company; for each: purpose, trustee/manager, beneficiaries/owners, protector, situs, key terms known, statements received. Who is touched by each and how they feel about it.
- **Concentrated positions and closely held businesses** — size relative to net worth, restrictions, succession intentions, whether [ESOP Co.] or a sale is under consideration.
- **Liquidity events** — past, pending, and possible (sale, inheritance, distribution, vesting), with dates and rough amounts.
- **Philanthropy** — vehicles (DAF, foundation), causes, who is involved, giving history and appetite.
- **Family office / central team** — existence, who leads it, what it does, how the client experiences it.
- **Existing governance** — family meetings, council, constitution, mission statement, family bank; rituals the family keeps.
- **Rising generation** — each member's stage, work, individuation, exposure to the wealth, who is advocating for them.
- **Transitions under way** — family (generation to generation), ownership, board, management; health changes; marriages and divorces.

### Two-level profile (Mode A)

**Family level** (one per family, built through SOP 41):

- Family map — households, members with stage of life, roles (principal, household lead, decision-maker, trustee, beneficiary, elder, council member), affinity members (spouses, in-laws, long-serving advisors).
- Entity and trust map for the whole family.
- Five-capitals summary — human, intellectual, social, spiritual, financial — from the family balance sheet (SOP 44).
- Shared values and goals; what the family says makes it different.
- Transitions under way and the next two expected.
- Full advisor map across all households; existing governance.

**Household level** (one per household): the seven areas plus the UHNW overlay, exactly as for a single client.

### Family context (Mode B)

- **Family-context node** — the client's position in the tree, entities in which they have an interest but no control, information they cannot access, the family's rules they must live within.
- **FCT table** — every member of the family central team by name, function, what they do for this client, contact protocol, and whether they may receive anything from the Advisor (T05 Part 4).

### Confidentiality flags

Every node carries one of three flags: **Shared** (may appear in family-level documents), **Household** (visible to the household only), **Private** (visible to the individual and the Advisor only). Default is Household for a couple and Private for anything told to the Advisor by one adult alone. Flags are set from T05/T14 consent and reviewed at every Rediscovery.

### Downstream uses

| Consumer | What it takes from the profile |
|---|---|
| Investment plan and IPS (SOP 13) | Values, goals, assets, process; entity/trust constraints; scope |
| CCM3 preparation (SOP 14) | Whole profile re-read; open questions from CCM2 |
| Wealth management plan (SOP 30) | All seven areas plus the overlay; the four advanced-planning areas map onto Assets, Relationships, Values, Interests |
| Expert-network case brief (T09) | Anonymised summary of the profile with the overlay; qualitative capitals |
| Contact plan (SOP 21) | Process and Interests; life-event triggers |
| Family discovery (SOP 41) | Household profiles roll up into the family level |
| Asks (SOP 50) | Held-away assets from the Assets area; relationships for introductions |

## Standards

- Captured in the client's words; no advisor interpretation on the map itself (interpretation goes in the [CRM] note).
- Complete: every area has at least one entry before CCM2; "not discussed" is recorded explicitly.
- Dated: every node shows when it was last confirmed.
- Traceable: the source of each asset figure (statement, client estimate, tax return).
- Confidential: flags set before the profile is shared with anyone, including sister-company specialists.

## Refresh cadence

- **Every progress meeting:** changes captured from the "what has happened since we last met" question (SOP 20) and logged within 24 hours.
- **Every Rediscovery meeting (SOP 10 variant):** full redraw with the prior map alongside; at least annually for ideal clients, and always at a mode or scope change.
- **On any trigger in SOP 22** (marriage, birth, death, sale, dispute, health change): the affected nodes are updated within one week.
- **Family level:** refreshed with the family balance sheet every one to three years (SOP 44) and before each annual family meeting.

## Owner

LA owns the profile; AA transcribes and maintains it in [CRM]; CSA files scans and documents. Sister-company specialists receive extracts only, per the confidentiality flags and the scope memo (SOP 71).

## Mode A / Mode B

### Mode A — whole family

- Build the family level through SOP 41 before drafting any family-level plan; household profiles are drawn in each household's own Discovery.
- Consent for what rolls up from a household to the family level is recorded on T14; anything not consented stays at Household or Private.

### Mode B — individual within a larger team

- Add the family-context node and the FCT table; note explicitly what the client cannot access so the investment plan's scope banner (SOP 13) is accurate.
- Nothing from the profile is shared with the family central team without the consent recorded on T05 Part 4, and never with a sister company that sits on that team unless the same consent covers it.
