# 10 — CCM1 Discovery Meeting

The Discovery Meeting is where the practice decides, with the prospective client, whether it can add substantial value — and, if so, builds the Total Client Profile that every later document is drawn from. It is a qualification and a listening exercise, never a sales presentation. The same procedure, run as a **Rediscovery meeting**, refreshes the profile of an existing client.

## Purpose

- Establish trust by positioning the Advisor as an expert with a disciplined process, not a product to sell.
- Uncover what is genuinely important to the client — values first, money fourth — and determine the client's dominant wealth personality (SOP 04).
- Collect everything needed to assess fit, scope the engagement (T05), and draft the investment plan (SOP 13).
- Leave with a precommitment and CCM2 in the calendar.

## Trigger

- Business development hands off a prospective client with T05 Part 1 completed, **or**
- An existing client is due for a Rediscovery meeting (annually, at a mode change, at a major life event, or when the profile is more than 18 months old), **or**
- A member of a Mode A family is being onboarded (SOP 42 calls this procedure).

## Roles

Owner: LA · Does: CSA (pre-meeting letter T01, logistics, photocopying/scanning of documents during the meeting, confirmation), AA (mind-map capture if the LA prefers to stay fully engaged; packet) · Consulted: [Firm Compliance] on recording consent.

## Timing

- Day 0. Ninety minutes maximum; sixty is often enough for a single household.
- CCM2 is scheduled in the room for **+2 weeks**.
- T05 Parts 2–5 completed by the LA within two business days; confirmation and recap within 24 hours.

## Inputs

- T05 Part 1 (handoff minimum): source of the relationship, family context, known advisors, known family office / central team, conflicts check cleared with [Firm Compliance], asset estimate.
- Documents requested in the pre-meeting letter: recent statements for all accounts, last two years of tax returns, trust and entity documents, insurance schedules, any existing investment policy.
- T03 interview guide; blank unlined sheet or T04 outline for the mind map; digital recorder.
- SOP 04 personality cues.

## Prep checklist

- [ ] Pre-meeting letter sent at T−7 with the document list and a two-line description of what the meeting is for (T01).
- [ ] Agenda drafted from T02 and attached.
- [ ] Waiting area free of market television and financial magazines; beverages ready; every team member briefed to greet the client by name.
- [ ] Recording consent wording approved by [Firm Compliance]; recorder tested.
- [ ] LA has read T05 Part 1 and any public information about the family's enterprise; no assumptions written into the profile in advance.
- [ ] For a couple: both partners invited and both names will go at the centre of the mind map.

## Procedure

1. **Greet and settle.** Formal greeting by name from everyone the client meets. Two minutes of genuine interest in the person before any business.
2. **State the purpose — qualify, don't sell.** Explain that the meeting exists to find out whether the practice is the right fit, that it works with a limited number of families for whom it can make a substantial difference, and that if the fit is wrong the Advisor will gladly point them to a better one.

    !!! script "Say something like"
        "Our purpose today is to understand what matters most to you and to be honest with each other about whether we're the right firm for your family. If we're not, I'll say so and help you find who is."

3. **Walk the agenda and ask for additions** (SOP 05 opening). Ask what would make this a valuable meeting for them.
4. **Explain the process and ask consent to record.** Say why: so the Advisor can listen without scribbling. Assure confidentiality whether or not they become a client. If they decline, the AA takes notes.
5. **Conduct the profile interview in Bowen's order** — Values → Goals → Relationships → Assets → Advisors → Process → Interests — using T03. Draw the mind map on unlined paper with the client's name (both names for a couple) in the centre, one branch per area, connecting lines where answers relate. Rules:
    - Ask **second-order questions**: when an answer is general ("security"), ask what specifically about it matters most.
    - Ask about **assets fourth**, never first; hand statements to the CSA to scan while the conversation continues.
    - Under **Advisors**, capture every professional and what has and has not worked with each; under **Process**, capture exactly how and how often they want to be contacted — this preference is honoured for the life of the relationship.
    - Listen for the dominant wealth personality and note it on the map (SOP 04).
6. **Add the family overlay** where the household is part of a larger family or wealth spans generations.

    !!! extension "Beyond Bowen"
        Ask each person present what they are passionate about and who they learn from — the two questions that reveal a calling and whether the person has the means to pursue it. Ask, without judgment, roughly what the family spends each year to preserve and grow its money and what it spends, on purpose, to develop its people; the ratio is a planning conversation in itself. Establish where the family sits in the generational arc (founder generation, siblings, cousins) and which transitions are under way. Choose two or three essential-question topics from T03 that fit this family — shared values, how decisions get made together, how the rising generation is being prepared — and ask them in your own words. Treat the answers as the seed of the family-level profile (SOP 11).

7. **Ask the Mode B mandate questions** when the client belongs to a family that has its own central team: what that team already does for them; what they want from the Advisor that they are not getting; who else will need to know about this relationship; what information they cannot access themselves. Record the family position and the central team by name and function on the Advisors branch.
8. **Assess fit — silently, then aloud.** Fit means: the ideal-client criteria (asset level, complexity the practice is built for, personality compatibility, mandate workability in Mode B), and evidence that the Advisor can make a substantial difference. Avoid the three most demanding personalities as primary clients unless the mandate is narrow and the economics are sound.
9. **If the fit is poor, say so graciously.** Thank them for their candour, explain that another firm would serve them better, name one or two if possible, and offer an introduction. Send a short note the same day. Log the outcome and inform the referring party in a confidentiality-safe way (T01).
10. **If the fit is good, describe the next steps** — the investment plan meeting in two weeks, the commitment meeting a week after that, the follow-up at forty-five days, and the regular progress meetings at the cadence they chose. Ask for a precommitment to move forward.

    !!! script "Say something like"
        "In two weeks I'll bring you a written plan that shows what I would do and why. If it makes sense to you then, we'll meet once more to commit to each other and put it in motion."

11. **Schedule CCM2 in the room.** Confirm the cadence preference they stated under Process.
12. **Close** (SOP 05): summarise what you heard back to them in one minute; ask if anything was missed; thank them for their openness.
13. **Within 24 hours:** written confirmation of CCM2 (T01); mind map scanned to the [Document vault]; T05 Parts 2–5 drafted; [CRM] updated with the profile, personality note, process preference, and advisor map.
14. **Within one week:** the one-week call (SOP 05) to answer questions that surfaced on reflection and to request any documents still outstanding.

### Variant — Rediscovery meeting

Run steps 1–14 for an existing client with these differences only:

- **No qualifying script.** Open by explaining that the practice has deepened how it understands the families it serves and wants to make sure it knows everything that matters now.
- **Compare to the prior profile.** Bring the old mind map; draw a fresh one; mark what changed. Ask specifically about assets held elsewhere and with whom, about advisors added or dropped, and about the rising generation.
- **Outputs:** refreshed profile; a list of held-away asset targets for SOP 50; advanced-planning hooks for the next expert-network case brief (SOP 32); any change to mode or scope on T05.
- **Sequencing rule:** with a sufficient base of existing ideal clients, complete at least ten Rediscovery meetings before any new-client effort — they surface the most value with the least acquisition cost.
- **Next step** is not CCM2 but a refreshed investment plan presented at the next regular progress meeting (SOP 20), or CCM2 if the scope has changed materially.

## Follow-up

- ≤ 24 hours: confirmation of CCM2, recap, [CRM] and [Document vault] updated, T05 drafted.
- ≤ 2 business days: LA completes T05 Parts 2–5; conflicts or consent questions raised with [Firm Compliance].
- ≤ 1 week: one-week call; outstanding documents chased by the CSA.
- Before CCM2: profile reviewed by the LA; investment-plan drafting begins (SOP 13).

## Do / Don't

**Do**

- Keep it to ninety minutes; the affluent have enough friends and not enough time.
- Listen far more than you talk; the map should be in the client's words.
- Record the process preference exactly and honour it forever.
- Practise the mind map on colleagues before running it with a $30M family.

**Don't**

- Don't present products, portfolios, or your firm's history — that is what the investment plan is for.
- Don't ask about money before you have asked about values, goals, and people.
- Don't promise advanced-planning services the expert network cannot yet deliver.
- Don't accept a client whose mandate you cannot actually execute (no information access, no authority to act).

## Mode A / Mode B

### Mode A — whole family

- Discovery with the principal household is the first step; it does **not** substitute for the family-wide discovery. Hand off to [SOP 41](../40-family-layer/41-family-discovery-and-executive-summary.md) for the confidential member interviews and the family balance sheet before drafting a family-level plan.
- Ask who else in the family should be interviewed and who would object; record the answers on T14.
- Both names of a couple sit at the centre of the map; each household in a family gets its own map (SOP 11).

### Mode B — individual within a larger team

- Qualification becomes "is the mandate workable?": can the Advisor get the information, does the client have authority to act on the assets in question, and is there a conflict with a sister company already serving the family?
- The Relationships and Advisors branches must capture the family-tree position and every member of the family central team by name and function.
- Output adds the advisor map and a proposed scope rung on T05; a courtesy notice to the family team is sent only if the client agrees (T01).
- The fee conversation and information-sharing consent are previewed here and settled at CCM3.

## Metrics

- Share of Discovery meetings with T05 Parts 2–5 completed within two business days (target 100%).
- Share of Discovery meetings ending with CCM2 scheduled in the room (target ≥ 90%).
- Rediscovery meetings completed per year against the existing-client base (target: every ideal client every 12–18 months).
