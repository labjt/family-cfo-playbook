# 14 — CCM3 Mutual Commitment Meeting

One week after the investment plan is presented, the prospective client returns to become a client. Both sides commit: the client to the plan and the process, the practice to the client's success. Documents are executed, the engagement is scoped in writing, the client is prepared for the emotions of the first weeks, and the first introductions ask is made.

## Purpose

- Make the commitment mutual and explicit — verbally and in signed documents.
- Execute everything needed to implement the investment plan, cleanly, in one sitting.
- Inoculate the new client against buyer's remorse and the financial media.
- Ask for qualified introductions for the first time.

## Trigger

CCM2 ended with a commitment to move forward and this meeting scheduled. The client has had the plan for a week.

## Roles

Owner: LA · Does: CSA (paperwork package with sign-here flags, engagement letter, T05, checks/transfer authorizations, confirmation), AA (final plan copy; notes) · Consulted: [Firm Compliance] (engagement letter, disclosures, conflicts register); trustee or [Trust Co.] where a trust is a party.

## Timing

- **+1 week after CCM2.** Meeting length 45–60 minutes; longer only if the number of accounts demands it.
- CCM4 scheduled in the room, **about six weeks out** (the "45-day" follow-up).

## Inputs

- Final investment plan and IPS (SOP 13, peer-signed).
- T05 completed and ready for signature; engagement letter drafted from it.
- Account-opening, transfer, and authorization forms per account/entity/trust; beneficiary and titling documents; entity resolutions where needed.
- Fee schedule; conflicts register entries for this client; disclosures.
- Concerns log from CCM2 with written answers.
- Notes on the client's personality and process preference (SOP 04, SOP 11).

## Prep checklist

- [ ] Paperwork package assembled per account with "sign here" flags; every form pre-filled; two-person check completed (SOP 15).
- [ ] Engagement letter states: the named client(s), mode and scope rung, what is in and out of scope, fee basis and schedule, information-sharing consent, how the engagement ends.
- [ ] Conflicts register: any fee-sharing, sister-company involvement, or product relationships listed for disclosure; [Firm Compliance] approval on file.
- [ ] LA has re-read the plan, the Total Client Profile, and every concern raised at CCM2 and can answer without notes.
- [ ] Copy of the plan for the meeting; the client's copy already at home.
- [ ] Agenda (T02 CCM3) sent with the pre-meeting letter (T01); SOP 05 prep complete.
- [ ] Introductions ask prepared: the profile of the family this practice serves best, in one sentence; Mode B variant ready.
- [ ] Recording not used at this meeting; the AA takes decision notes.

## Procedure

1. **Greet and reinforce.** As SOP 05. Acknowledge that the client has had time to think.
2. **Collect all questions first, then answer.** Ask what came up in the week. List every question before answering any; answer them in order, in full.
3. **Confirm readiness.** Ask whether the client is ready to move forward, become a client, and set the plan in motion. If hesitation appears, return to step 2; never push past it.
4. **Walk the engagement letter.** Who the client is, what we will do and not do, how we are paid, what we may share and with whom. Sign T05 alongside it.
5. **Hold the fee conversation properly.** State the fee in dollars and as a rate, in the context of what it buys; confirm the client understands every way the practice or its affiliates are paid; note that this conversation repeats every year.

    !!! script "Say something like"
        "Here is exactly what you pay, in dollars, and what it covers. We'll go over it again every year, and I'd rather you ask about it than wonder."

6. **Disclose conflicts.** Read the conflicts register entries aloud (sister-company involvement, fee sharing, product relationships) and how each is managed. Obtain acknowledgement.
7. **Execute the documents.** Take the time to explain each form before it is signed; collect checks and transfer authorizations now. The CSA checks each signature against the package list before the meeting ends.
8. **Congratulate the client.** Pause. Mark the moment as an important step toward what the client said matters, in their own words from Discovery.

    !!! script "Say something like"
        "You should be pleased with yourself — this is the step most families put off for years. Everything from here is execution."

9. **Coach for the first weeks.** Explain that second thoughts are normal, especially for anyone used to the financial media; suggest treating market commentary as entertainment. Explain that gains arrive unevenly and that missing short periods costs more than staying invested; expect down periods.
10. **Make the introductions ask #1.** Describe the kind of family the practice serves best and ask whether anyone comes to mind; then "who else?"; then ask for a personal introduction. Record names; contact details only after the flow stops (SOP 50).
11. **Set up the 45-Day Follow-up.** Explain its purpose (organizing everything the transfers generate); ask the client to keep every piece of paperwork and bring or upload it; schedule it about six weeks out.
12. **Close.** State what happens next and by when (SOP 15 timeline); thank the client for the trust placed in the practice.
13. **Confirm in writing within 24 hours (T01).** Documents executed, transfers initiated, the CCM4 date, what the client will receive from custodians and what to do with it.

## Follow-up

- Same day: CSA opens T07 for every account; documents scanned to the [Document vault]; originals per [Firm] policy.
- Within 24 hours: confirmation sent; T05 and engagement letter filed; [CRM] updated with commitment date, fee basis, consent, and referral names.
- Within 48 hours: handwritten thank-you for any introduction given; SOP 50 follow-through begins.
- Within one week: the LA calls to check for questions that surfaced after signing.

## Do / Don't

**Do**

- Have every form ready and checked; the client should sign, not hunt.
- Explain each document; speed comes from preparation, not from skipping explanation.
- Say the fee out loud in dollars.
- Ask for introductions plainly and stop after "who else?" — do not sell.

**Don't**

- Don't execute anything the client has not seen explained.
- Don't hide a sister-company relationship or a fee split behind a disclosure page.
- Don't shorten the coaching on remorse and media because the client seems confident.
- Don't leave without CCM4 on the calendar.

## Mode A / Mode B

### Mode A — whole family

- One engagement letter per household plus a family-level letter where the family engages as a unit; T14 (family map, decision rights, confidentiality matrix) signed with T05.
- Where trusts are parties, the trustee signs the trust's IPS addendum and account documents; [Trust Co.] follows SOP 71.
- Frame the commitment as a compact the family enters freely and re-affirms each year (SOP 43); introductions ask #1 becomes "which other members or branches should we be serving?"

### Mode B — individual within a larger team

- Signatories may include a trustee or the family custodian's authorized signer; obtain the FCT's required approvals before the meeting (SOP 33).
- The engagement letter carries the scope banner from the plan and the information-sharing consent; a courtesy copy goes to the FCT only per T05.
- Introductions ask #1 is reframed: ask to be introduced *into the family team* — the family CFO, CIO, trustee, counsel — so coordination starts now. Do not ask for prospects.
- Fee disclosure extends to any fee sharing with a sister company that also serves the family centrally.

## Metrics

- Share of CCM3 meetings held within 5–10 days of CCM2.
- Share of packages executed with zero returned forms (NIGO rate at custodian, target < 2%).
- Share of new clients for whom the introductions ask was made and recorded (target 100%).
