# 16 — CCM4 45-Day Follow-up Meeting

About six weeks after commitment, the transfers have generated a stack of confirmations, statements, and notices. This meeting turns that stack into an organized, explained record the client can use for years; it demonstrates the practice's standard of service; it defuses the first short-term performance number; and it makes the second introductions ask.

## Purpose

- Organize and explain every document the implementation produced, in a notebook designed to last the relationship.
- Place the first 45 days of performance in a lifetime perspective before annualized figures mislead the client.
- Review the plain-language summaries of any new structures.
- Ask for introductions a second time, and schedule the first Regular Progress Meeting at the cadence the client chose.

## Trigger

Scheduled at CCM3 for roughly six weeks out; implementation (SOP 15) has reached day-30 reconciliation.

## Roles

Owner: LA · Does: CSA (notebook assembled, documents indexed, packet), AA (performance context, structure summaries) · Consulted: none usually; trustee or [Trust Co.] if a trust's first statement needs explaining.

## Timing

- **~45 days (six weeks) after CCM3.** Meeting length 45–60 minutes.
- First Regular Progress Meeting scheduled in the room, **about 90 days out** or per the client's stated preference.

## Inputs

- Completed T07 with the day-30 reconciliation signed; exceptions closed or scheduled.
- Notebook (T08) populated: investment plan overview, IPS, wealth-management-plan placeholder, statements and confirmations, executed documents, structure summaries (T21).
- The paperwork the client saved and brought or uploaded.
- First-period performance report with the annualization issue understood by the AA.
- Client's process preference (cadence and channel) from the Total Client Profile.

## Prep checklist

- [ ] Notebook built to the T08 index with permissions set per T05/T14; a physical binder if the client prefers paper.
- [ ] Every custodian document since CCM3 filed and indexed; the client's own stack matched against ours.
- [ ] One-page reading guide for each document type (statement, confirmation, tax notice) ready.
- [ ] Performance figure for the period known; the reframe prepared regardless of whether it is up or down.
- [ ] Structure summaries (T21) final for every new trust, entity, or designation.
- [ ] One small joint decision identified for a family to make together at this meeting (Mode A).
- [ ] Introductions ask prepared; agenda (T02 CCM4) sent with T01; SOP 05 prep complete.

## Procedure

1. **Greet and reinforce.** As SOP 05.
2. **Gather questions first.** Ask what has happened since commitment and what questions the paperwork raised. List them; answer in order.
3. **Explain the notebook.** Walk the sections: why each exists, what goes in it, and how it will grow over five-plus years. Show where the plan, the IPS, and the coming wealth management plan live.
4. **Organize together.** Take the client's saved documents and ours, and place each in its section, explaining how to read it — what a statement shows, what a confirmation confirms, what a tax notice means and who acts on it. The client leaves knowing where everything is and that the practice will keep it that way.
5. **Review the structure summaries.** For each new trust, entity, or designation: what it is, who is involved, what happens next, and the communication plan the client owns (T21). Confirm the client is comfortable with what to tell whom.
6. **Put the first 45 days in perspective.** Before the client reads an annualized figure, explain that six weeks of returns say nothing about a lifetime plan; that no one can call the market short term; and that the plan works by making sound decisions and staying invested through both kinds of period.

    !!! script "Say something like"
        "You'll see a number on this page that looks either brilliant or alarming — it's six weeks annualized, and it tells us nothing. What matters is the goals on page one, and the decisions we make over years."

7. **Make one small decision together (Mode A).** Choose a modest, low-stakes item the family can decide jointly today — a reporting format, a meeting rhythm, a first family-meeting date — so the family banks an early success before harder questions arrive.
8. **Make the introductions ask #2.** Describe the family the practice serves best and ask who comes to mind; "who else?"; ask for a personal introduction (SOP 50). Record names.
9. **Confirm the cadence and schedule the first progress meeting.** Restate the contact preference the client gave at Discovery; confirm it still holds; schedule the first Regular Progress Meeting (about 90 days out or per preference) and explain that it will present the advanced-planning plan.
10. **Close.** Summarize what was organized, what the client should do with future documents (send to us; keep nothing you cannot find), and what comes next.
11. **Confirm in writing within 24 hours (T01).** Next-meeting date, the notebook index as sent, any open items.

## Follow-up

- Within 24 hours: confirmation and index sent; [CRM] updated; introductions recorded and handwritten thanks sent (SOP 50).
- Within one week: the LA calls to catch questions that surfaced after the client re-read the notebook.
- Within two weeks: the AA opens the expert-network case brief (T09) so the advanced-planning plan is ready for the first progress meeting (SOP 32).

## Do / Don't

**Do**

- Design the notebook for the length of the relationship, not for this quarter.
- Explain every document; assume nothing about what the client can read.
- Deliver the reframe whether the number is good or bad — a good number teaches the wrong lesson just as well.
- Keep the meeting about organization and perspective; save advanced planning for the next meeting.

**Don't**

- Don't skip the notebook because the client "isn't a paperwork person" — build the digital version and walk it.
- Don't quote annualized short-period returns as evidence of anything.
- Don't let the introductions ask slide because the client is busy; describe, ask, "who else?", stop.
- Don't leave without the first progress meeting on the calendar.

## Mode A / Mode B

### Mode A — whole family

- The notebook has a family level and a household level; access permissions follow T14's confidentiality matrix.
- The joint decision in step 7 is deliberate and small; record it as the family's first decision under this engagement (SOP 43 builds on it).
- Structure summaries feed the principal's staged-disclosure plan; the LA offers to attend the conversation with the rising generation when asked.

### Mode B — individual within a larger team

- The notebook covers mandated assets only; do not gather or file family-level documents the individual cannot access.
- Agree the reporting format with the FCT before the first progress report so the individual's package fits the family's consolidated reporting (SOP 24, SOP 33).
- Introductions ask #2 stays reframed toward the family team; ask about other members only through the individual and per T05.
- Under B-narrow, this meeting may be a 30-minute call with the trustee to walk the trust's first statement.

## Metrics

- Share of CCM4 meetings held 40–50 days after CCM3.
- Share of clients with a complete, indexed notebook at CCM4 (target 100%).
- Share of CCM4 meetings with the first progress meeting scheduled in the room (target 100%).
