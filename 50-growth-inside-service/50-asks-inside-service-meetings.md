# 50 — Asks inside service meetings

Business development is handled elsewhere. What remains inside the service relationship are two asks that belong to the client experience itself: the invitation to bring held-away assets under one plan, and the invitation to introduce the practice to people the client cares about. Both are made at fixed moments in the sequence, in the same words every time, and both are followed through and thanked whatever the answer.

## Purpose

- Complete the client's financial picture by bringing assets held elsewhere into the plan — most families at this level have several advisors, and the Discovery profile shows exactly what sits where.
- Grow the practice through people the client already trusts, without a marketing effort that would take time away from serving them.
- Do both in a way that deepens the relationship rather than straining it.

## Trigger

- **Part A (held-away assets):** every Regular Progress Meeting; immediately after an advanced-planning win; when the profile or reporting shows assets outside the plan.
- **Part B (introductions):** the Mutual Commitment Meeting (SOP 14), the 45-Day Follow-up (SOP 16), and every Regular Progress Meeting (SOP 20).
- Both only where T05 permits; never in a Discovery meeting.

## Roles

Owner: LA (makes every ask; nobody else) · Does: CSA (thank-you notes, contact records, prospect letters, report-back to the client), AA (held-away asset map from the profile and reporting) · Consulted: [Firm Compliance] for gifts and any referral arrangement.

## Timing

| When | What |
|---|---|
| Before each RPM | AA lists held-away assets and their managers from SOP 11 and SOP 24; CSA confirms on T05 that asks are permitted |
| In the meeting | Asks come after the plan discussion and remaining questions, before scheduling the next meeting |
| Same day | Handwritten thank-you for any introduction; [CRM] record of each ask and outcome |
| Within one week | Each introduced prospect contacted; the client told what happened |
| After any outcome | Thank the client again; gift within the limits set by [Firm Compliance] |

## Inputs

- The Total Client Profile (assets and advisors areas) and the quarterly reporting package.
- T05: asks permitted, and in what form.
- The annual satisfaction survey results (SOP 25) — asks land best when satisfaction is high and are deferred when a service issue is open.
- The practice's one-paragraph description of the families it serves best (from SOP 01), used verbatim in every introductions ask.

## Prep checklist

- [ ] Held-away asset map current: what, where, who manages it, approximate size, any known dissatisfaction.
- [ ] No open service failure with this client (SOP 23); if there is, defer both asks.
- [ ] T05 permits the asks; Mode B limits noted.
- [ ] Ideal-family description ready; handwritten note cards and stamps on hand.
- [ ] [CRM] opportunity records ready to open.

## Procedure

### Part A — held-away assets

1. **Set expectations at every RPM.** Remind the client that the practice cares about the whole picture — total allocation, total cost, total risk — including assets held elsewhere.

    !!! script "Say something like"
        "Part of my job is to keep the whole picture in view, including the accounts we don't manage; may we look at how those fit with the plan?"

2. **Review the relationship.** Ask what the client likes and does not like about working with the practice; use the annual survey (SOP 25) as the formal version. A strong relationship, not performance, is what earns additional assets.
3. **Identify the opportunity from the profile.** Name the specific held-away asset and why bringing it in serves the plan (allocation, cost, tax, simplicity, a single view for the family).
4. **Ask.** Offer a second opinion on how the asset is managed, or ask directly whether the client would like the practice to manage it going forward. Ask when satisfaction is highest — at a progress meeting, or just after an advanced-planning solution has landed.
5. **Thank the client**, whatever the answer, for the trust already placed in the practice.

### Part B — introductions

6. **Set the stage.** Explain, once, that introductions let the practice spend its time serving families rather than marketing, and that the practice would welcome serving people the client cares about.
7. **Ask, using the ideal-family description.**

    !!! script "Say something like"
        "We work best with families like yours — several generations, real complexity, and a wish to keep the family together. Who else comes to mind who might value what we do for you?"

8. **Keep asking** — "who else comes to mind?" — until the names stop. Expect two to five.
9. **Gather background and contact details only after the names stop**, never interrupting the flow.
10. **Ask for a personal introduction** — a call or note from the client so the prospect expects to hear from the practice. At this level a personal introduction is expected; a cold call is not made.
11. **Commit to follow up** on every name, and say how.
12. **Wrap up** by thanking the client and restating that their help lets the practice focus on them.
13. **Same day:** handwritten thank-you note (print if handwriting is poor; email only as a last resort).
14. **Call each prospect within a week.** Goal: a Discovery meeting.
    - *No* (usually "I already have an advisor") → offer a complimentary second opinion on their current plan.
    - *Maybe* (usually cost) → there is no cost for Discovery, and the practice only proceeds where it can add real value.
    - *Yes* → schedule; send the pre-meeting letter (T01) listing what to bring.
15. **Report back to the client** — a short note, mindful of the prospect's confidentiality; silence devalues their effort.
16. **Thank again** with a gift within [Firm Compliance] limits; a dinner for the couple beats dinner with the Advisor.

## Follow-up

- Every ask, outcome, and follow-through is logged in [CRM] the same day.
- Second-opinion and held-away reviews are scheduled as a defined project with an expert where needed.
- The client hears the result of every introduction, whether or not it becomes a relationship.

## Do / Don't

**Do**

- Ask in the same words every time; the consistency is the professionalism.
- Ask when satisfaction is high; defer when a service issue is open.
- Follow through on every name — the client will mention you to them.
- Thank regardless of outcome.

**Don't**

- Don't appear too busy to take on another family; the client will not add to your burden.
- Don't chase introductions that do not fit the practice; judgment, not volume.
- Don't interrupt the flow of names to take contact details.
- Don't ask a prospect for anything; offer them a Discovery meeting or a second opinion.

Common mistakes: not asking; asking once and never again; making yourself seem too busy; failing to follow through; abandoning the ideal-family profile for a large but wrong-fit prospect.

## Mode A / Mode B

### Mode A — whole family

- Consolidation of assets held in trusts goes through the trustee relationship (SOP 46); the ask is made to the trustee and the beneficiaries together, never around the trustee.
- Introductions are framed as other members or branches the family would like the practice to serve, routed through the elders or council where they exist (SOP 45); a new member enters through SOP 42.
- The capacity framing, if used, is about serving the family well, not about scarcity.

### Mode B — individual within a larger team

- Held-away assets: only those within the individual's own discretion and outside the family's central management, and only after confirming on T05 that the mandate welcomes it. Nothing is solicited that the FCT or a sister company of [Firm] manages.
- Introductions: other members through the individual, with the FCT informed as agreed on T05; plus the ask to be introduced into the family team (CFO, CIO, trustee, counsel) so coordination starts.
- The scarcity or capacity framing is dropped entirely.

## Metrics

- Share of RPMs where both asks were made when permitted (target 100%).
- Held-away assets identified versus brought under the plan, per year.
- Introductions received and Discovery meetings held from them, per year.
