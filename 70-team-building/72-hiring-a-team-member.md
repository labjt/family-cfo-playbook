# 72 — Hiring a team member

The practice hires in a fixed order — a Client Service Associate first, a Service Advisor second, an Associate/Analyst third — and only when the triggers in SOP 70 say so. This procedure runs each hire the same way: the role is defined before the advertisement, the search produces real choice, the process is never shortened for a candidate in a hurry, and the offer pays the position rather than the person's history.

## Purpose

- Fill each seat in SOP 03 with someone who is productive within the first quarter and on a path the practice can sustain.
- Prevent the classic first-hire mistakes: the one-horse race, the custom deal, the veiled partnership promise, the relative nobody will manage.
- Keep the client experience stable through the hire — the procedures do not change; who performs them does.

## Trigger

A trigger in SOP 70 is reached or clearly approaching, the five readiness tests pass, and the bogey worksheet on T28 shows the hire is affordable through a bad year.

## Roles

Owner: LA · Does: CSA (postings, scheduling, candidate correspondence, reference logistics — for the first hire, [Firm shared services]), [Firm] human resources where it exists (background checks, offer paperwork, compensation bands) · Consulted: a sister-company leader or the firm's head of advice as a second interviewer; [Firm Compliance] on licensing and non-solicitation language.

## Timing

| Step | Elapsed |
|---|---|
| Role defined and description written (T26) | Week 0–1 |
| Search open; ten qualified résumés | Weeks 1–5 (longer for a Service Advisor — they are scarce) |
| Screens and first interviews | Weeks 4–7 |
| Work sample, checks, second interviews | Weeks 6–9 |
| Offer and acceptance | Week 9–10 |
| Start | Notice period + 2 weeks to prepare onboarding (SOP 73) |

Start the search when the trigger is approaching: a professional takes up to a year to reach full productivity.

## Inputs

- SOP 03 role definition; SOP 70 trigger reading and readiness tests; T28 bogey and capacity worksheet.
- [Firm] compensation bands and, where available, industry salary surveys for the role.
- The library itself — the new person's curriculum and the proof that the recipes exist.

## Prep checklist

- [ ] The role's responsibilities, reporting line, measures of success, required and desirable qualifications written on T26.
- [ ] Internal compensation range set (survey midpoint ±20%, reconciled with [Firm] bands) and the factors that move a person within it.
- [ ] Scorecard weights set for the three dimensions (technical; client relationship; business development) — for a CSA, technical and relationship only.
- [ ] Interview panel named (the LA plus one other; never the LA alone for a professional hire).
- [ ] Sourcing plan: inside [Firm] first, then external channels.
- [ ] Non-solicitation and offer templates reviewed by counsel / [Firm Compliance].

## Procedure

1. **Confirm the trigger and the affordability.** Record the SOP 70 reading, the readiness tests and the bogey result in the hiring file. If the surplus does not cover a bad year, borrow more (SOP 71) and revisit next quarter.
2. **Define the role before the advertisement.** Complete T26: responsibilities; place in the practice and reporting line; key measures of success; required skills and knowledge; required experience; desirable extras. Keep the compensation range internal.
3. **Build the scorecard.** Three dimensions — technical and theoretical knowledge; client-relationship skill; business-development capacity — weighted for the role. A Service Advisor is scored heavily on the first two and lightly on the third; nobody is hired for sales.
4. **Source widely; inside the firm first.** Post inside [Firm] and ask sister-company leaders for candidates; then professional associations, job boards, professional networks, and referrals from colleagues and centers of influence. Do not begin interviewing until there are **at least ten qualified résumés**; if there are fewer, widen the search. Referred candidates submit a résumé and go through the identical process.
5. **Screen résumés against the description**, not against a feeling. Reject early and courteously.
6. **First interview.** Past experience; career goals; proficiency in the professional topics the role needs; communication style. Note the candidate's questions — a candidate who asks about the service process and the career path is signaling seriousness.
7. **Work sample or short case.** For a Service Advisor: a small planning or portfolio case using the practice's own templates (T06/T10) and a five-minute presentation of it. For a CSA: an implementation-tracker exercise and a client-correspondence draft (T01). Score it against the scorecard.
8. **Background check and references.** The background check is mandatory and precedes the second interview. Ask each reference for the name of someone else who worked with the candidate and call them too; unselected references speak more freely.
9. **Optional matching test** for communication and thinking style, used to shape the second interview's questions rather than to decide.
10. **Second interview.** Probe the concerns raised by the sample and the checks; test the ability to work inside a team and to hold a client's confidence; discuss the career path honestly. Answer the equity question with the scripts below and nothing more.
11. **Decide with the scorecard**, not with the calendar. If no candidate clears the bar, reopen the search — a wrong first hire costs more than a late one. If the strongest candidate is a relative or friend, hire only if the practice will manage them exactly as anyone else and they have already built a career elsewhere.
12. **Make the offer in writing.** Title and reporting line; start date; salary within the range; how the incentive works, honestly; benefits and time off as they actually are; non-solicitation language drafted by counsel; the probationary review at 90 days. **Do not haggle** — listen to a well-argued objection and, if valid, reissue a revised offer once. **Pay the position**, not the candidate's blended memory of their best year. **Never shorten the process** because a candidate must decide by Monday.
13. **Complete the start-date checklist.** Accounts and licenses; [CRM] and [Custodian] access at the right level; a desk and the reading order; the first-month meeting calendar with named roles; the onboarding plan (T27). Hand over to SOP 73.

### Candidate archetypes — what each brings and what to check

| Archetype | Strength | Check before hiring |
|---|---|---|
| Entry-level professional | Theory, energy, affordability | A decade from business development; needs the full curriculum and second-chair time |
| Technician (planner, analyst wanting to advise) | Technical depth; slots into many roles | Client skill untested; compensation expectations may exceed the role |
| Existing service professional | The ideal Service Advisor | Rare; ask why they are available; good firms keep them |
| Career changer | Client skill, maturity, sometimes networks | Technical gaps; salary history that will not fit the range |
| Experienced broker or advisor with a book | Capability | Product habits; difficulty sharing clients; this is a merger conversation, not a hire |

### The equity question — how to answer

!!! script "Say something like"
    "Ownership is possible in this practice for someone who becomes a lead advisor and contributes to growth; the criteria will be written and applied the same way for everyone, and I make no promise today."

Never imply a promise you do not intend to keep, and never dodge the question; a clean "not now, and here is what would have to be true" keeps trust.

### Hiring sequence for this practice

1. **CSA** — support track; sits in overhead; removes routine execution from the LA.
2. **Service Advisor** — the first professional; must be able to serve existing relationships independently from the first quarter. Prefer this over a paraplanner if only one professional hire is affordable: the leverage is far greater.
3. **Associate / Analyst** — when relationship size justifies a third professional level and there is enough substantive work for it.

## Follow-up

- Same day as acceptance: welcome note; onboarding plan (T27) started; team and, where relevant, [Firm] leadership informed before any client hears of it.
- Before day one: the first 30 days of client meetings scheduled with the new person's named role in each (SOP 73).
- Unsuccessful candidates told promptly and courteously — some will be colleagues or referral sources later.

## Do / Don't

**Do**

- Write the role first; advertise second; interview third.
- Insist on real choice — ten qualified résumés — even when a good candidate appears early.
- Give the work sample real weight; it predicts better than the interview.
- Hire for genuine warmth as well as competence; clients assume competence and leave over relationships.

**Don't**

- Don't hire someone to watch you work; the role must be productive from the first month.
- Don't custom-compensate; don't blend salary with a payout.
- Don't hire a professional expecting them to bring business.
- Don't skip the background check, ever.

## Mode A / Mode B

### Mode A — whole family

- Families are told about a new team member before they meet them, in the confirmation letter (T01), with the person's role stated.

### Mode B — individual within a larger team

- Where the family's central team must know who has access to the individual's information, the FCT is informed of the new hire per the protocol on T05 before access is granted.

## Metrics

- Qualified résumés per search (target ≥ 10) and weeks to offer.
- New-hire productivity: weeks until the first independently owned deliverable (CSA) or independently run progress meeting (SA).
- Retention at 12 and 24 months.
