# 74 — Team operating rhythm

Once there is a team — even a team of one plus borrowed specialists — the practice runs on a calendar of short, disciplined internal meetings and reviews. This procedure fixes that rhythm: what happens weekly, monthly, quarterly, semiannually and annually; the numbers reviewed at each; the quality-control tools that let the Lead Advisor delegate without exposing clients; and the culture rules the team holds itself to.

## Purpose

- Keep every client meeting, open item and red flag visible to the whole team every week.
- Read the practice's economics on the cadence at which each ratio actually changes.
- Institutionalize quality control so that delegation never lowers the standard.
- Make decisions rather than discussing them, and revisit the ones that have stalled.

## Trigger

Standing calendar. From the first hire onward (and, for the monthly and quarterly reviews, from today with [Firm shared services] in the room where useful).

## Roles

Owner: LA · Does: CSA (agendas from T28, minutes, dashboards, the service-failure log), SA/AA (their sections; peer reviews) · Consulted: [Firm] finance for the P&L; sister-company leaders for the borrowed-expertise pipeline.

## Timing

| Cadence | Meeting | Length |
|---|---|---|
| Weekly | Team meeting | 30 minutes |
| Weekly | One-on-one, LA with each team member | 20 minutes (first year), then biweekly |
| Monthly | Principal review | 45 minutes |
| Quarterly | Quarterly review and state-of-the-team | 2 hours; never cancelled, never rescheduled twice |
| Semiannual | Staffing check | inside the quarterly review |
| Annual | Planning, budget, reviews, compensation, equity conversation | half a day, in the Q4 slot of T15 |

## Inputs

- [CRM] pipeline of scheduled CCM, progress, family and trustee meetings; trackers (T07, T10); red-flag list (SOP 22); service-failure log (SOP 23).
- Monthly P&L for the profit center from [Firm] finance; hours logs (T28).
- Borrowed-expertise engagements in progress (T25).

## Prep checklist

- [ ] Agendas from T28 circulated the day before; anyone may add an item.
- [ ] Dashboard figures updated for the monthly and quarterly meetings.
- [ ] Peer-review queue current: every plan or proposal due in the next two weeks has a reviewer named.

## Procedure

1. **Weekly team meeting.** Walk the next two weeks of client meetings — who attends with what named role, packet status, anything the client must be told beforehand. Then open service items and who has the ball; red flags since last week; implementation tracker exceptions; borrowed engagements due. Decisions recorded with owner and date; nothing is "discussed" twice without a decision.
2. **Weekly one-on-ones.** Progress against the onboarding grid or the quarter's targets; one correction, one thing to do more of; blockers the LA must remove.
3. **Monthly principal review.** EBOC and overhead percentage against the bands in SOP 70; the service-failure log — every entry traced to a step in an SOP that was missing, unclear or skipped; the pipeline of borrowed-expertise engagements and their scorecards; hours by professional. Fifteen minutes on the numbers, thirty on what to change.
4. **Quarterly review.** Common-sized P&L against the 40/35/25 guideline; capacity check on T28 against the SOP 70 triggers; the SOP review with SOP 60 (which procedures changed, which failed); client review for fit, risk and unpriced customization; and the **state-of-the-team** conversation — honest, no agenda item skipped: what is working, what is not, what each person needs.
5. **Semiannual staffing check.** Revenue per staff head and per professional; relationships per professional; the roadmap block in SOP 70 updated; a hiring decision made or explicitly deferred with a date.
6. **Annual cycle (Q4).** Budget built by cost owner — each person drafts the lines they spend — and loaded so budget-versus-actual can be reviewed monthly; performance reviews against stated standards *before* compensation decisions; bonus frequency matched to review frequency; the equity conversation held even if there is nothing to decide; the porch-of-indecision list reviewed (hiring, succession, compensation structure, pricing, the firm relationship) and each item given a decision or a date.
7. **Quality-control tools, always on.** Peer review and sign-off on every investment plan and wealth management plan before presentation; LA sign-off on proposals and client documents; one meeting per year on every relationship attended by a second professional (rotation) so another set of eyes sees the work and the client; random review of a sample of portfolios and files each quarter.
8. **Productivity measures** kept per professional in [CRM]: relationships owned; second-chair relationships; revenue managed; growth of managed relationships; organic growth from existing clients; new relationships served. Reviewed quarterly; they feed the promotion tests (SOP 73) and, later, incentives.
9. **Decision hygiene.** Written agendas; decide rather than defer; a materiality threshold below which each person acts alone (SOP 03) and above which the item comes to the meeting; disagreements resolved in the room, not agreed-to-disagree; hard decisions supported by a one-page analysis.
10. **Culture rules, enforced by example.** The team owns the client experience; "our" clients, never "my"; no puck-passing; no exceptions for brilliance; leaders arrive prepared and on time; critics are brought inside and given real roles; small joint successes are engineered before hard problems are attempted.

!!! extension "Beyond Bowen"
    Bowen supplies the standard (WOW service, red flags, blueprints improved by staff). The cadences, ratios, quality-control tools and culture rules are drawn from Palaveev and Hughes, adapted to a one-professional practice that is growing.

## Follow-up

- Minutes with decisions and owners sent the same day; the dashboard (T28) updated before the next meeting.
- Service-failure lessons that point to an SOP are logged to SOP 60 within the week.
- Quarterly review actions checked at the next monthly review.

## Do / Don't

**Do**

- Keep the meetings short and the agendas written; decide.
- Review the numbers on their natural cadence — EBOC monthly, staffing semiannually.
- Treat every service failure as evidence about a procedure.
- Hold the state-of-the-team review even when things seem fine — especially then.

**Don't**

- Don't cancel the quarterly review; "we talk all the time" is not a substitute.
- Don't judge different people by different standards (effort for the familiar, results for the new).
- Don't let one person's excellence excuse behavior the team would not accept from anyone else.
- Don't leave decisions on the porch for years; a non-decision is a decision.

## Mode A / Mode B

### Mode A — whole family

- The weekly walk includes family meetings and trustee meetings with the attendee list checked against the family's confidentiality matrix (T14).

### Mode B — individual within a larger team

- Items concerning a Mode B client are discussed only among team members inside the consent on T05; the borrowed-expertise pipeline shows the wall status for any sister company that also serves the family.

## Metrics

- Quarterly reviews held as scheduled (target 4 of 4).
- Plans and proposals with a recorded peer review before presentation (target 100%).
- Service failures traced to an SOP change within the month (target 100%).
