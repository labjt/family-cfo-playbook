# 73 — Onboarding a team member and transitioning relationships

A new hire earns the client's trust the same way the Advisor did: by doing visible, competent work in the room. This procedure runs the first ninety days on a plan, puts the new person in every client meeting with a named role, gives them a real mix of relationships rather than the leftovers, and defines the two tests that decide when a relationship moves from the Lead Advisor to the Service Advisor — without the client ever feeling handed down.

## Purpose

- Reach productivity fast: a CSA owning execution within a month, a Service Advisor leading routine meetings within six.
- Make the team, not a person, the client's experience — while honoring every client's stated contact preference.
- Grow the first professional into a relationship owner, so the practice is not permanently capped at the Advisor's hours.

## Trigger

An accepted offer under SOP 72; or an existing team member changing seat (for example CSA to operations lead); or a borrowed specialist becoming a standing part of the team.

## Roles

Owner: LA · Does: the new hire (the plan is theirs to execute), CSA (accounts, calendar, introductions in correspondence), SA (buddy for an AA) · Consulted: [Firm] HR and [Firm Compliance] for access and licensing.

## Timing

| Window | Focus |
|---|---|
| Before day one | Access, reading order, first-month meeting calendar with named roles |
| Days 1–30 | Curriculum; shadow with a role in every meeting; owns notes and follow-through |
| Days 31–60 | Presents one defined analysis per meeting; owns a project; CSA owns the tracker |
| Days 61–90 | Second chair on large relationships; SA owns a first set of smaller relationships; 90-day review |
| Months 4–12 | Progression against the two tests; rotation meeting; business-development apprenticeship begins |
| Month 12 | Full productivity expected; relationship-manager test on the target number of relationships |

## Procedure

1. **Before day one — set the stage.** Send the welcome note and the reading order. Book the first-month meetings with a named role in each. Tell the team, and any family that will meet the new person in month one, before they arrive.
2. **Day one — the welcome.** Introduce the new person to every team member and to the borrowed-bench contacts they will work with. Walk the library's map (README) and the role's SOPs. Explain the two promotion tests and the 90-day review so the path is visible from the first day.
3. **Days 1–30 — learn by doing in the room.** The new professional attends **every** client meeting with a named role from the menu: owns the notes and the follow-through on decisions; or presents one defined analysis; or is introduced as the named specialist for one topic; or owns one project (for example collecting cost basis for a transfer). Introduce them each time by name, role, and what they will do today — and promote their skills without hedging. A CSA shadows the LA on correspondence and paperwork, then takes the tracker (T07) and confirmations by week three.

    !!! script "Say something like"
        "Sam trained in planning and will be tracking every decision we make today and making sure each one happens; from now on you'll hear from Sam on the follow-through."

4. **Days 31–60 — visible ownership.** The SA presents one analysis per meeting and owns a project with a client-facing deliverable; the AA owns packets and case briefs; the CSA owns the meeting cycle end to end (SOP 05) with the LA reviewing. Weekly one-on-one with the LA; corrections given in private, credit given in front of the client.
5. **Days 61–90 — the mix.** Give the SA a genuine mix: a few smaller relationships to own outright *and* second chair on the largest, most complex ones — never only the small clients. Record second-chair status in [CRM]. Update each affected client's contact plan (SOP 21) and confirmation letters: who to call for what, with the LA still the first name for anything strategic.
6. **The 90-day review.** Score against T26's measures; confirm probation; set the next-quarter targets; ask the new person what in the library was unclear — and fix the SOP (SOP 60).
7. **Months 4–12 — progression.** Move routine progress meetings from co-attended to SA-led with the LA present to SA-led alone, relationship by relationship, gated by the **relationship-manager test**: the SA runs most scheduled meetings independently; the client calls the SA first; the client brings new plans and life changes to the SA; the client gives the SA both praise and complaints; the client asks the SA for more resources. When all five hold, record the SA as relationship manager and the LA moves to quality control on that relationship.
8. **Start the business-development apprenticeship in year one** even though results are years away: teach opportunity recognition with existing clients first (held-away assets, planning openings — SOP 50); bring the SA to the LA's development conversations to observe; introduce them to referral sources; push them into professional and community bodies early.
9. **The relationship-owner test (later).** The SA conducts *all* meetings including strategy and portfolio decisions; the client seeks no other senior professional's advice; the SA has brought new assets or services into the relationship; the SA can delegate parts of the work to others with the client's acceptance. When all four hold, the relationship is recorded as owned by the SA — owned meaning lead responsibility; every relationship belongs to the practice.
10. **Quality control throughout.** The LA reviews every plan and proposal before it is presented (SOP 74); one meeting a year on each SA-led relationship is attended by the LA or another professional; random reviews of documents and portfolios continue.
11. **Guard against the stall.** If by month 12 the first professional shows no movement toward the relationship-manager test, decide deliberately: more coaching with a dated target, or a change. A stalled first hire blocks the practice's growth and every later hire.

!!! warning "The process preference still governs"
    A client who asked in Discovery to hear only from the Advisor keeps that until they say otherwise. Team introductions are offered, not imposed; a few reluctant clients are not grounds for abandoning the model.

## Follow-up

- Weekly for the first quarter: one-on-one with the LA; the onboarding grid (T27) updated.
- After each client meeting in the first quarter: two-minute debrief on what the new person did well and what to change.
- Month 6 and month 12: progression reviewed against the two tests; contact plans updated; [CRM] second-chair and relationship-manager fields current.

## Do / Don't

**Do**

- Put the new professional in every meeting from week one, with something real to do.
- Promote their skills to the client; uncertainty on the Advisor's face becomes the client's avoidance.
- Give large, complex relationships as second chair — they carry the budget and the lessons.
- Tell clients about personnel changes before they hear it from a competitor or a returned call.

**Don't**

- Don't let them merely observe; observation teaches little and signals less.
- Don't hand over only the small clients.
- Don't expect business development from a professional a decade short of it.
- Don't transfer a relationship because the calendar says so; the tests decide.

## Mode A / Mode B

### Mode A — whole family

- New attendees at family meetings are cleared against the family's confidentiality matrix (T14) and introduced to the council or principals first; consider a welcome ritual when a new adviser joins a family's circle for the long term.

### Mode B — individual within a larger team

- Access to the individual's information for a new team member is granted only within the consent on T05; the FCT is told of the change per the agreed protocol before the new person appears on correspondence.

## Metrics

- Weeks to the CSA's first independently run meeting cycle; months to the SA's first independently led progress meeting (target ≤ 6).
- Relationships passing the relationship-manager test at 12 and 24 months.
- 90-day review completed on time; SOP corrections raised by new hires (a healthy number is not zero).
