# 71 — Engaging borrowed expertise

The practice serves $30M+ families with one professional today. It can do so because the firm's sister companies — [Trust Co.], [ESOP Co.], sister RIAs, tax, insurance and lending specialists — and [Firm shared services] can be borrowed per matter. This procedure makes that borrowing systematic: scoped in writing, walled where it must be, credited fairly, delivered through the Lead Advisor, and logged so it feeds the capacity model. It covers outsourced paraplanning and back-office vendors the same way.

## Purpose

- Put the right expertise in front of a family within days, without the client ever feeling handed off.
- Keep the relationship, the decisions and the accountability with the LA while the work is done by someone else.
- Protect confidentiality inside a firm whose sister companies may already serve the same family in another capacity.
- Make every engagement improve the bench roster in SOP 31.

## Trigger

Any of: a Total Client Profile gap, a tracker item (T07/T10), an expert-network meeting output (SOP 32), or a client request that needs expertise the LA does not hold or hours the LA does not have — or a routine function (paraplanning, reporting, transfers) that the practice has chosen to outsource.

## Roles

Owner: LA · Does: CSA (request logistics, memo circulation, hours log, roster update), AA (data pack for the specialist) · Consulted: ISB or vendor specialist; [Firm Compliance] on disclosure, fee sharing and walls.

## Timing

| Step | When |
|---|---|
| Request and resource choice | Within 2 business days of the trigger |
| Scope memo agreed (T25) | Within 5 business days |
| Client introduction | Before the specialist sees any client data beyond what the memo allows |
| Delivery and LA sign-off | Per the memo; never later than the next scheduled client meeting it feeds |
| Debrief and scorecard | Within one week of delivery |
| Standing-vendor review | Annually, with the external-relationship review in SOP 31 |

## Inputs

- The item that triggered the need, with the client's TCP context (SOP 11) and T05 (mode, scope, consent).
- The bench roster and scorecards (SOP 31); the firm's policy on internal referrals, credit and fee sharing.
- Any existing relationship the sister company has with this family (checked in [CRM] and with the sister company).

## Prep checklist

- [ ] Need stated in one sentence: what question must be answered, for whom, by when.
- [ ] T05 consulted: is this matter in scope, and who may receive the client's information?
- [ ] Conflict and wall check: does any sister company already serve this family or its central team?
- [ ] Resource chosen (internal bench / external network / the client's own advisor) using the rule below.
- [ ] T25 drafted.

## Procedure

1. **State the need and choose the resource.** Write the one-sentence need. Apply the rule: use the **internal bench** when the expertise exists in the firm and independence is not compromised; use the **external network** when independence matters (for example insurance, or a second opinion on a sister company's own product), when the expertise is missing internally, or when the client prefers it; use the **client's existing advisor** when they already have a competent one — coordinate under SOP 33 rather than duplicate.
2. **Clear conflicts and declare the wall.** Ask the sister company whether it serves this family in any capacity. If it does — especially if it sits on a Mode B family's central team — record the confidentiality wall on T25: which people may see which information, and that nothing learned for this engagement flows to the sister company's other relationship with the family. Confirm with [Firm Compliance] where a wall is needed.
3. **Write the scope memo (T25).** Deliverable, timeline, data the specialist will receive, whether the specialist is client-facing, who signs off, and the credit or fee basis. A memo of five lines is enough for a small matter; no matter proceeds without one.
4. **Agree credit and compensation, then disclose.** Follow [Firm] policy. Where internal credit is paid for a referral it is a modest share of first-year revenue (norms in the industry cluster around 5–15%) and always below what an external referral source would receive; legal fees are never shared. Disclose to the client in writing what is being paid to whom, through [Firm Compliance]. Investment-management fees for the Advisor's own work are priced as the premium for coordinating everything else, not padded by the specialist's charge.
5. **Introduce the specialist to the client** — by name, role, and what they will do for this family, in the meeting or in the confirmation letter (T01). State plainly that the LA remains the client's point of contact.

    !!! script "Say something like"
        "For the succession question I've asked our firm's ESOP team to model the options; you'll meet Dana at our next meeting, and everything still comes back through me."

6. **Supply the data pack.** The AA or CSA sends only the information the memo allows, in the format the specialist needs, with the client's consent noted on T05.
7. **Deliver through the LA.** The specialist works to the LA unless the memo names them client-facing for a defined part. Questions from the client go to the LA; the LA answers or brings the specialist in. Whoever receives a question owns it to completion — nobody is "put through to someone."
8. **Quality check and sign-off.** The LA reviews the deliverable for fit with the family's profile and the plan before anything reaches the client. Complex items are presented with the specialist in the room (SOP 20).
9. **Debrief and score.** Within a week: what was delivered, on time or not, how the client responded, what would be done differently. Score expertise, integrity, professionalism, chemistry, timeliness and client feedback on T25; update the bench roster in SOP 31.
10. **Log the hours.** Record borrowed hours against the relationship in [CRM] and on T28 so that capacity planning in SOP 70 counts what the bench is carrying — that number is part of the case for the next hire.
11. **Review standing arrangements annually.** For any vendor or sister-company service used routinely, ask the six questions: is their strategy a fit with ours; exclusive or one of several, and what does exclusivity buy; how concentrated is our dependence and does it buy better service; how durable are they (ownership, exit risk); how much will they customize; how will clients perceive them if they look them up.

!!! warning "Anti-captivity"
    Own the process even when the platform or a sister company does the work. Keep the data, the templates and the client relationship in the practice's own systems so that the service survives a change of vendor or a reorganization inside the firm.

!!! extension "Beyond Bowen"
    Bowen's expert network assumes external free agents. The internal-bench protocol, the confidentiality wall inside a federated firm, the credit norms and the capacity log are our own, informed by Palaveev's profit-center and vendor rules.

## Follow-up

- Within 24 hours of delivery: the client receives the LA's summary and knows the next step.
- Within one week: debrief held, scorecard filed, roster updated, hours logged.
- Annually: standing vendors and bench members reviewed together with the external network (SOP 31).

## Do / Don't

**Do**

- Use the internal bench generously — it is the practice's competitive edge — but scope every engagement in writing.
- Tell the client who is being paid what, before the work starts.
- Keep one thread: the LA is the voice to the client; the specialist speaks in the room with the LA present.

**Don't**

- Don't let a sister company become the family's primary contact through the back door.
- Don't share a client's information with a sister company that serves the family elsewhere without the wall declared and the client's consent.
- Don't accept a deliverable you cannot explain to the client yourself.
- Don't build the practice's process inside a vendor's system.

## Mode A / Mode B

### Mode A — whole family

- The bench and the external network are coordinated by the LA as the family's personal CFO; the family's incumbent advisors are brought in under SOP 33, not replaced by the bench.
- Where the family has a council, the LA tells the council which specialists are engaged on family-level matters.

### Mode B — individual within a larger team

- Check first whether the sister company already serves the family's central team; if so the wall on T25 is mandatory and [Firm Compliance] signs it.
- The family's counsel and trust company are the default experts for family-level matters; the bench fills gaps for the individual's own affairs only, with the FCT aware as agreed on T05.
- Credit and fees are disclosed to the individual and, where the FCT gatekeeps professional relationships, to the FCT.

## Metrics

- Share of borrowed engagements with a scope memo before work started (target 100%).
- Borrowed hours per quarter, by relationship (feeds SOP 70 triggers).
- Average scorecard rating of bench members; number of members below threshold acted on within a quarter.
