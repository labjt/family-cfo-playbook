Set Up the Practice

Do these steps in order and the practice runs itself. Each one is a few minutes of clicking and pasting. You do not need to read anything else first.

Read this first

2 minutes

Start here 2 minutes

You are not learning a system. You are installing one.

The written playbook has 38 procedures. You are not going to memorise them. You are going to paste most of them into Hazel, and Hazel will run them for you.

How this works

  • Every step below is a few minutes of clicking and pasting. Nothing needs to be written by you.
  • Tick a step when it is done. Your progress is saved in this browser.
  • Do them in order. Each phase ends with something that actually works.
  • You can stop after any phase and come back. Phase 1 alone is worth doing.

What you will have at the end

  • Hazel prepares an agenda before every client meeting, sitting on your calendar.
  • Hazel joins the meeting, takes the notes in your format, and drafts the follow-up email.
  • The seven-area client profile fills itself in from meetings, email and your CRM.
  • Your planning method, written down once, applied to every plan Hazel drafts.
  • You review and approve. Hazel never sends anything to a client on its own.
The honest limit

Hazel has no idea that a Discovery meeting comes before an Investment Plan meeting. It has no concept of a sequence and it will not stop you skipping a step. The order lives in your head and in the Playbook — Hazel carries the execution, not the discipline.

Before you paste anything

Hazel's screens change often and their own documentation currently contradicts itself in a few places. If a button is not where a step says it is, look for the nearest thing with a similar name — the sequence is right even when a label has moved.

Sign up as the Owner, and say yes to the calendar.

You become the person who can manage billing, roles and firm settings. Everyone you add later inherits the structure you set now.

Do this

  3. Click Get started, then choose the base plan. You can add the tax and planning agents later.
  4. Pick monthly or annual, add a payment method, and start the 14-day free trial.

The one-shot prompt

The calendar consent screen appears once, on your first sign-in. If you dismiss it, Hazel never asks again — you have to go and find it in settings, and until you do, meeting prep and agendas barely work. Say yes the first time.

Why not invite the team yet

New members get access to everything imported from your CRM by default, and your CRM's permissions do not carry across. For a book of $30M families that is a confidentiality problem waiting to happen. Set up client access properly first — that is a later step.

Done when

Step 3 12 minutes

Connect the CRM first. Everything else depends on it.

The CRM is what creates your client list inside Hazel. Without it you have an empty product — the custodian does not supply contacts, and email history will not backfill.

Do this, in this order

  1. Go to Settings → Integrations.
  2. Connect your CRM. Expect a message saying the sync may take ten to fifteen minutes. Wealthbox, Salesforce, Redtail, HubSpot and Quivr are supported.
  3. Connect email and calendar if the sign-in prompt did not already do it. Hazel pulls three years of past email, but only for people who exist as contacts in your CRM — which is why the CRM goes first.
  4. Connect Altruist if that is where you custody. Review the Data Access Consent screen and click Agree & Connect. Access is read-only: Hazel can never trade or move money.
  5. Check each row shows a green dot.
Before you connect the CRM

Hazel only imports records that pass its filters. In Wealthbox that means active people and households marked as client or prospect; other systems have their own rules. If a family is filed oddly in your CRM, it will simply not appear in Hazel. Ten minutes tidying the records of your top twenty households now saves a lot of confusion later.

If you do not custody at Altruist

Everything else still works — meetings, notes, profiles, planning, chat over your CRM and email. You lose live balances, holdings, transactions and beneficiary lookups. Altruist is currently the only custodian Hazel connects to; Schwab and Fidelity are listed as coming, with no date.

Done when

Your households appear under Contacts, and you can open one and see its details.

Step 4 6 minutes

Set the four defaults that touch compliance.

These decide what gets recorded, what gets kept, who can see it, and what appears at the bottom of anything a client reads. Set them once, deliberately, before the first real meeting.

Do this

  1. Go to Settings → Meeting Settings.
  2. Confirm Auto-schedule Notetakers is on, and set Bot Join Minutes Ahead to 5 so the notetaker is waiting before you are.
  3. Leave Bot Name as Hazel Notetaker. Renaming it to something less obvious undercuts the fact that clients can see a recorder in the room — do not do it.
  4. Review Save Videos and Save Transcripts against what compliance told you. Both are on by default and changes only apply to future meetings.
  5. Check the default meeting visibility. It ships as Contact access — anyone who can see the client can see the meeting.
  6. Find the client disclaimer setting and write one. It is empty until somebody fills it in, and an empty one means client-facing documents go out with no footer at all.
Phone calls record silently

If you turn on phone recording, understand what it does: the announcement plays only to the phone that dialled in. A client merged into the call afterwards hears nothing. Nobody is told the call is being recorded unless you say so out loud. If you use this at all, make the disclosure a spoken habit at the start of every call.

Done when

All six are set, and you can state from memory what gets recorded, what gets stored, and who can see it.

Agenda

Paste into “Agenda”
You are preparing a financial advisor for a first meeting with a prospective family with substantial wealth. This is meeting one of a five-meeting process. Its only purposes are to understand what matters to this family and to decide whether the firm is a fit. Nothing is being sold at this meeting.

Prepare a one-page agenda for 90 minutes, covering these seven areas in this exact order:
1. Values — what is genuinely important to them about their money
2. Goals — what they want to achieve, and by when
3. Relationships — who matters to them, including people outside the family
4. Assets — what they hold, where it sits, and who manages it
5. Advisors — every professional they use, and what has and has not worked
6. Process — how and how often they want to be contacted
7. Interests — what they care about outside of money

Key Instructions:
1. Do not reorder these areas. Assets appear fourth on purpose; opening with money signals selling rather than listening.
2. Include no product, performance, market or portfolio discussion. This meeting has none.
3. Using the CRM, email history and any connected custodial data, list what is already known about this household under each of the seven headings, and mark clearly what is still unknown. The unknowns are the real agenda.
4. Name every professional already on record — attorney, accountant, trustee, insurance — so the advisor can ask what has and has not worked with each.
5. If anything suggests this household belongs to a larger family that has its own family office, trust company or lead advisor, add three questions about what that team already handles and what this client wants from this advisor instead.
6. End with two items: agree next steps, and schedule the second meeting two weeks out.
7. Short headings and bullets. No paragraphs. One page.

Reminder email

Write a short reminder email to a prospective family before a first meeting, sent by the advisor.

Key Instructions:

Meeting notes

9. Interests: what they care about outside money.

Follow-up email

Paste into “Follow-Up Email”
You are an expert financial communication assistant. Write a follow-up email to a prospective family after a first meeting, sent by the advisor.

Key Instructions:
1. Between 150 and 250 words. Professional but warm, unhurried, and entirely free of sales language.
2. Open by thanking them for their openness and naming one specific thing they said that mattered — a goal, a person, a value. Never a generic opener.
3. In one short paragraph, play back the two or three things the advisor heard matter most to them. This proves you listened and is the whole point of the email.
4. Say what happens next and when: the advisor will bring a written plan in two weeks, on the date agreed in the meeting.
5. List anything they agreed to send as a short plain list under the line "To send when you get a chance:".
6. Discuss no products, performance, markets or fees.
7. Use no bold and no italics anywhere except the line "To send when you get a chance:".
8. Sign off simply, with no marketing footer.
Why assets come fourth

Opening a first meeting with money tells a family you are selling. Opening with what they care about tells them you are listening — and it is the only order that reliably surfaces the things that actually drive the plan.

Phase 2 · Make the client record fill itself

about 25 minutes

Step 7 15 minutes

Upload the paperwork for your top ten families.

Facts that come from a document arrive already confirmed. Ten minutes of uploading gives a family a trustworthy record before you have had a single meeting.

Do this, for each of your ten largest relationships

  1. Open Contacts, pick the household, open its Documents tab.
  2. Drag in the core packet: most recent custodial statements, the latest tax return, insurance declaration pages, and the estate documents — the will and any trust deeds.
  3. Wait for each file to reach the green In profile state. Files are read one at a time per household.
  4. For anything older, check the date on the document row and set it by hand if Hazel guessed. Newer facts overwrite older ones, so a mis-dated old statement can overwrite something current.
  5. Open the Data tab, spend two minutes in Review, and assign an owner to anything sitting under Needs owner.
Export, do not scan

Pull PDFs straight from the custodian portal or the tax software. Scanned or photographed pages read badly and are the most common cause of a failed upload. Limit is 10 MB per file.

Uploading to the household vs the person

A document on the household is shared with everyone in it. A document on an individual is linked only to them. That single choice is the only privacy control you get inside a family record — see the warning in the next step.

Done when

Ten households each show documents In profile, and their Data tabs have real content in the accounts, insurance and estate sections.

Step 8 10 minutes

Change four small habits and the record maintains itself.

The profile only learns from what it can hear. These four habits are the difference between a record that grows on its own and one you end up typing.

The four habits

  1. Say the number, the institution and the owner out loud. "Her rollover IRA at Fidelity, about one-point-two." A figure that is only on a screen cannot be captured, and "the IRA" with no owner named goes into a queue for you to sort out later.
  2. Get the "no" on the record. "So — no long-term care cover today, correct?" turns a silence into a recorded, confirmed fact. Absences matter as much as holdings.
  3. Recap for sixty seconds at the end of every meeting. It is the cheapest way to make sure the important things were actually said.
  4. Spend two minutes in Review afterwards. Verify what you would act on — not everything. Financial figures heard in conversation start unconfirmed until somebody confirms them.

Where the rest of the profile goes

Hazel's client record has six fixed sections and you cannot add your own. Four of your seven areas fit — goals, assets, advisers and the estate structure. Three do not: values, process preferences and interests. Put those in a note on the household, where Hazel will read them and use them in chat, prep and agendas.

Paste as a note on each household, then fill it in
VALUES — what they told us matters most about the money, in their words:

GOALS BEYOND THE NUMBERS — what the money is actually for:

HOW THEY WANT TO BE WORKED WITH — channel, frequency, who to contact, what to never do:

INTERESTS AND PEOPLE — what they care about outside money; who matters to them:

FAMILY POSITION — which generation; who else in the family we serve; who their other advisers are:

CONFIDENTIAL — anything a member asked us not to share, and who it must not reach:
Read this before you write anything sensitive

Access to a household in Hazel is all or nothing. Anyone who can see the family can see the whole record, every document and every meeting — and firm Owners can always see everything. There is no per-person privacy inside a household. If something genuinely must not reach another family member or another adviser, do not put it in the shared household record at all. This is a hard limit of the product, not a setting you have missed.

Done when

Your ten households each have the note above filled in, and you have caught yourself saying an account owner's name out loud in a real meeting.

Title and description

Paste into “Title”, then “Short description”
Advanced Planning Review

Reviews one family across wealth enhancement, transfer, protection and giving, and returns a status table per area with the five things to do next.

The instructions

Paste into “Detailed instructions”
You are in Advanced Planning Review mode.

Your job is to review one family across the four permanent areas of advanced planning and produce a written review the advisor will take into an expert-network meeting. Produce four tables and one closing list, in the order set out below, and nothing else. No cover paragraph, no summary before the tables, no closing pleasantries.

Work only from the household's profile, its uploaded documents, its meeting history, and any file attached to this conversation. Where the record does not establish something, say so in the row. Do not fill a gap with what is typical.

The four areas, in this order, with these definitions:
1. Wealth enhancement — the best return consistent with the family's risk tolerance, after tax and after fees.
2. Wealth transfer — passing assets to the next generations tax-efficiently, and in the way the family actually wants.
3. Wealth protection — shielding the family from creditors, litigants, divorcing spouses and catastrophic loss.
4. Charitable planning — carrying out the family's giving intentions, often while serving the other three.

Rules:
1. Every row takes exactly one status: Confirmed (documented and current), In progress (started but not finished), Not addressed (no evidence anywhere in the record), or Not applicable (state in one clause why it does not apply).
2. Never state a dollar figure, a date, a rate or a document name that is not in the source material. Where an item needs a number nobody has supplied, write "Needs: " and name the document to collect, and mark the row Not addressed.
3. Any federal threshold must be verified before you state it. Before naming the estate tax exemption, the annual gift exclusion, the generation-skipping exemption, or any published IRS rate, search the web for the current year's figure and use that. If an uploaded document or a knowledge file gives a different figure, state both, in this exact form: "Our file says X; the current YEAR figure is Y." Never state a threshold from memory.
4. Anything touching a trust, a partnership, an LLC, a foundation or any other legal entity is routed to counsel. Write "Counsel" in the Route to column and describe the question, never an action to take.
5. Add "+ second review" in the Route to column wherever the family's exposure appears to exceed the current federal exemption, or a structure spans more than one generation, or a structure spans more than one state.
6. This is not legal or tax advice and nothing here is a recommendation to a client. Produce observations and options for the advisor to weigh.
7. Where an item belongs to two areas — a trust that is both transfer and protection — put it under the area it mostly serves and write "Also: " and the other area in the Notes column. Do not repeat the row.

Output four tables, one per area, each headed with the area name, with these columns in this order:
Item | Status | What the record shows | What it would take | Priority | Route to | Notes

Item: the planning question, in a short phrase.
Status: one of the four above.
What the record shows: what the documents and meetings actually establish, or "No evidence in the record".
What it would take: the next concrete action, or the document to collect.
Priority: High, Medium or Low, judged by the effect on the family's life, not by tax saved.
Route to: Advisor, Counsel, CPA, Trustee, or Insurance specialist. Append "+ second review" where rule 5 applies.

The after-meeting review. Skip it for a fortnight and the client record fills with unconfirmed figures, the CRM goes stale, and the agendas Hazel prepares get worse — because they are built from the notes you never filed. It is a self-reinforcing loop in both directions.
