# 03 — Roles and RACI

Every procedure in this library names roles, never people. This reference defines the roles, records who covers each one today, states the trigger for filling it, and fixes the delegation rules that keep the client experience intact as the team grows.

## Purpose

- Give every SOP a stable vocabulary for "who does this step."
- Let the practice run today (solo plus borrowed specialists) and tomorrow (a full team) **without rewriting the client-facing procedures** — only this table changes.
- Make the boundary between the Advisor's own team, borrowed sister-company experts, external experts, and the family's other advisors explicit.

**Rule:** where a role is unfilled, the LA performs it — the procedures do not change.

## Support track vs professional track

- The **support track** (CSA, operations) makes administrative decisions and is not expected to pursue a professional career. The **professional track** (AA → SA → LA) makes professional judgments about a client's plan and portfolio.
- Moving from support to professional is a **change of track**, not a promotion; it requires the hiring process in SOP 72.
- Whoever owns operations today is named here: `[Advisor]` until the CSA is hired. The path from CSA to an operations lead is a stated career option, so operations does not become a second-class job as the team grows.

## Delegation ladder

**Never delegate (LA only):** the Discovery and family-executive-summary interviews; presentation of the investment plan and the wealth management plan; the additional-assets and introductions asks; drop/defer/do coaching; the service-failure apology call; facilitation of the expert-network meeting; facilitation of family meetings unless an external facilitator is engaged.

**Delegate first (in this order as hires arrive):**

1. Routine execution — scheduling, confirmations, paperwork, notebook, [CRM] logging, reporting assembly → CSA.
2. Plan and portfolio production, periodic reviews, supporting analysis → SA / AA.
3. Routine progress meetings — first co-attended, then led by the SA with the LA present, then independent → SA.
4. Relationship management (the client calls the SA first) → SA, when the relationship-manager test in SOP 73 is met.
5. Relationship ownership (all meetings including strategy) → SA, when the relationship-owner test in SOP 73 is met.
6. Business development — last; it needs a decade of experience and is out of scope here.

## Lean RACI convention

Every procedure's `## Roles` line uses three verbs only:

- **Owner** — accountable for the outcome; there is exactly one.
- **Does** — performs named steps.
- **Consulted** — supplies input or review before a step completes.

"Informed" is deliberately omitted; who gets told what is governed by the contact plan (SOP 21) and the coordination protocol (SOP 33).

## Operating rules that protect the relationship

- **Whoever has the ball is the quarterback.** The person who receives a client request owns it to completion. "Let me put you through to someone" is not an acceptable answer inside this practice.
- **The relationship stays with the LA.** Borrowed and external experts are introduced by name, role, and what they will do for this client; they never become the client's primary contact unless the LA and the client agree (SOP 71).
- **Language.** The team says "our clients" and "the family we serve," never "my clients." Possessive language is corrected on the spot.
- **Independent-action scope.** The LA acts alone on anything within the profit center's authority. Matters with more than a stated materiality threshold of impact on the firm, on a sister company, or on a future partner (default: the equivalent of one quarter of a professional salary) are discussed with the relevant firm leader first.
- **Three questions the LA asks about every engagement:** who pays the fee; who is the client; who is advocating for the rising generation. If the first two answers are "the senior generation" and the third is "no one," the engagement is at risk and SOP 40 applies.
