# 04 — High-net-worth psychology

Wealthy clients are not alike, and treating them alike is the commonest service error. This reference gives the team one table of the dominant client personalities, what to listen for at Discovery, and how to present, pace, and contact each — plus the awareness cues that matter when the client is a family rather than a person.

## Purpose

- Identify each client's dominant personality at Discovery (SOP 10) and record it in [CRM].
- Reinforce it at every greeting and in every presentation choice (SOP 05).
- Design family meetings for the mix of personalities in the room (SOP 43).

## The nine personalities

Shares are approximate proportions of the affluent population in the underlying research; use them to calibrate expectations, not to label a client.

| Personality | Share | What they want from the Advisor | Discovery cues — listen for | Presenting the plan | Contact style | Risk flag |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Family Steward | about one in three | The family taken care of; conservative; not investment-minded | "my children," "security," "what happens to them," questions about the next generation before the portfolio | Lead with what the plan does for the family; keep investment detail short; show the protection and transfer pieces | Warm, regular, family news welcome; the couple together | — |
| Independent | about one in six | The freedom money buys; investing is a means, not an interest | "so I don't have to think about it," travel, second careers, "make work optional" | Show the goal (freedom date, income) and that the plan gets there; minimal process talk | Efficient; fewer, purposeful contacts; honor their stated cadence exactly | — |
| Phobic | about one in nine | Relief from a responsibility they dislike; trust in the person | Discomfort with numbers, "I just want someone I can trust," avoidance of technical questions | Plain language, no jargon, one page; emphasize who is looking after them and how | Reassuring, personal, proactive in downturns; never a data dump | — |
| Anonymous | about one in twelve | Privacy above all; will concentrate assets with an advisor who protects it | Questions about who sees what, reluctance to share, few other advisors | Confidentiality first; consolidated reporting as a privacy benefit; explain the consent rules (SOP 02) | Discreet; single point of contact; nothing by open channels | — |
| Mogul | about one in thirteen | Control; investing as an extension of power; decisive | "I decide," wants options not recommendations, impatience with process | Present choices with clear trade-offs and let them choose; be crisp; do not over-explain | Direct, fast, respectful of time; agenda-driven | — |
| VIP | about one in fifteen | Prestige; affiliation with leading institutions and advisors | Names of firms and people, status of the arrangement, how others see them | Emphasize the calibre of the network and the firm's standing; polished materials | Formal; the LA personally; recognition matters | — |
| Accumulator | about one in sixteen | A bigger portfolio; performance-focused; frugal | Return figures, comparisons, fee questions ahead of goals | Numbers-forward, benchmark-aware; keep the goal frame visible | Frequent performance information; expect scrutiny | caution — demanding, transactional |
| Gambler | about one in twenty | The excitement of investing; high risk tolerance; involved | Stories of trades, appetite for ideas, wants to discuss markets | Contain the appetite inside the plan; a small self-directed sleeve if the firm allows | Will want frequent market talk; set boundaries | caution — time-consuming, plan-eroding |
| Innovator | about one in twenty-five | Leading-edge and complex products; technically sophisticated | Asks about structures, new vehicles, complexity for its own sake | Explain the investment policy in depth once; then hold the line on simplicity | Technical, detailed, expects the IPS to be read | caution — complexity for its own sake |

The last three are the ones most interested in investing itself and, in the research, the most demanding and least loyal. The practice does not refuse them, but it accepts them knowingly, prices the service accordingly, and holds the plan.

## Rules of use

1. **Determine the dominant personality at Discovery.** Everyone shows traits of several; almost everyone has one dominant type. Record it in [CRM] with the cues you heard.
2. **Reinforce it at every greeting.** The first minute of every meeting speaks to what this client values — the family, the freedom, the privacy, the standing — before any business (SOP 05).
3. **Match the presentation, not the advice.** The plan is the plan; the length, order, and vocabulary of its presentation change by personality. A Phobic gets one page and a person; an Innovator gets the investment policy statement.
4. **Match the contact plan.** The stated process preference from Discovery governs (SOP 21); the personality suggests the tone and what non-portfolio contact will be welcome.
5. **Re-check after transitions.** A liquidity event, a death, or retirement can shift a client from Independent to Family Steward, or from Mogul to Phobic. Re-read the cues at the next progress meeting.

## The family mix

A family is several personalities across two or three generations — often a Family Steward founder, an Independent in the second generation, and an Innovator or Gambler among the rising generation. Design family meetings (SOP 43) for the mix: an agenda with something for the steward (protection, continuity), the independent (what this frees them to do), and the technically minded (the investment policy, in an appendix); ground rules that stop the Mogul from deciding for everyone; and a facilitator when the mix is combustible.

!!! extension "Beyond Bowen"
    **Rising-generation cues, not diagnoses.** Hughes describes patterns a large founder's shadow can produce in heirs. Use these only to notice and ask, never to label: the dutiful steward whose own dreams are crowded out; the member who learned the size of the wealth by accident; the one slowly made comfortable into passivity; the one raised as if the money did not exist and now unsure what is real; the anxious heir measuring up; the one who constrains every choice to protect the family name; the one who wants to give it all away to be free. The Advisor's job is individuation — helping each member find work, relationships, and a voice of their own — and that is done through the family layer (SOP 40, 42, 44), not through the investment plan.

!!! note "Formal assessments"
    Learning-style, values, work-style, and personality instruments are useful to families and are referred to qualified specialists through the expert network (SOP 31). The practice does not administer or interpret them; it records the client's stated preferences and honors them.
