# T26 — Job description, scorecard and interview guide

## Purpose

The forms used in SOP 72: a job-description template, three ready-to-adapt role summaries, the three-dimension scorecard, interview topics for both rounds, work-sample briefs, reference-check topics, and the offer checklist.

## How to use

- Complete Part 1 before advertising. Keep Part 1's compensation block out of the posting.
- Score every candidate on Part 3 after each stage; decide from the scores, not from the last conversation.
- The role summaries in Part 2 are starting points — edit the measures to the practice's current numbers.

## Part 2 — Role summaries to adapt

**Client Service Associate (support track; overhead)**
Owns: scheduling and confirmations (SOP 05, T01); meeting logistics and packets; sign-here paperwork; the implementation tracker (SOP 15, T07); client notebook and [Document vault]; [CRM] hygiene; reporting assembly (SOP 24); service-failure intake (SOP 23); red-flag monitoring (SOP 22). Measures: confirmations within 24 hours (100%); zero administrative errors on transfers; tracker current weekly; every meeting ends with the next scheduled.

**Service Advisor (professional track; direct expense)**
Owns: investment plan and IPS production (SOP 13, T06); periodic reviews and the quarterly package analysis; routine progress meetings — co-attended, then led with the LA present, then independent (SOP 20); the advanced-planning tracker (T10); watching for held-away assets and planning openings. Attends every client meeting with a named role. Measures: first independently led progress meeting by month 6; relationship-manager test (T27) passed on ≥ 5 relationships by month 12; plan drafts accepted with minor edits.

**Associate Advisor / Analyst (professional track, entry)**
Owns: analysis for plans and reviews; meeting packets; tracker upkeep; expert-meeting case briefs (T09); executive-summary drafting support (T17). Second chair on large relationships. Measures: packets complete three days before meetings; case briefs accepted without rework; one defined analysis presented in a client meeting per month by month 3.

## Part 4 — Interview topics

**Round one (experience and proficiency)** — walk through the last two roles and why each ended; the career the candidate wants in five years; explain a planning or portfolio concept to a non-expert; describe a client situation that went wrong and what they did; how they prefer to receive feedback; what they want to know about how this practice serves families.

**Round two (concerns and fit)** — the specific gaps the work sample and references raised; a scenario in which a client asks them something outside their remit (looking for "I'll own it and bring in the right person," not "I'll transfer you"); a scenario in which the LA is unavailable and a family calls with a problem; their view on sharing relationships across a team; the equity question, answered with the SOP 72 script; their questions for us.

## Part 5 — Work-sample briefs

- **Service Advisor:** a two-page family case (assets, entities, goals, one advanced-planning issue) → draft the executive summary of an investment plan on T06 and a one-page tracker on T10; present in five minutes; the panel asks two client-style questions.
- **Associate / Analyst:** the same case → a case brief on T09 and the packet checklist for a first progress meeting.
- **Client Service Associate:** a set of transfer instructions with one deliberate inconsistency → complete T07 and draft the client confirmation on T01; the panel looks for the inconsistency being caught and raised.

## Part 6 — Reference-check topics

Confirm dates and role; the best work the person did; how they handled a mistake; how clients or colleagues spoke of them; whether they would hire them again and for what; **who else worked closely with them** — then call that person too.

## Part 7 — Offer checklist

- [ ] Title, track, reporting line, start date
- [ ] Salary within range; incentive described as it really works, including that it can be zero
- [ ] Benefits and time off as they actually are
- [ ] Non-solicitation language drafted by counsel / [Firm Compliance]
- [ ] 90-day review named
- [ ] Written; one revision at most; no haggling
- [ ] Acceptance recorded; team told before clients; onboarding plan (T27) opened
