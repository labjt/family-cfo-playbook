# T22 — Trustee–beneficiary meeting pack

## Purpose

Everything needed to prepare, run, and follow up a trustee–beneficiary meeting under SOP 46: the two introductory letters for a new relationship, the two premeeting checklists, the standing agenda with timings, the annual mutual-assessment form, the appreciative reflection, and the protector's check-in note.

## How to use

- The AA assembles the pack; the LA reviews; the CSA sends the accounting and proposed agenda **two weeks** before the meeting and collects the beneficiary's additions **one week** before.
- Use Part 1 only for a new relationship. Parts 2–4 every meeting; Part 5 once a year; Part 6 after an unusually engaged meeting; Part 7 for the protector's annual round.
- Letters are outlines to be written in each party's own voice. Never send a form letter.

## Part 1 — Introductory letters (new relationship)

**Trustee → beneficiary — outline**

1. Who I am and how I came to this role (two or three sentences).
2. Proposed time, place, and length (suggest two hours with an optional meal afterward); practical logistics; a question about any needs or preferences.
3. What the creator intended, in one sentence, and my hope for how we will work together.
4. What must happen for you to leave this meeting thinking it was worthwhile? — asked, not assumed.
5. What I plan to cover: getting to know each other (your questions about me included); your questions about trusts and about this trust — please read the instrument first; your goals, hopes, and concerns; my role and yours, and the roles of anyone else in the picture (protector, investment adviser, committee); how requests for funds work and how I analyze them; investments to whatever depth you want, with a sample statement; open questions.
6. Please add anything to the agenda; there are no obvious questions.
7. A follow-up call one week after the meeting for what surfaces on reflection.

**Beneficiary → trustee — outline**

1. Who I am: what is going on in my life, what matters to me, my plans, how I see the trust fitting in; gratitude where I feel it.
2. What I would like on the agenda (co-designing it).
3. Advance notice of any distribution request, with documents to follow.
4. A request for the agenda and any preparation materials.

## Part 2 — Premeeting checklists

**Trustee (and the Advisor supporting the trustee)**

- [ ] Instrument, correspondence, latest statement, and prior notes reviewed.
- [ ] The highest purpose of this meeting written in one line; what an excellent outcome would look and sound like.
- [ ] My narratives about this beneficiary, the creator, and the meeting examined; anything that does not serve both parties set aside.
- [ ] Any pending request read with the enhancement standard in mind; one question prepared that would open a deeper conversation.
- [ ] Good news: how will I respond with real interest?
- [ ] The big questions considered as appropriate — is this person self-aware; have they sought their calling; do they have a mentor; can they express gratitude and joy; are they active in the wider world; do they evaluate advisers well — and am I equipped to help where growth is needed?
- [ ] Mindset chosen: caution where warranted, advancement where possible.

**Beneficiary (coached by the Advisor where asked)**

- [ ] Instrument, correspondence, and notes reviewed; the running master list of questions updated and brought.
- [ ] Agenda reviewed; items added; would this agenda allow a great outcome?
- [ ] Any request: how would it enhance my life, and what difference would that make?
- [ ] My narratives about the trustee, the creator, and the meeting — where they came from and whether they help.
- [ ] My strengths, and how I will bring them into the room.

## Part 3 — Standing agenda (business meeting, private, social afterward)

| Block | Items | Suggested time |
|---|---|---|
| Opening | What do we each hope to accomplish; what must happen for this to be a success · agenda confirmed, additions and deletions · significant life changes since last time and their implications · status of prior to-dos and what we learn from it | 15 min |
| Annual review (once a year) | Beneficiary's hopes, challenges, opportunities for the year · trustee's, in role · what is working and what could improve · beneficiary's assessment of the trustee (Part 5) · trustee's assessment of the beneficiary (Part 5) · disputes: resolvable today, or next steps | 30–45 min |
| Technical | Statement, tax returns, quantitative items; a sample statement walked through for a new beneficiary | 20 min |
| Distributions | New requests · pending requests · anticipated requests (T23) | 20–30 min |
| Close | Anything else · to-dos with owners and dates · did we accomplish what we hoped; what worked; what to change next time · next meeting scheduled | 10 min |

## Part 6 — Appreciative reflection (after an unusually good meeting; one week)

1. Recall a meeting between us that was genuinely collaborative: what made it work — the setting, the preparation, what each of us did, and how that let each of us contribute fully?
2. Rate our most recent meeting from one to five for engagement. What earned it that number, even if low? What, specifically, would have made it one point higher — who would have done what, with what effect?
3. What practices bring out the best in our meetings? What do I do well and should do more of? What does the other party do well and should do more of?
