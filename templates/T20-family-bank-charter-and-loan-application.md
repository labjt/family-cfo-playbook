# T20 — Family bank charter and loan application

## Purpose

The working documents for a family bank under SOP 47: a charter outline the family completes in its own words (counsel reviews), the two application forms, a decision record, and the loan register.

## How to use

- The LA drafts the charter with the principals or the council; every participant — lender or borrower, including trustees — signs concurrence. Counsel reviews loan documentation and tax treatment before the first loan.
- Applicants complete the relevant form and present it to the board in person. The CSA files everything and keeps the register; the AA reviews borrower reports.
- Qualitative portions of every application are circulated to all participants; personal financial detail may be redacted.

## Part 1 — Charter outline

| Section | What the family writes |
|---|---|
| 1. Mission | Why the bank exists: a lender whose return is measured in members' independence and the growth of the family's human, intellectual, social, and spiritual capital, and in circulating what members learn |
| 2. Values | The family's mission and values statement, and how the bank advances them |
| 3. Participants | Who may lend (members, households, trusts with trustee agreement), who may borrow (adult members; conditions for others), and the concurrence each signs |
| 4. Funding | Sources and initial capital; how new capital is added; whether repayments recirculate |
| 5. Officers and board | Chair, secretary, treasurer; board composition across generations and branches; advisers (the Advisor, counsel, tax) with voice but no vote; terms; the investment-committee liaison |
| 6. Meeting rules | Cadence (quarterly); agenda circulated in advance; applicants heard in person; minutes; decision rule (consensus sought; majority with reasons recorded); conflicts — a member abstains on their own or their household's application |
| 7. Loan types | Investment loans (business plan and application to a commercial standard; feasibility discussion; business reports; repayment) · Enhancement loans (how the loan increases independence and adds to the family's capitals; repaid in independence and capital) · Grants, if any, and for what purposes |
| 8. Terms (policy choices) | Interest: below market / interest-free but repayable / other · Term and repayment schedule · Collateral: none / stated · Purposes funded: ventures, education, homes, health, career starts · No-bailout rule: adopted / not adopted · Emergency provision |
| 9. Application and decision procedure | Forms (Parts 2–3); calendar; who reviews; how the decision and reasons are communicated; appeal or resubmission |
| 10. Reporting | Investment-loan reports quarterly; enhancement-loan reflections annually; the bank's annual report to the family meeting |
| 11. Transparency rule | Every participant receives the qualitative portions of every application; financial detail may be redacted |
| 12. Managed slices | Whether the bank funds training allocations (T19), size, coaching, reporting |
| 13. Review and amendment | Annual review at the family meeting; amendment rule; dissolution |
| 14. Concurrence | Signatures of all participants; date; renewed annually |
