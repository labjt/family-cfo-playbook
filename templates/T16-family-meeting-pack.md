# T16 — Family meeting pack

## Purpose

The fill-in forms for SOP 43: the design frame, the ground-rules sheet, pre-work forms, the mission/values run-sheet, the two agendas, the exercise menu, story prompts, the rituals calendar, the minutes template and the takeaway round.

## How to use

Complete Part 1 with the family's leaders four weeks before any meeting; send Parts 3 and 8 with the invitation; bring Parts 2, 4–7 and 9–11 to the room. All wording is the practice's own; the family is encouraged to rewrite it in their voice.

## Part 2 — Ground rules for signature

We meet to stay connected, to learn, and to decide together. To make that possible we agree to:

1. **Be here.** Devices away, on time, pre-work done.
2. **One voice at a time.** We let each other finish.
3. **Play it back before pushing back.** We show we understood before we answer.
4. **Say "I."** We speak for ourselves, not for "everyone."
5. **Hold our outcomes loosely.** We come to decide, not to win.
6. **Drop the "but."** We begin a reply with what we heard.
7. **Candor with care.** We say the hard thing in a way that can be heard.
8. **Park, don't drop.** What isn't settled today gets an owner and a date.
9. **What's said here stays here** unless we agree otherwise.
10. **These rules bind all of us**, whoever is oldest or pays.

Signed: ______________________ (every attendee) · Date: ______ · Reviewed at each meeting.

## Part 6 — Annual family meeting agenda

**Day 1 — connect and learn**
1. Open; rules; a rising-generation member reads the mission/values statement.
2. Individual track: each member's update (3 minutes each; no questions until all have spoken).
3. Learning session (chosen by the family: a trust explained; how the family's money is held; giving; a skill).
4. Generational track: each generation caucuses — what we want to share with the others, what we want to learn from them — then reports.
5. Story time (Part 8); grandparent–grandchild lunch (parents elsewhere).

**Day 2 — the business of the family**
6. The four health questions (SOP 43 Part C).
7. Capitals review — T18 year on year.
8. Family-level tracker (T10): drop / defer / do on open items.
9. Money and structures: the year in review; trusts and entities; family bank and investor allocation (SOP 47); giving.
10. Review of representatives and advisors; the fee conversation.
11. Decisions requested (proposer · options · recommendation · decision).
12. Rising generation: what is your dream, and how can we invest in it? (separate from money decisions)
13. Rituals for the year's passages (Part 9).
14. Next steps with owners and dates; next meeting fixed; gratitude round; close.

## Part 7 — Exercise menu

- **What matters most to me, and why.** A round the table; the secretary lists every answer; the list becomes raw material for the values statement.
- **Founding values vs present values.** The whole family names the founders' values; each generation separately names today's; compare and discuss the gaps.
- **The long view.** Each person writes what they would want remembered, looking back from very old age; read in small groups.
- **Twenty years out.** Each person describes the family two decades from now — who is where, what the family does together, what it owns.
- **Which hats do I wear?** Each member lists every role they hold in the family (parent, child, owner, trustee, beneficiary, employee, board member) and names the two that conflict most.
- **Across the generations.** Each generation answers: what we'd like the others to know; what we'd like to learn from them.
- **A life well spent.** Elders describe one as themselves; the rising generation as if looking back at fifty; compare.
- **Allies, neutrals, opponents.** Before a contested decision, each person privately maps where others stand and tests whether their own position is best for the whole.

## Part 8 — Story prompts (sent to the designated storyteller in advance)

1. Tell us about a person who shaped you, and what of them lives on in how you act.
2. Describe a time the family — or you — nearly lost something that mattered, and what saved it.
3. What did you learn the hard way that you'd like the young ones to learn the easy way?
4. What are you proudest of that nobody outside this room knows?
5. If a great-grandchild asked what our family stood for, what would you want them to hear?

Formats: a story after dinner · the eldest retelling a story they heard as a child · a rising-generation member interviewing a grandparent with the prompts · stories generated in the across-the-generations exercise.
