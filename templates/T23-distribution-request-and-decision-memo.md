# T23 — Distribution request and decision memo

## Purpose

A single pack that carries a discretionary distribution request from first conversation to documented decision: the beneficiary's written request, the supporting documents, the analysis worksheet the trustee (with any Distribution Committee and the Advisor) works through, the decision memo, and the request calendar. It makes the distributive function transparent, repeatable, and defensible.

## How to use

- The beneficiary completes Part 1 (with the Advisor's coaching if asked) and attaches Part 2. Much of it is assembled together in conversation — the form is a thinking aid, not a hurdle, and the trustee will not demand every item every time.
- The trustee, advised by any Distribution Committee and by the Advisor, works Part 3 in order. **The trustee decides; advisers advise.**
- Part 4 is written for every discretionary decision, approved or declined. Part 5 sets expectations at onboarding.
- Emergencies bypass the calendar, not the analysis.

How would approval enhance my life — my growth, my aims, my independence — rather than simply support it? What would be different in a year, and in ten?

## Part 3 — Analysis worksheet (trustee, committee, Advisor)

**Stage 1 — the conversation (before or after the written request)**

| Question explored | Notes |
|---|---|
| What motivates the request; is there a larger purpose beneath it? | |
| How does it square with the creator's intent (purpose statement, letters of wishes)? | |
| How does it square with the trust's language? | |
| Is there a better route to the goal than trust funds? | |
| Will the beneficiary have their own stake in it? | |
| If approved: best outcome, short term and long term? | |
| If declined: what changes; what alternatives exist; what would a good outcome after a "no" look like? | |
| Growth question inserted (e.g., what does having the trust pay for this cost you in independence?) | |

**Stage 2 — the enhancement standard**

Is making — or not making — this distribution more likely to enhance the beneficiary's life (maturation, pursuit of their own aims) than to subsidize it? Consider life stage explicitly.
Assessment: ______

**Stage 3 — the document**

Would approval be a reasonable exercise of discretion under the trust's terms? ______
If the language is narrower than the purpose: note for the structural review (SOP 48) — ______

!!! note "Note"
    Three uses of the memo: a template for the next request, a record for successor trustees, and evidence if the decision is ever questioned in the family or in court.
