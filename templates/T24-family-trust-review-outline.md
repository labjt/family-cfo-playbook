# T24 — Family Trust Review outline, ten-year review worksheet, and fiduciary selection questionnaire

## Purpose

Four working tools for SOP 48: the engagement outline for a Family Trust Review; the ten-year trustscape review worksheet; a questionnaire for selecting (or briefing) a trustee or protector; and a Distribution Committee charter checklist.

## How to use

- Part 1 is completed with the reviewer and the principals at commissioning; the Advisor coordinates data and interviews.
- Part 2 is the AA's worksheet every three years and at any trigger in SOP 48.
- Part 3 is worked with any prospective trustee or protector — by the candidate for their own decision, by the creator as a selection instrument, and by the Advisor when briefing a named fiduciary at drafting time. Candidates who arrive asking these questions are a good sign.
- Part 4 goes to counsel when a Distribution Committee is being authorized.

## Part 3 — Trustee / protector selection questionnaire (our topics)

**First response**

1. Who created the trust, who are the beneficiaries, and when would the role actually begin?
2. Why were you chosen — and do you believe the reasoning?
3. What is your first feeling about it, and what inclines you to decline or accept?

**Document homework**

4. Having read the instrument closely, can you say what the trust is for?
5. Who receives what, under what circumstances, and when does it end?
6. Is there more than one trustee, and how are duties divided?

**Self-inventory**

7. What story do you carry about trusts, beneficiaries, and family wealth?
8. Are you yourself a beneficiary or a recipient of wealth, and how has that gone?
9. How do you handle your own financial administration — willingly or as a chore?
10. Have you made significant gifts or created trusts, and what did you learn?

**Yellow flags (none disqualifying; each argues for slowing down)**

11. Are the beneficiaries known to you or strangers?
12. Was the creator's relationship with them close or strained; is there a history of conflict, cut-offs, or litigation?
13. Children of more than one marriage; multiple branches; multiple generations?
14. Known addictions or behavioral issues among present or future beneficiaries?
15. Assets needing special expertise; unusual directions to hold assets; a family business in the trust?

**Deeper questions**

16. Are you humble about what you do not know, and willing to close the gaps?
17. Are you comfortable overseeing a relative's spending, and will it change the relationship?
18. In a disagreement with a beneficiary, what would you do, and whom would you consult informally?
19. Have you already discussed goals and expectations with the beneficiary?
20. Are you looking forward to it, and how will the beneficiary benefit?
21. Will you be paid, how will that be set, and how do you feel about it?
22. How significant is the liability, and are you comfortable with it?
23. Is this a long-term commitment you can keep, and what is the exit if you cannot?
24. Which "caps" do you wear with this beneficiary, and can the trustee cap sit on top whenever you act as trustee?
25. For a protector: can you stay dormant, ask "how is it going?" once a year, and act only when asked?

## Part 4 — Distribution Committee charter checklist (for counsel)

- [ ] Authorized in the instrument (or added by settlement / reformation / decanting / protector action, or convened by the trustee on its own initiative)
- [ ] Tied to the trust's purpose statement
- [ ] Function: advises the trustee on distributions; **never** approves or declines
- [ ] Composition: one or several members chosen for wisdom, listening, self-awareness; may be officers of a firm engaged for the purpose
- [ ] Appointment by the creator, trustee, or an independent party, in consultation with primary beneficiaries; beneficiaries cannot add or remove members
- [ ] Terms: five years; two-term cap; age-out; each member nominates a successor with existing members' approval, reverting to the trustee
- [ ] Costs paid by the trust; compensation disclosed
- [ ] Formal review every three years by trustee, protector, and primary beneficiaries
- [ ] Not combined with the protector role
- [ ] Information sharing between trustee and committee authorized
