# T17 — Family executive summary pack

## Purpose

Everything the LA needs to run SOP 41: the kickoff record, the interviewee list, the interview guide, the confidential notes form, the report outline, the leader-review checklist and the sharing plan.

## How to use

- Parts 1–2 at kickoff; Part 3 in every interview; Part 4 typed within 48 hours; Part 5 drafted after the last interview; Parts 6–7 before and after the leader review. All parts live in the family's restricted folder in the [Document vault].
- Prompts are conversation starters, not a script; choose no more than five overlays per interview.

## Part 3 — Interview guide (60–90 minutes)

**Opening (5 min).** Thank them; restate confidentiality (themes only, never attribution); ask permission to record or take notes; confirm the time available.

**Two questions to begin (10 min).**
- What are you most absorbed by right now — in work, in life, in something you're building?
- Who do you learn from at the moment, and how did you find them?

**Topic A — What's working (10 min).**
- When this family is at its best, what is happening?
- What would you protect at any cost?
- Who in the family do others turn to, and for what?

**Topic B — What's coming (15 min).**
- In the next two years, what will test the family? In the next twenty?
- Which transition is underway — a generation stepping up, a business or property changing hands, leadership of the money or of the family?
- What does the family avoid talking about?

**Topic C — What you'd do (15 min).**
- If you were running this, what would you change first?
- What would you need — information, a decision, a person — to play your part?
- Which decisions do you think the family should make together, and which should stay with households?

**Topic D — What you'd learn (5 min).**
- What do you hope to understand about yourself from this? About the family?

**Topic E — What you'd give (5 min).**
- What do you want to come out of this process? What are you willing to put in — time, a role, money, patience?

**Household areas (if this person has not had a Discovery — 15 min).** Values · goals · relationships · assets and where held · advisors · how they want to be worked with · interests (see T03).

**Overlays (choose up to five — 10 min).**

| Overlay | Prompts in our words |
|---|---|
| Deciding together | How does this family make a decision that affects everyone? Who is heard last? |
| Growing each member | Is every member becoming more capable, or only more comfortable? |
| Each generation's own dream | Is the rising generation living its own ambitions or someone else's? |
| Calling before stewardship | Do members find their work first, and their duties to the family second — or the reverse? |
| Attract or obligate | Do people stay in this family's enterprise because they want to, or because they can't leave? |
| Elders | Who mediates, who remembers, who holds the rituals? Is anyone being prepared for that? |
| The long view | Whose grandchildren do we think about when we decide? |
| Generational position | Are you one household, a set of siblings, several clans, or a tribe? |
| Three circles | Family, ownership, management — which gets the least attention? |
| Budget comparison | Roughly what does the family spend a year on managing money, and what on developing people? |
| Family money vs personal money | Has any asset ever been set aside as *the family's* rather than someone's own? |
| Pitfalls | Do heirs know what is coming? Does every heir have a real role? Is ownership passing while the senior generation can still teach? |
| Culture inventory | Which of these does the family actually share — a business, a place, a giving tradition, a set of hobbies, a story of origin, an education path, a reputation? |
| Three Ds | Where is the family divided, dissatisfied, or drifting apart? |

**Close (5 min).** What should I have asked you? Who else should I be talking to? Thank them; say when they will hear the outcome.

## Part 5 — Report outline

**Family executive summary — [Family] — date — confidential**

1. **Purpose and process.** Why the family did this; who was interviewed (by role, not name); the instrument; dates.
2. **Overarching themes.** Five to eight, each one paragraph; state where generations or branches differ.
3. **Areas of strength.** What to protect; what to build on.
4. **Key challenges and opportunities.** Grouped: relationships · decisions and governance · money and structures · the rising generation · transitions underway. Each with the evidence (how many raised it, without attribution).
5. **Recommendations and next steps.** Three to six, each with a proposed owner, a first step, and a date. Typical: hold a family meeting on the summary; a learning session on the trusts; a values/mission session; a decision the family needs to make.
6. **Appendices.** T18 results (per capital and family averages, with range); draft family map and trustscape (T14 Parts 1–2); transitions inventory (T14 Part 6); glossary.
