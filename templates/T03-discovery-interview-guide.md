# T03 — Discovery interview guide

## Purpose

The interviewer's guide for CCM1 and Rediscovery. It keeps the seven areas in order, supplies prompt questions in the practice's own words, and adds the overlays for large families and for Mode B mandates. It is a guide, not a script — follow the client.

## How to use

- Read it before the meeting; do not read from it in the meeting. Keep the mind-map sheet (T04) in front of you, not this guide.
- Use one open prompt per area, then **go one level deeper** on every general answer: *what specifically… · what would that look like… · why does that matter to you…*
- Assets are fourth. If the client starts with money, listen, note it, and return to values.
- Mark the personality cues you hear. Tick the overlay prompts you used so the AA knows what was covered.

## The seven areas

### 1. Values
- When you think about your money and your life, what matters most?
- What would you never want the wealth to do to your family?
- What did you learn about money growing up that still guides you?
- If we are working well together in five years, what will be true that isn't today?
- *Deeper:* what specifically about that matters most? What would it look like in practice?

### 2. Goals
- What do you want to achieve over the long run — for yourself, your family, your enterprise, the world?
- What has to happen in the next two years? The next ten?
- What would you do if you never had to think about work or money again?
- Which goals are shared across the family, and which are yours alone?
- *Deeper:* when, for whom, and what does "done" look like?

### 3. Relationships
- Who counts in your life? Tell me about each of them.
- Who depends on you, and on whom do you depend?
- Who in the family is easy to talk to about money, and who isn't?
- Anyone outside the family — or any pets — you'd want us to keep in mind?

### 4. Assets (fourth)
- Walk me through what you own and where it sits — accounts, entities, trusts, property, businesses. Documents to the CSA as we go.
- What is held elsewhere, and with whom? How is it going?
- Which positions are large relative to the whole? Any restrictions?
- What income arrives each year and from where? What debt?
- What events could change the picture — a sale, a vesting, an inheritance, a distribution?

### 5. Advisors
- Who do you rely on today — accountants, attorneys, trustees, other managers, insurance, a family office?
- What has worked with each, and what hasn't?
- Who among them do you trust most? Who would you change if it were easy?
- Have your advisors ever met one another?

### 6. Process
- How do you like to work with an advisor — how often, by what channel, in how much detail?
- Who should be in the room when we meet? Who should never be?
- How do you prefer to make decisions — quickly, after reflection, together with someone?
- What has annoyed you about advisors' communication in the past?

### 7. Interests
- What do you do that has nothing to do with money?
- What are you involved in — boards, causes, faith, sport, collecting?
- Where do you go to relax, and with whom?

## UHNW overlay prompts

- **Entities and trusts:** for each: why it exists, who is trustee or manager, who benefits, who protects it, where it sits, how the people touched by it feel about it. Do you receive and read the statements?
- **Concentrated positions and businesses:** what share of the whole; restrictions; succession intentions; has anyone raised an employee-ownership route or a sale?
- **Liquidity events:** anything pending or possible in the next three years; what would you do with the proceeds?
- **Philanthropy:** how you give today; what vehicles; who in the family takes part; appetite.
- **Family office / central team:** does one exist; who leads it; what does it do for you; how do you experience it?
- **Governance:** family meetings, a council, a constitution, a mission statement, a family bank; rituals the family keeps.
- **Rising generation:** each person's stage and work; how much they know; who is looking out for them.
- **Transitions:** what is changing in the family, the ownership, the board, the management; health.

## Family prompts

!!! extension "Beyond Bowen"
    Use with a household that belongs to a larger family, or where wealth spans generations. Choose; do not run them all.

- **Calling and mentor:** what are you passionate about? Who do you learn from?
- **Budget comparison:** roughly what does the family spend each year to protect and grow its money — fees, advisors, lawyers, accountants? And what, deliberately, on developing its people — education beyond tuition, gatherings, coaching, learning to give? What does that ratio say?
- **Generational position:** are we talking with the founding generation, siblings, or cousins? What has moved from one generation to the next already, and how did it go?
- **Essential-question topics** (in your words): what the family stands for; whether the family sees itself as bound by choice, not only by blood; how decisions are made together and whether that works; whether each generation is finding its own dreams rather than living the founder's; who the family's elders are and what they do; how the family thinks about the seventh generation.
- **Pitfall probes:** do the heirs know what is coming — the money and the responsibility? Does every heir have a role they consider important? Are there mixed messages between the parents about the wealth? Has the founder waited too long to pass real responsibility? Is succession a shared vision or a decree?
- **Institutionalisation:** has any asset been formally set aside as the *family's* rather than someone's personal money? Who decided that, and how?

## Mode B mandate prompts

- What does your family's team already do for you, and how well?
- What do you want from us that you are not getting?
- Who else will need to know that we are working together, and how would you like that handled?
- What information about your own affairs can you not get access to today?
- Is there anyone — a trustee, the family office, a sibling — whose approval matters for what you're asking us to do?
- Are there sister companies of ours already working with your family? (Check conflicts with [Firm Compliance].)

## Personality cues

| Personality | Listen for | Adjust |
|---|---|---|
| Family steward | Everything framed around the family's security; cautious; little interest in investing | Lead with family outcomes; simplify the mechanics |
| Independent | Freedom, options, exit; investing is a means | Emphasise what the plan makes possible |
| Phobic | Discomfort with money talk; chooses on trust | Slow down; fewer numbers; more reassurance |
| Anonymous | Privacy first; concentrates assets with whoever protects them | Confidentiality explicitly; discretion in everything |
| Mogul | Control; decisiveness; power | Options with a clear recommendation; respect the decision |
| VIP | Prestige; affiliation with leading names | Credentials of the network; quality of materials |
| Accumulator | Portfolio size; performance; frugality | Be candid; a demanding client — check fit |
| Gambler | Excitement; high risk tolerance; very involved | Usually a poor fit for this practice |
| Innovator | Complex products; technically savvy | Usually a poor fit; if taken, narrow mandate |

## Closing and precommitment

- "Let me tell you what I heard…" — one-minute summary in their words; ask what was missed.
- "Here is what happens next…" — the plan meeting in two weeks, commitment a week later, follow-up at forty-five days, then your cadence.
- "Does it make sense to take the next step together?"
- If the fit is poor: "I don't think we're the right firm for what you need — here's who I'd suggest, and I'd be glad to introduce you."
- Schedule in the room. Thank them for their candour.
