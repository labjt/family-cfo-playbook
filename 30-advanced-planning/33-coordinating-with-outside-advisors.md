# 33 — Coordinating with outside advisors

Every family at this level already has professionals, and the largest have a central team — a family office, a trust company, counsel, a lead advisor. Bowen names working with them as one of the three tasks of relationship management but gives no method. This procedure is the method, written first for Mode B (where the Advisor serves individuals inside a family with its own team) and then for Mode A (where he coordinates the family's incumbent professionals himself).

## Purpose

- Make the Advisor a reliable, low-friction colleague to the family's central team so that his individuals get better outcomes and the FCT never feels bypassed.
- Give the client one thread of communication, one map of who does what, and no surprises from any direction.
- Handle disagreement between advisors privately and let the client decide.

## Trigger

- Engagement scoping (T05) identifies an FCT or incumbent advisors — before CCM2 in every new engagement.
- A new advisor enters the picture (new counsel, a family office is formed, a trustee changes).
- Any recommendation on T10 touches something another advisor owns.
- A service failure or dispute involves another advisor.
- Quarterly, by the coordination call; annually, by the advisor-map review.

## Roles

Owner: LA · Does: CSA (advisor map upkeep, consent letters, call scheduling, cc discipline), AA (data packages, reconciliation of positions between advisors) · Consulted: [Firm Compliance] on any information-sharing question; the FCT contact.

## Timing

| Step | When |
|---|---|
| Advisor map drafted | after CCM1, before CCM2 |
| Introductions and consent letter | between CCM2 and CCM3; confirmed in the engagement letter |
| Communication protocol agreed | within 30 days of CCM3 |
| Coordination call | quarterly, two weeks before the client's progress meeting |
| Advisor-map review | annually at the first progress meeting of the year |

## Inputs

- T05 Parts 2, 4, and 5: who the client is, what may be shared, and the counterparties.
- The Total Client Profile's Advisors area (SOP 11): what has worked and not worked with each advisor.
- The scope banner of the investment plan (SOP 13) and the area tags on the wealth management plan (SOP 30).
- Documents the client has authorized us to receive from other advisors: trust instruments, family investment policy, entity charts, prior plans.

## Prep checklist

- [ ] Advisor map drafted: for each advisor — name, firm, function, what they own for this client, how they prefer to communicate, and whether the client wants us in contact with them.
- [ ] Consent letter drafted from T05 Part 4 for the client to sign, naming what we may share with whom and what we may receive.
- [ ] The three questions answered on T05 (who pays, who is the client, who advocates for the rising generation).
- [ ] Any conflict between the practice or a sister company and an outside advisor logged in the conflicts register (SOP 31).

## Procedure

### Mode B first — the Family Central Team

1. **Map the advisors.** Complete the advisor map from CCM1 and T05. Distinguish who *owns* a function (decides), who *executes* it, and who *is informed*. Confirm the map with the client before contacting anyone.
2. **Let the client make the introductions.** Ask the client to introduce the Advisor to the FCT contact by a short message or a three-way call. Never introduce yourself to a family's central team unannounced.

    !!! script "Say something like"
        "So your family office is never surprised by anything we do, would you introduce me to the person there who coordinates your affairs? A short note from you is all it needs."

3. **Send the consent letter.** One page, signed by the client, stating what the practice may share with the FCT and receive from it, for what purpose, and for how long. Copy to [Firm Compliance]. Nothing is shared before it is signed.
4. **Hold a first coordination call.** Purpose: state the mandate (scope rung from T05), learn the FCT's own process and calendar, agree the communication protocol, exchange the documents each side needs (family investment policy and trust constraints inbound; the individual's IPS scope banner outbound), and identify anything already under way that affects the individual.
5. **Write the communication protocol.** Who contacts whom for what; single thread per topic; cc rules (the client is copied on substantive exchanges unless they have asked otherwise); turnaround expectations; how urgent matters are escalated; what is confidential from the FCT under the client's consent.
6. **Coordinate before acting.** Any recommendation touching a family structure is drafted as a recommendation *to take to* family counsel or the FCT, presented to the client that way, and — with consent — previewed with the FCT before the client's progress meeting. The FCT should never learn of a recommendation from the client after the fact.
7. **Run the quarterly coordination call.** Twenty minutes, two weeks before the client's progress meeting: changes on either side, items in flight, calendar for the family (meetings, reviews, tax deadlines), anything the FCT needs from the practice's reporting (SOP 24).
8. **Handle disagreement privately.** If the practice disagrees with an FCT position, raise it with the FCT first, in writing, with the analysis; document both positions; present both to the client neutrally; the client decides. Never argue the point in front of the client, and never assign blame for another advisor's failure — the practice still owns recovery for its client (SOP 23).
9. **Review the map annually** and on every trigger in SOP 22: new advisors, departures, changes in who decides.

### Mode A — the family's incumbent professionals

10. **Map and meet the incumbents.** The client's CPA, attorney, other managers, and trustees. Ask what has worked and what has not; state that the practice's role is to coordinate, not replace, and that the client has authorized contact.
11. **Convene the advisers as a standing advisory board.** Propose to the family that its outside advisers meet together once or twice a year with the Advisor as convener. Bring a professional facilitator to the **first** meeting so that professional defensiveness does not derail it. Agenda: the family's goals in the advisers' words; the map of who owns what; open items across the four planning areas; the calendar.

    !!! extension "Beyond Bowen"
        Families are routinely surprised that their advisers have never met; when they do, problems no single adviser could solve get solved. The first joint meeting sets the tone — hence the facilitator. This is Bowen's expert network convened as the family's own board rather than issue by issue.

12. **Use the annual owners meeting.** Where the family holds an annual owners or family meeting (SOP 43), invite all advisers for the relevant portion so owners know whom to call for what. Provide each adviser's one-page summary of anything new they have done for the family, in plain language, with a communication plan (T21).
13. **Work through the family council** when one exists (SOP 45): it is the family's voice to outside professionals; the Advisor coordinates the advisers on the council's behalf and reports to it, without displacing individual households' direct relationships.
14. **Act as translator in adversarial processes.** In prenuptial negotiations or contested estate matters, help the family articulate its positive intent and convey it to both sets of lawyers, so legal process does not damage the relationship the structures are meant to protect (SOP 22, SOP 46).

## Follow-up

- Within one week of each coordination call: notes to the FCT contact and the client; tasks in [CRM]; advisor map updated.
- Before each progress meeting: the LA checks that every recommendation touching another advisor's domain has been previewed with them.
- Annually: advisor map and consent letter re-confirmed with the client; conflicts register updated.

## Do / Don't

**Do**

- Let the client introduce you; let the client decide when advisors disagree.
- Preview anything that touches another advisor's domain with them before the client hears it.
- Keep one thread per topic and copy the client on substance.
- Ask the FCT what *they* need from the practice, not only what the practice needs from them.

**Don't**

- Don't share anything the consent letter does not cover, including the existence of the relationship.
- Don't solicit assets managed by the FCT or a sister company of [Firm] (SOP 50).
- Don't let a coordination failure become the client's problem to referee.
- Don't take a family-structure recommendation to the family without counsel's knowledge.

## Mode A / Mode B

### Mode A — whole family

- The Advisor is the coordinator; incumbent professionals may join the expert network (SOP 31) with the client's agreement.
- The standing advisory board (step 11) and the annual owners meeting (step 12) are the two coordination forums; the family council, where it exists, is the interface.

### Mode B — individual within a larger team

- Steps 1–9 in full. The scope rung sets the depth: B-full — quarterly call and preview of every structure-touching item; B-partial — quarterly call and participation in the FCT's process on invitation; B-narrow — reporting to the mandate's owner on the agreed schedule and nothing more.
- On the death or incapacity of a principal, the FCT leads; the Advisor supports his individuals and follows the FCT's communication plan.

## Metrics

- Advisor map current for every client (annual review completed).
- Share of structure-touching recommendations previewed with the relevant advisor before the client meeting (target 100%).
- Disagreements escalated to the client without a written two-position memo (target zero).
