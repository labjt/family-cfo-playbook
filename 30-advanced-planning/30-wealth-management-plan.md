# 30 — The wealth management plan

The wealth management plan is the document that turns the practice from investment consulting into wealth management. It has three parts — the profile, the investment plan, and the advanced-planning plan — and the third part is a living tracker that is presented at the first Regular Progress Meeting and revisited at every one after. This spec defines what the document contains, how it is written, and how often it is refreshed.

## Purpose

- Give the client one integrated view of everything the practice is doing and proposing across investments, tax, transfer, protection, and giving.
- Give the Advisor and the expert network a single prioritized list of actions, each with an owner, a status, and a next step, so nothing is lost between meetings.
- Make the plan safe to present: every recommendation has a rationale, an expert, a consequence check, and a client decision recorded against it.

## Contents

### Part 0 — Cover and scope banner

- Client(s) and households or entities covered; plan version and date; the mode and scope rung from T05.
- **Scope banner:** one paragraph stating what the plan covers and what it does not (for example, trusts administered by another fiduciary, assets managed by the family's central team). Mode B: every Part 3 section is tagged *in scope* / *family-team-owned* / *coordination-only*.
- The one-page "big picture": the four areas with their completed, in-progress, and future items on a single page (T10 summary block).

### Part 1 — Total Client Profile summary

The seven areas of SOP 11 in one to two pages: values, goals, relationships, assets (including held-away assets and where they sit), advisors, process preference, interests. In Mode A, the family-level map is summarized here and the household detail sits in an appendix.

### Part 2 — Investment plan

A pointer to the investment plan and IPS (SOP 13, T06) with the current version date and any changes since the last plan. Do not duplicate the IPS here.

### Part 3 — Advanced-planning plan

One section per area, each with the same structure:

1. **Definition** (Bowen's four areas, in the practice's words): *wealth enhancement* — the best return consistent with the client's risk tolerance after tax; *wealth transfer* — passing assets to the next generations tax-efficiently and in the way the client wants; *wealth protection* — shielding the family from creditors, litigants, divorcing spouses, and catastrophic loss; *charitable planning* — fulfilling the family's giving intentions, often while supporting the other three.
2. **Current situation** — two to five sentences from the profile and the documents reviewed.
3. **Recommendations**, prioritized by the impact each would have on the client's life, each with: the recommendation; the expert responsible (ISB / EEN / COA); the rationale; the consequence check (what could go wrong, who is affected); the client decision — drop / defer / do (Mode B: refer to family team); the status — completed / in progress / future; a SMART next step with owner and date.
4. **Open questions for counsel or the family** — items the practice cannot decide.

The four areas are not silos; the plan cross-references items that belong to two areas (a trust that is both transfer and protection).

#### Agenda menus by area

The following are checklists of what to consider, not recommendations. Each item enters the plan only after the expert-network meeting (SOP 32) and counsel where required.

!!! extension "Beyond Bowen — wealth enhancement menu"
    Investor allocation: which family member or entity holds which asset, matched to the 20 / 50 / 100-year horizons (SOP 47) · family bank and intra-family loans, documented arm's-length with counsel · a tax-drag and fee audit across all managers and structures · deferral over income where the client's goals allow · reserve mechanisms in trusts for recurring obligations.

!!! extension "Beyond Bowen — wealth transfer menu"
    Estate-tax exposure above the exemption and the generation-skipping picture · gift versus transfer: purpose, message, and the recipient's readiness decided before the vehicle · who-before-what: the giver's own goals, then the recipient, then the structure · the equality default and early, clear communication of any exception · staged disclosure: existence and purpose of structures first, numbers later · a preamble or letter of wishes and purpose language beyond the standard distribution formula · a one-page plain-language summary and a communication plan for every new trust, gift, or plan (T21) · the do-no-harm consequences check and the perpetual-trust counterweight conversation before any dynasty or long-term structure · addiction and behavioral-health provisions reviewed with counsel · fund growth assets into trusts before they appreciate · for counsel's consideration: leveraged sale to a trust, intentionally defective grantor trust, valuation discounts, trust situs review · the insurance-objections checklist as devil's advocate for any ILIT or premium-finance proposal (fees compounding, long-horizon certainty, counterparty lock-in, cash payouts consumed) · business succession, including the ESOP path assessed with [ESOP Co.].

!!! extension "Beyond Bowen — wealth protection menu"
    The humane trustscape: distribution committee, office of the beneficiary, a narrow mediative protector, institutional trustee (SOP 46, SOP 48) · protector provisions and a workable trustee-succession mechanism · the prenuptial process — months before the wedding, disclosure of trust interests, family policy set in advance · asset-protection structures with counsel · property and casualty review, identity-theft exposure, personal security · jurisdictional and residency review as a counsel-only conversation; expatriation is out of scope · family properties treated as an identity anchor: a property map, a policy against dividing them, and a plan for who uses and maintains them.

!!! extension "Beyond Bowen — charitable planning menu"
    Placement rules: a section on the family's responsibility to the outside world in the mission statement; the share of financial and qualitative capital devoted to giving on the family balance sheet; a formal vehicle broad enough for every member to participate · the seven readiness questions (tradition, obligation or choice, organizations supported, sectors, board roles, giving at death, giving as much as wished) · donor-advised fund versus private foundation · a grandchild–grandparent giving program with age-scaled participation · young-adult engagement rules: open process, minimal restrictions, subjects that draw them in, let a disappointing grant teach · philanthropy as the family's governance rehearsal.

### Part 4 — Decision log and next steps

A dated log of every client decision (drop / defer / do / refer) with the reasoning recorded at the meeting, and a consolidated next-steps table by owner and date.

### Part 5 — Appendix

Expert-network contributors and their roles; documents reviewed (returns, estate documents, trust instruments, policies, entity documents); glossary of structures named in the plan; Mode A: the household-level profiles.

## Standards

- **Length:** the big picture fits one page; Part 3 is as long as the family's situation requires but each recommendation fits half a page.
- **Language:** plain English; every structure is named in the glossary; no product names in the body.
- **Completeness:** no recommendation without an expert, a rationale, a consequence check, and a next step. "Establish additional priorities" is a legitimate future item in each area.
- **Ownership:** the Advisor is the author and quarterback; experts contribute sections and are named; the client hires the attorney directly and legal fees are never shared.
- **Review:** peer-reviewed by a second professional and signed off by the LA before presentation; drafted from the expert-network meeting output, never from a single specialist's proposal.
- **Disclosure:** any fee or commission arrangement with a contributing expert is disclosed in the plan and cleared with [Firm Compliance].
- **Confidentiality:** the plan respects the consent matrix on T05 / T14; Mode A household detail is not circulated to other households.
- **Sequence:** the plan is presented only at a Regular Progress Meeting (SOP 20); nothing in it is executed before counsel and the consequences check.

## Refresh cadence

- Tracker (T10) updated within 48 hours of every RPM and every expert-network meeting.
- Full plan re-issued annually, or on any trigger in SOP 22 (liquidity event, death or incapacity, marriage, business sale, relocation).
- Part 1 refreshed whenever the profile changes; Part 2 on the annual IPS review.

## Owner

LA owns the document; AA maintains the tracker and drafts sections from expert input; CSA files each version to the [Document vault] and the client notebook (T08).

## Mode A / Mode B

### Mode A — whole family

- The plan has a family level (structures, governance items, philanthropy, family bank) and household levels; family-level items are presented at the annual family meeting (SOP 43) and tracked on the family-level T10.
- Trust items are routed through the trustee relationship in SOP 46; the family's other advisors are named in Part 5 and coordinated under SOP 33.

### Mode B — individual within a larger team

- The scope banner is mandatory; each Part 3 section carries an *in scope* / *family-team-owned* / *coordination-only* tag from T05.
- Items touching family-level structures are written as questions or recommendations for the FCT or family counsel, never as actions the Advisor will execute.
- Distribution of the plan follows the consent on T05; family-team-owned sections may be shared with the FCT only when the individual has authorized it.
