# 31 — Expert network

Advanced planning cannot be delivered by one person. This procedure builds the team of specialists the Advisor brings to each family — an external core, an extended external roster, and the sister-company bench inside [Firm] — vets them, puts them on written terms, and keeps them working as one team with the Advisor in charge.

## Purpose

- Put the two core external experts in place **before** advanced planning is promised to any family.
- Turn [Firm]'s trust, ESOP, tax, insurance, and lending capabilities into a named, scored, on-call bench.
- Fix the rules of engagement: the clients are the Advisor's, fees are disclosed, conflicts are managed, and no expert becomes a client's default contact.

## Trigger

- The practice commits to offering advanced planning (initial build).
- A Total Client Profile or network meeting reveals a need no current member can meet.
- A member fails a review, leaves, or a conflict cannot be managed.
- Annually, on the service calendar (T15), for the review of every external relationship.

## Roles

Owner: LA · Does: CSA (roster, scorecards, written terms on file, disclosure filings), AA (shortlist research, reference calls, brief preparation) · Consulted: [Firm Compliance] (licensing, fee sharing, disclosures), sister-company leaders for bench nominations.

## Timing

| Stage | Target |
|---|---|
| Core two external members identified and on terms | before the first family is told advanced planning is part of the service |
| Internal bench mapped | within 30 days of this SOP's adoption; refreshed each quarter |
| Extended roster | built need by need, each within 60 days of the need appearing |
| Vetting one candidate | shortlist to decision in 4–6 weeks; trial matter within the following quarter |
| Annual review | Q3 on the service calendar, before the Q4 planning cycle |

## Inputs

- The four advanced-planning areas (wealth enhancement, transfer, protection, charitable) and the issues currently open across all families (T10 trackers).
- [Firm]'s directory of sister companies and their specialists; [Firm Compliance] rules on fee sharing, gifts, and licensing.
- Existing relationships: the client's own advisors (mapped on T05) — potential members, never automatically excluded.
- Referrals from other advisors and from the clients' own professionals.

## Prep checklist

- [ ] Needs inventory: for each family, which of the four areas has an open item that needs expertise the practice does not hold.
- [ ] Bench nominations requested from each sister company with a one-paragraph description of what the practice needs from them.
- [ ] Vetting interview guide and reference-call script ready (below).
- [ ] Written-terms template reviewed by [Firm Compliance].
- [ ] Roster and scorecard set up in [CRM].

## Procedure

### Build

1. **Define the network from the clients' needs, not the market.** List the open issues by area; group them into capabilities. Every capability gets exactly one route: core external member, extended external member, internal bench, or the client's own advisor.
2. **Fill the core two first.** A private-client attorney who is a partner in a high-end boutique (not a trust officer inside a bank or insurer) and a truly independent insurance specialist who receives no carrier incentives. The attorney addresses tax, estate, and legal questions; the insurance specialist works alongside the attorney on risk-transfer structures. Until both are on terms, advanced planning is described to clients as "coordinated," not "delivered."
3. **Map the Internal Specialist Bench.** For each sister company, name the person, what they solve, how to engage them (SOP 71), the economics [Firm] applies, and the confidentiality wall if that company also serves a client's family centrally.

    | Bench member | Solves | Typical trigger |
    |---|---|---|
    | [Trust Co.] — trust officer / fiduciary lead | trust administration, trustee services, distribution process, trust reviews | any trust in the family map; SOP 46 and 48 |
    | [ESOP Co.] — transaction lead | business succession and liquidity via an employee-ownership sale | a closely held business with no successor or a sale under consideration |
    | Sister RIA — planning analyst | plan modelling, IPS drafting capacity, second-chair analysis | capacity gaps before the first hire (SOP 70) |
    | Tax specialist | complex income tax, entity structure, state residency | multi-entity families; a liquidity event |
    | Insurance / risk | property-casualty review, liability exposure | wealth-protection items on T10 |
    | Lending | intra-family and secured credit structuring | investor allocation and family-bank funding (SOP 47) |

4. **Extend the external roster as needs appear.** Rely on the core two to bring in their own experts first (an actuary behind the insurance specialist, a corporate tax lawyer or valuation specialist behind the attorney). Add a direct member only where the practice will use them more than once a year: credit, corporate tax, derivatives and concentrated-stock strategies, securities law, income tax, actuary, valuation.

    !!! extension "Beyond Bowen"
        Families at this level need members Bowen's roster does not include: a **family-governance facilitator** for first family meetings, mission sessions, and crises; a **counselor or coach** for individual members; people able to sit on a **Distribution Committee** (advising a trustee, never deciding) or in an **Office of the Beneficiary** (chosen and paid by the beneficiary — never housed inside a family office or inside [Firm]). Vet these on the personne-de-confiance qualities: an interest in the family's culture, belief in orderly evolutionary change, and willingness to be a "number two."

### Vet

5. **Shortlist five.** Sources: the core members' own recommendations, other advisors, professional bodies, the clients' existing advisors. Never run a one-candidate search.
6. **Interview for the four traits** — specific expertise, integrity, professionalism, chemistry — plus the practice's own fit. Ask:
    - Describe the last three matters like ours you handled; what did the client decide and why?
    - How do you work when another professional quarterbacks the relationship?
    - Whom do you bring in when a matter is outside your depth, and how are they paid?
    - How do you communicate with clients between milestones?
    - What would make you decline a matter we send?
    - How are you compensated on the work we would send, in full?
    - What does a family that is unhappy with you say about you?
7. **Check references the candidate did not choose.** Ask each reference whom else the candidate works with, then call those firms; ask a client-side reference how the candidate handled a disagreement.
8. **Run a trial.** A meaningful but not mission-critical matter, or — for counselors, coaches, and facilitators — the two-meeting trial: two sessions to assess fit on both sides before any standing engagement; either party may stop without cost.

    !!! extension "Beyond Bowen"
        Counselor / coach selection criteria: training; a medically accepted license where the work touches mental health; experience in both practice and counseling; a match between that experience and the family's actual challenge. Readiness on the client side matters as much as the professional's credentials.

### Onboard

9. **Put the relationship in writing.** Scope of what the practice will send; the rule that the Advisor remains the client's point of contact and decision coordinator; communication protocol (who contacts the client, cc rules, turnaround); confidentiality and the internal wall where relevant; compensation and disclosure; conflicts declared at signing; how either side ends the relationship.
10. **Welcome the member into how the practice works.** A one-hour session on the consultative process, the five-meeting sequence, the family layer, the trackers, and the network-meeting format (SOP 32). Introduce them to the CSA who will schedule and chase.

    !!! extension "Beyond Bowen"
        Mark the arrival of a new adviser with a small ritual — an introduction at the next network meeting and, for a family-facing member, a named introduction to the family at the next meeting. People who are formally welcomed perform their roles better.

11. **Add to the roster and open a scorecard**: responsiveness, quality of work, client feedback, fee transparency, willingness to collaborate, conflicts declared. Score after every matter.

### Maintain

12. **Run the quarterly network meeting** (SOP 32). This is where the team bonds and where most disagreement about approach is worked out before it reaches a client.
13. **Keep the network running well** — five rules: hold a common vision (comprehensive, client-centered work with capable peers and fair reward); be clear who is in charge (the clients are the Advisor's; the Advisor decides what is best for them); formalize how and when members communicate; improve the meetings by asking members for feedback; keep members motivated by making sure good work becomes income for them.
14. **Review every external relationship annually.** Six questions per member: is the fit still strategic; exclusive or one of several, and what does that cost or buy; how concentrated is our business with them and does it buy priority; how durable are they (ownership change, retirement, exit); how much do they customize for us; how do clients perceive them? Never become captive — keep the practice's own process even where a member does most of the work.
15. **Maintain the conflicts register.** For every member: the conflict (fee sharing, dual roles, sister-company relationships, drafting attorney named as trustee), the management step, who has been told. Acknowledge, document, disclose to all affected parties, manage — never assume away.
16. **Replace or remove** when a scorecard falls below standard for two matters, a conflict becomes unmanageable, or a client asks. Do it directly and respectfully, in writing, with a handover of open items.

## Follow-up

- Within one week of any new member joining: written terms signed, roster updated, disclosure filed with [Firm Compliance], introduction scheduled.
- Within one week of every matter closing: scorecard updated; client feedback captured at the next progress meeting.
- Annually: review results and roster changes recorded; conflicts register re-confirmed; the family told of any change that affects them (SOP 21 first-to-know rule).

## Do / Don't

**Do**

- Have the core two in place before promising advanced planning.
- Let the core members bring their own sub-experts before adding direct members.
- Disclose every fee arrangement to the client in writing; keep the practice properly licensed for anything it is paid on.
- Charge a premium for the investment work that carries the coordination, and a project fee for strategies with no product.

**Don't**

- Don't accept any share of legal fees — the client hires the attorney directly.
- Don't use an insurance specialist who is incented by a carrier.
- Don't let a member become the client's primary contact, or contact a client outside the agreed protocol.
- Don't house an Office of the Beneficiary inside [Firm] or a family office; the beneficiary owns it.

!!! warning "Compensation and disclosure norms"
    Typical arrangements: a split of insurance commissions between the practice and the specialist; a finder's fee or points on credit; a share of commissions on derivative transactions; no share of legal fees ever. All of it is disclosed to the client, approved by [Firm Compliance], and recorded on the conflicts register. Internal cross-referral economics inside [Firm] follow firm policy and are always set below what an external referral source would receive.

## Mode A / Mode B

### Mode A — whole family

- The client's incumbent CPA and attorney are candidates for the network, not competitors; invite them into the standing advisory board (SOP 33).
- Family-facing members (facilitator, counselor) are introduced through the family map's communication rules (T14).

### Mode B — individual within a larger team

- The family's counsel and trust company **are** the private-client attorney and the fiduciary. The practice's network fills gaps only, with the FCT's knowledge, and never duplicates a role the FCT already holds.
- Any fee sharing is disclosed to the individual and, where the FCT gatekeeps professional relationships, to the FCT.
- Where a sister company of [Firm] sits on the FCT, the internal wall is stated in the written terms and in the scope memo (T25).

## Metrics

- Core two on terms: yes/no (a gating metric).
- Share of open advanced-planning items with a named expert assigned within 30 days.
- Average scorecard across members; number of unmanaged conflicts (target zero).
