# 32 — Expert-network meeting

The quarterly meeting at which the Advisor presents one to six families to the expert network and leaves with a draft advanced-planning map for each. It is the engine behind Part 3 of every wealth management plan and the place where the network becomes a team.

## Purpose

- Convert each family's Total Client Profile into prioritized, expert-assigned actions across wealth enhancement, transfer, protection, and charitable planning.
- Give every family the benefit of several specialists thinking together, without the family paying for a roomful of hourly time.
- Bond the network through regular, structured collaboration.

## Trigger

- Standing: quarterly on the service calendar (T15), monthly if the family count justifies it.
- Gating: the two core external members (SOP 31) are on terms.
- Per family: a family's investment plan has been accepted (CCM3) and its first advanced-planning plan is due for the first progress meeting; or a life event on T10 or in SOP 22 opens a new set of questions.

## Roles

Owner: LA (facilitates; presents every case) · Does: AA (case briefs, scenario worksheet, draft map), CSA (agenda circulation, attendance, minutes, follow-up tasks) · Consulted: EEN members, ISB members invited for the cases that need them.

## Timing

| When | What |
|---|---|
| T−3 weeks | Families selected (one to six); consent confirmed for each; experts invited by need |
| T−2 weeks | Case briefs drafted by the AA from the Total Client Profile and T10 |
| T−1 week | Agenda and briefs circulated (T09); conflicts declaration requested |
| T+0 | Meeting — 90 minutes for up to three families; half a day for six |
| T+3 days | Draft advanced-planning map per family sent to the assigned experts for correction |
| T+2 weeks | Map finalized; recommendations scheduled into each family's next progress meeting |

## Inputs

- Total Client Profile (SOP 11) and, in Mode A, the family map and five-capitals summary (T14, SOP 44).
- The current tracker (T10) — what is completed, in progress, or planned.
- Consent status on T05 / T14: which facts may be shared, and whether the brief must be anonymized.
- Prior-quarter statements and the investment plan's scope banner (SOP 13).
- Open questions raised by the client at the last progress meeting.

## Prep checklist

- [ ] Case list agreed: which families, in what order, and the one decision each case needs.
- [ ] Case briefs (T09) complete: seven areas in summary, structures, issues, and the questions for the network — one page each.
- [ ] Anonymization applied where consent is limited; names replaced by case codes in circulated material.
- [ ] Experts confirmed for each case; the attorney and insurance specialist attend every meeting.
- [ ] Conflicts declaration collected from each attending member.
- [ ] Agenda circulated with time allotted per case.
- [ ] Video or phone bridge tested; in person for the first two meetings with any new member.

## Procedure

1. **Open** (5 minutes). Restate the purpose — decisions and priorities for the families on the list — and the confidentiality rule for the room. Confirm conflicts declarations.
2. **Present the case** (LA, 10 minutes). Walk the one-page brief: who the family is, what they value and want, the structures in place, the issues, and the specific questions. Do not present solutions.
3. **Identify the key issues** (all, 10 minutes). Let each expert name what they see from their discipline; the AA records issues on the scenario worksheet.
4. **Build scenarios** (15 minutes). For the two or three issues that matter most: what happens if the family does nothing; what happens under each credible course of action; what the family has said they care about that constrains the answer.
5. **Identify opportunities** (10 minutes). Concrete actions per area — enhancement, transfer, protection, charitable — with the expert who would carry each.
6. **Prioritize** (5 minutes). Rank by impact on the family's stated goals, then by urgency and cost. Mark each as **completed / in progress / future** on the draft map. Prioritization is the Advisor's call; the experts advise.

    !!! extension "Beyond Bowen"
        Scenario prompts to run when they apply: a **liquidity event** ahead (sale, IPO, settlement); a **concentrated position**; **business succession**, including whether an employee-ownership sale through [ESOP Co.] fits; a **generational transition** in leadership or ownership; a member **marrying in** (prenuptial timing, disclosure of trust interests); **incapacity** of a principal; a **trust termination** or large distribution within ten years; **family-office formation**. Add the family's five-capitals picture to the brief: an action that grows only financial capital while draining human or social capital is not a good recommendation.

7. **Assign owners and next steps** (5 minutes). Every "in progress" or "future" item gets an expert, a next step, and a date. Decide which items warrant the expert joining the family's next progress meeting in person or by video.
8. **Close the case** by reading back the draft map. Move to the next family.
9. **Close the meeting**: five minutes for what worked and what to change next time; schedule the next meeting.
10. **Debrief** (LA and AA, same day): finalize the draft maps, note anything to tell the family before the progress meeting so there are no surprises.

    !!! script "Say something like"
        "For this family, if we could only get one thing done this year, which item would you choose and why?"

## Follow-up

- Within three days: draft maps to assigned experts for correction; minutes to attendees.
- Within two weeks: maps final; T10 updated; each family's next progress meeting agenda updated with the items to present and which expert attends.
- Before each progress meeting: the LA calls any expert who will present to align on how the recommendation will be framed.

## Do / Don't

**Do**

- Present the family, not a product; let the room find the solutions.
- Keep to one to six families per meeting; depth over coverage.
- Bring the relevant expert into the family's meeting for complex structures rather than relaying their advice second-hand.
- Ask members every quarter how to make the meeting better.

**Don't**

- Don't circulate identifiable client detail beyond what consent allows.
- Don't let an expert take a recommendation directly to the family before the Advisor has presented the map.
- Don't turn the meeting into a pitch session for any member's products.
- Don't skip a quarter because "nothing changed" — transitions are usually visible in the room before the family names them.

## Mode A / Mode B

### Mode A — whole family

- Family-level items (governance, family bank, trusts across households) are presented as family cases; household-level items as household cases, with the family map's confidentiality matrix (T14) governing what each expert sees.
- Recommendations that touch family structures are routed through the family council or the annual family meeting (SOP 43), not to individual members.

### Mode B — individual within a larger team

- **B-full:** run the meeting scoped to the mandate; the brief is anonymized and limited to the individual's own affairs; anything touching family structures becomes a question for family counsel, framed as a recommendation to take to the FCT.
- **B-partial:** the case is reviewed only if the FCT invites the practice's input; otherwise an annual internal scoping check replaces the network review.
- **B-narrow:** not run; the mandate is delivered and reported.

## Metrics

- Families reviewed per quarter against families due.
- Share of "in progress" items with an owner and a date (target 100%).
- Median days from meeting to final map (target ≤ 14).
