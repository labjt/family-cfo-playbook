Phase 5 · The planning agent, if you want it

about 30 minutes — optional

Step 16 5 minutes

Decide whether to turn this on at all.

Hazel's planning agent is a well-built retirement-adequacy engine. Your families are not asking whether they will run out of money. Read the two columns and make a decision — either answer is fine.

Three details that tell you who it was built for

  • The three personas it ships with are life stages organised around a retirement date: high earners aged 28 to 44, pre-retirees 55 to 67, and retirees 65 and up.
  • The default plan includes an emergency fund measured against three to six months of spending, and a debt schedule that pays off balances above 6% APR first.
  • One of the two rules it ships with is about avoiding a Medicare surcharge bracket.
What to do

Skip it for now. Run the practice on the meeting types and the client record for a quarter. Those two things carry most of the value and cost you nothing extra. Come back to this page when the rest is habit — and when you have a real household you would use it on.

If you do turn it on

Treat it as a drafting tool for the narrative and shape of a deliverable, not as an analysis engine. It writes a good document in your voice. The $30M analysis stays where it already is: in your head, in your instructions to it, and in the numbers you hand it. It is also a paid add-on on top of the base platform, so there is real money attached to the decision.

Done when

You have decided yes or no. If the answer is no, tick this step, skip Steps 17 and 18, and go straight to Phase 6 — that is a perfectly good outcome and nothing later depends on it.

Step 17 12 minutes

Set the firm rules before you build a single plan.

Three settings decide what every plan is allowed to say and what appears at the bottom of the client's copy. Set them now and every plan inherits them. Set them later and you will be re-reading plans you already sent.

Do this — the disclaimer first

  1. Go to Settings → Planning and open Base template.
  2. Find Client disclaimer. The box shows suggested wording, but it is not in force until you edit and save it. Until somebody does, every plan and every PDF goes out with no footer at all.
  3. Paste in the wording your compliance group approves. This is the one planning setting that works backwards — Hazel reads it fresh each time a plan opens, so fixing it later also fixes plans you already built.

Then the guardrails

  1. On the same pane, look at Guardrails — the never-do rules that no persona, plan or request can override.
  2. Two ship by default. One stops Hazel proposing a Roth conversion that crosses a Medicare surcharge bracket; that is not what drives decisions at this level, so remove it. The other stops Hazel naming a specific security or ticker — keep that one unless your investment documents are meant to name instruments.
  3. Click + Add a rule and add your own. Two to six is the right number. The test: could a colleague read a plan and tell whether it broke the rule?
Suggested guardrails — add one at a time
Never present a probability-of-success figure as the headline conclusion of a plan.

Never state a transfer-tax exposure, exemption amount or gifting capacity as settled. Name the exposure and refer the decision to counsel.

Never recommend creating, amending or terminating a trust or entity, and never recommend a change to a beneficiary designation. Describe the question and route it to counsel.

Never treat an illiquid holding as available to fund spending unless a current valuation or a signed sale agreement is on file.

Then the numbers the solver cannot model

  1. Still on Base template, open Planning assumptions. These are free-text label and value rows joined to every plan.
  2. The value accepts a number, a percentage or a plain sentence — so this is where anything the built-in settings have no field for belongs.
  3. Add the rows below, filling in the figures yourself.
Suggested assumption rows — label, then value
Longevity age | 95

Check every threshold, every year

Exemptions and exclusions change. Hazel will repeat whatever you type here, indefinitely, without noticing it has gone stale. Put a diary note to re-read this pane every January, and never let a figure from this list reach a client without checking it first.

Which setting wins

Where an instruction and an assumption disagree about a number, the assumption wins. Rules cannot be loosened by anything — not a persona, not a plan, not a request. Instructions are guidance Hazel weighs. Put anything that must always happen in a rule or in the layout, not in an instruction.

Done when

The disclaimer is saved in your own words, you have two to six rules that fit your practice, and the assumption rows are filled in with figures you have checked this year.

Procedure: SOP 30 — the wealth management plan

Step 18 14 minutes

Build one persona that actually matches your families.

A persona is how you tell Hazel what kind of client this is and how a plan for them should read. Build one, and every plan for a family like yours comes out in your shape instead of somebody else's retirement template.

Do this

  1. Go to Settings → Planning and find Client persona in the left rail.
  2. Click New persona. Four fields appear.
  3. Starting template — choose Base template. The shipped presets bring their own retirement-shaped design with them, which is the thing you are trying to avoid.
  4. Name — something you will recognise in a list. Multi-generational family works.
  5. Description — paste the block below. This is what Hazel matches households against, so it does the real work.
  6. Age min and Age max — leave both blank. Your families are not defined by an age band, and a band would pull the wrong households in.
  7. Click Create.
Paste into “Description”
A family with substantial multi-generational wealth, typically thirty million dollars or more, held across several structures — trusts, partnerships, a closely held operating business, or a concentrated stock position. Whether they can afford to retire is not the question. The plan turns on how the wealth is structured, how and when it moves to the next generation, how much of it can actually be turned into cash, and how the family makes decisions together. There are usually other advisers already involved: an attorney, an accountant, sometimes a trustee or a family office.

Then write the instructions

  1. The persona's pane opens. Find Hazel instructions and click Add instruction.
  2. Add the eight below one at a time, in this order. Press Enter after each — Enter commits the row and opens the next.
  3. Order matters: Hazel works down the list, so the first ones carry the most weight. There are no drag handles, so if you want a different order you have to retype the rows.
  4. Each instruction is capped at 400 characters. A longer paste is refused outright rather than trimmed.
Paste into “Hazel instructions” — one row at a time
Lead with structure and transfer. Open with what the family owns, through which entities, and what happens to it in the next generation. Do not open with retirement adequacy.

Do not include an emergency fund section or a debt paydown schedule. Neither is a live question for this family, and both read as advice written for somebody else.

Treat any probability-of-success figure as background rather than as the conclusion. If it appears at all, place it late and say plainly what it measures and what it does not.

Name every trust, entity and operating business the record supports, with who controls it and who benefits from it. A plan that only discusses "the portfolio" has missed most of the balance sheet.

State transfer-tax questions as questions for counsel, never as settled figures. Name the exposure, name the decision that follows from it, and name who has to answer it.

Where a holding is illiquid — a business, real estate, a concentrated position — say so explicitly and keep it separate from what can actually fund spending.

Write for an audience that includes the family's attorney and accountant. Assume the document will be forwarded to them and read closely.

Keep every section to one page or less. Cut adjectives. Say the number and the decision it drives.
Instructions cannot set numbers

Writing "assume 4% real returns" or "hold five years of cash" in an instruction does nothing to the maths. Numbers live in the assumption panes from Step 17, and the assumption always wins. Instructions decide what a plan leads with, what it leaves out, and how it reads.

Last, the shape

  1. On the same pane, open Plan design. This is the layout: which sections exist, in what order.
  2. Remove the modules that do not belong — the emergency fund and the debt schedule — and add sections for the four areas you actually plan across. An empty tab with a heading and instructions works: Hazel composes it from those instructions alone, so you do not have to find a module that matches.
  3. Set Strictness to Exact if you want every plan to come out in the same shape — the setting exists for exactly this, a deliverable format the firm has standardised on.
Read the first few

Even on Exact, a plan occasionally publishes with something not matching the design. Read the first three plans this persona produces properly, before you put one in front of a family.

Done when

You have built a plan for one real household, it opened with structure rather than retirement, and there was no emergency fund section in it.
