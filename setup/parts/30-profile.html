Phase 2 · Make the client record fill itself

about 25 minutes

Step 7 15 minutes

Upload the paperwork for your top ten families.

Facts that come from a document arrive already confirmed. Ten minutes of uploading gives a family a trustworthy record before you have had a single meeting.

Do this, for each of your ten largest relationships

  1. Open Contacts, pick the household, open its Documents tab.
  2. Drag in the core packet: most recent custodial statements, the latest tax return, insurance declaration pages, and the estate documents — the will and any trust deeds.
  3. Wait for each file to reach the green In profile state. Files are read one at a time per household.
  4. For anything older, check the date on the document row and set it by hand if Hazel guessed. Newer facts overwrite older ones, so a mis-dated old statement can overwrite something current.
  5. Open the Data tab, spend two minutes in Review, and assign an owner to anything sitting under Needs owner.
Export, do not scan

Pull PDFs straight from the custodian portal or the tax software. Scanned or photographed pages read badly and are the most common cause of a failed upload. Limit is 10 MB per file.

Uploading to the household vs the person

A document on the household is shared with everyone in it. A document on an individual is linked only to them. That single choice is the only privacy control you get inside a family record — see the warning in the next step.

Done when

Ten households each show documents In profile, and their Data tabs have real content in the accounts, insurance and estate sections.

Step 8 10 minutes

Change four small habits and the record maintains itself.

The profile only learns from what it can hear. These four habits are the difference between a record that grows on its own and one you end up typing.

The four habits

  1. Say the number, the institution and the owner out loud. "Her rollover IRA at Fidelity, about one-point-two." A figure that is only on a screen cannot be captured, and "the IRA" with no owner named goes into a queue for you to sort out later.
  2. Get the "no" on the record. "So — no long-term care cover today, correct?" turns a silence into a recorded, confirmed fact. Absences matter as much as holdings.
  3. Recap for sixty seconds at the end of every meeting. It is the cheapest way to make sure the important things were actually said.
  4. Spend two minutes in Review afterwards. Verify what you would act on — not everything. Financial figures heard in conversation start unconfirmed until somebody confirms them.

Where the rest of the profile goes

Hazel's client record has six fixed sections and you cannot add your own. Four of your seven areas fit — goals, assets, advisers and the estate structure. Three do not: values, process preferences and interests. Put those in a note on the household, where Hazel will read them and use them in chat, prep and agendas.

Paste as a note on each household, then fill it in
VALUES — what they told us matters most about the money, in their words:

GOALS BEYOND THE NUMBERS — what the money is actually for:

HOW THEY WANT TO BE WORKED WITH — channel, frequency, who to contact, what to never do:

INTERESTS AND PEOPLE — what they care about outside money; who matters to them:

FAMILY POSITION — which generation; who else in the family we serve; who their other advisers are:

CONFIDENTIAL — anything a member asked us not to share, and who it must not reach:
Read this before you write anything sensitive

Access to a household in Hazel is all or nothing. Anyone who can see the family can see the whole record, every document and every meeting — and firm Owners can always see everything. There is no per-person privacy inside a household. If something genuinely must not reach another family member or another adviser, do not put it in the shared household record at all. This is a hard limit of the product, not a setting you have missed.

Done when

Your ten households each have the note above filled in, and you have caught yourself saying an account owner's name out loud in a real meeting.
