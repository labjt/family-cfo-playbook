Step 19 5 minutes

Three habits, and the system stays alive.

Everything you have installed only works if these three things happen. They take about fifteen minutes a week in total.

Before every client meeting — 2 minutes

  • Open the meeting in Hazel and check the meeting type is right. This is the single most important click in the whole system: it decides the agenda, the notes format and the follow-up email.
  • Check the household is attached. If it is not, Hazel cannot file the notes anywhere.
  • Read the agenda it prepared. Add anything it could not know.

After every client meeting — 8 minutes

  • Review the notes, fix what it misheard, and sync them to your CRM. Nothing syncs on its own.
  • Review the tasks it generated and sync those too.
  • Read the follow-up email, make it sound like you, and send it.
  • Spend two minutes in the client's Review queue confirming the figures you would actually act on.

Friday — 5 minutes

  • Look at the week's meetings. Anything still sitting at Ready for Review has notes nobody has filed.
  • Check the decisions from any progress meeting made it into the tracker.
The one that breaks first

The after-meeting review. Skip it for a fortnight and the client record fills with unconfirmed figures, the CRM goes stale, and the agendas Hazel prepares get worse — because they are built from the notes you never filed. It is a self-reinforcing loop in both directions.

Done when

You have run one real meeting end to end: right type selected, notes reviewed and synced, follow-up email sent.

Step 20 5 minutes

Four things to revisit, and when.

Set these as recurring reminders now, while you are thinking about it. Each one takes under an hour.

WhenWhat
MonthlyRead one set of meeting notes as if you were the client. If the notes are wrong in the same way twice, fix the meeting type's prompt rather than the notes.
QuarterlyRun the expert-network cycle from the Playbook. Check whether any prompt you keep editing by hand should just be changed at the source.
Twice a yearRe-read your Modes and your meeting types. They drift out of date faster than you expect, and nothing tells you they have.
AnnuallyThe fee conversation, the engagement re-scoping, and the client survey — all from the Playbook. If you turned on the planning agent, review its assumptions with whoever owns investment policy.

When something is wrong

  • Notes are in the wrong shape. Edit the meeting type's Meeting Notes prompt, not the notes. You are correcting it once instead of every week.
  • Hazel keeps missing something. It was probably never said out loud. Say the number, the institution and the owner.
  • The notetaker did not join. Almost always the meeting host's platform settings, not Hazel — external participants are blocked.
  • Chat stops responding. Start a new one. One topic per conversation.
  • You are fighting the tool. The written procedure still stands on its own. Hazel is how the practice runs, not what it is.
That is the whole build

Twenty steps. From here it is meetings, reviews, and the occasional prompt edit. When you want the reasoning behind any of it, the Primer explains it; when you want the full procedure, the Playbook has it. You do not need to read either to keep going.

Done when

Four reminders are in your calendar.
