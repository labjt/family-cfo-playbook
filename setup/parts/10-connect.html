Phase 0 · Before you touch anything

about 40 minutes

Step 1 15 minutes

Check the seven things that can stop you cold.

Every one of these has wasted somebody a week. Four of them are decisions you cannot take back. Do this before you create an account, not after.

Work down this list

CheckWhy it matters
Has anyone else at the firm already tried Hazel?There is one free trial per firm, not per person. If the trust company or another RIA arm has already trialled it, yours may be gone. Ask before you sign up.
Agree who signs up first, and who is the Owner.Two people signing up separately create two firms that cannot be merged. Undoing it needs Hazel support to deactivate an account. This is the most expensive mistake available on day one.
Is your email Microsoft 365 / Enterprise, or Google?On-premise Microsoft Exchange is not supported. Email and calendar drive meeting prep, agendas and daily priorities — without them there is not much product left.
Will IT allow the Hazel app in your tenant?Large firms usually block third-party sign-in apps by default. Get it allow-listed before you try to connect anything.
Will your meeting platform let an outside participant join?The notetaker joins as an external guest. Teams and Google Meet both have tenant policies that block this. IT may say no — find out now.
Which single CRM will you connect?Hazel connects one CRM only. Connecting one disables the rest. If the trust company, the RIA and the ESOP arm each run their own, you have to choose, and the choice decides which clients exist inside Hazel at all.
Has compliance seen this?Three things they will ask about, so bring them first: the email connection requests a send permission (Hazel drafts but never sends); client meetings get recorded; and the security attestation is SOC 2 Type I today, with Type II expected imminently — verify which is current before you brief them. Say this accurately

The published attestation is SOC 2 Type I, with an annual independent penetration test. Type I says the controls are well designed; Type II says they work over time — and Type II is what a firm owning trust companies will ask for. Type II is expected imminently, so check the current status at trust.hazel.ai the day you brief compliance rather than repeating what this guide says. Either way, do not let it be summarised as just "SOC 2 certified" — name the type.

Done when

All seven have an answer, and you know who is signing up. If any of the middle three came back "no", stop — the rest of this guide will not work until IT changes something.

Step 2 8 minutes

Sign up as the Owner, and say yes to the calendar.

You become the person who can manage billing, roles and firm settings. Everyone you add later inherits the structure you set now.

Do this

  1. Go to auth.hazel.altruist.com and sign in with your work email or Altruist single sign-on.
  2. When it asks to connect your calendar, say yes immediately. See the warning below.
  3. Click Get started, then choose the base plan. You can add the tax and planning agents later.
  4. Pick monthly or annual, add a payment method, and start the 14-day free trial.
  5. When it offers to invite your team, click Skip. Do not invite anyone yet.
The one-shot prompt

The calendar consent screen appears once, on your first sign-in. If you dismiss it, Hazel never asks again — you have to go and find it in settings, and until you do, meeting prep and agendas barely work. Say yes the first time.

Why not invite the team yet

New members get access to everything imported from your CRM by default, and your CRM's permissions do not carry across. For a book of $30M families that is a confidentiality problem waiting to happen. Set up client access properly first — that is a later step.

Done when

You are inside Hazel, your trial has started, and your calendar shows as connected.

Step 3 12 minutes

Connect the CRM first. Everything else depends on it.

The CRM is what creates your client list inside Hazel. Without it you have an empty product — the custodian does not supply contacts, and email history will not backfill.

Do this, in this order

  1. Go to Settings → Integrations.
  2. Connect your CRM. Expect a message saying the sync may take ten to fifteen minutes. Wealthbox, Salesforce, Redtail, HubSpot and Quivr are supported.
  3. Connect email and calendar if the sign-in prompt did not already do it. Hazel pulls three years of past email, but only for people who exist as contacts in your CRM — which is why the CRM goes first.
  4. Connect Altruist if that is where you custody. Review the Data Access Consent screen and click Agree & Connect. Access is read-only: Hazel can never trade or move money.
  5. Check each row shows a green dot.
Before you connect the CRM

Hazel only imports records that pass its filters. In Wealthbox that means active people and households marked as client or prospect; other systems have their own rules. If a family is filed oddly in your CRM, it will simply not appear in Hazel. Ten minutes tidying the records of your top twenty households now saves a lot of confusion later.

If you do not custody at Altruist

Everything else still works — meetings, notes, profiles, planning, chat over your CRM and email. You lose live balances, holdings, transactions and beneficiary lookups. Altruist is currently the only custodian Hazel connects to; Schwab and Fidelity are listed as coming, with no date.

Done when

Your households appear under Contacts, and you can open one and see its details.

Step 4 6 minutes

Set the four defaults that touch compliance.

These decide what gets recorded, what gets kept, who can see it, and what appears at the bottom of anything a client reads. Set them once, deliberately, before the first real meeting.

Do this

  1. Go to Settings → Meeting Settings.
  2. Confirm Auto-schedule Notetakers is on, and set Bot Join Minutes Ahead to 5 so the notetaker is waiting before you are.
  3. Leave Bot Name as Hazel Notetaker. Renaming it to something less obvious undercuts the fact that clients can see a recorder in the room — do not do it.
  4. Review Save Videos and Save Transcripts against what compliance told you. Both are on by default and changes only apply to future meetings.
  5. Check the default meeting visibility. It ships as Contact access — anyone who can see the client can see the meeting.
  6. Find the client disclaimer setting and write one. It is empty until somebody fills it in, and an empty one means client-facing documents go out with no footer at all.
Phone calls record silently

If you turn on phone recording, understand what it does: the announcement plays only to the phone that dialled in. A client merged into the call afterwards hears nothing. Nobody is told the call is being recorded unless you say so out loud. If you use this at all, make the disclosure a spoken habit at the start of every call.

Done when

All six are set, and you can state from memory what gets recorded, what gets stored, and who can see it.
