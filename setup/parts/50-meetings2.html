Step 11 8 minutes

Build the Investment Plan meeting type

Meeting two writes itself: the plan read back in the client's own words, every objection captured, and a commitment asked for without a single form being signed.

Do this

  1. Go to Settings → Meeting Settings.
  2. Click Add Meeting Type, name it Investment Plan, click Save.
  3. Find it in the list, click Edit, and replace all four prompts with the blocks below.
  4. Under Match on Key Phrases, add: investment plan, plan presentation.
  5. Click Save and Regenerate.

Agenda

Paste into “Agenda”
You are preparing a financial advisor for the second of five meetings with a family with substantial wealth. The first meeting was a Discovery interview covering seven areas: Values, Goals, Relationships, Assets, Advisors, Process and Interests. At this meeting the advisor presents a written investment plan built from that interview, asks whether it is the right plan, and asks for a commitment to proceed. No money moves and nothing is signed at this meeting.

Prepare a one-page agenda for 60 to 75 minutes, in this order:
1. Read back the seven areas of the client profile in the client's own words, and invite corrections
2. Present the plan section by section: goals in writing, the diagnostic of where they are now, the recommended structure, how it maps to each goal, and what it costs
3. Note that the investment policy statement exists, where it sits, and that it is reviewed annually — do not walk through it
4. Ask for a commitment to move forward, and say plainly that nothing is executed today
5. Ask directly what worries them about the plan, the fees or the process
6. Explain that investments are the foundation only, and that tax, transfer, protection and charitable questions come next through the expert network
7. Agree next steps and schedule the commitment meeting one week out

Key Instructions:
1. Draw on the Discovery meeting notes, CRM, email history and any connected custodial data. Quote the client's own language from the Discovery meeting wherever it exists — the read-back is the most important part of this meeting.
2. List every goal already on record, and mark anything that looks unconfirmed or contradictory so the advisor can correct it in the room.
3. List every concern, question or objection the client raised at the Discovery meeting that has not yet been answered.
4. State clearly what this plan does not cover, so the advisor can say it before the client asks.
5. Include no market commentary, product names or performance projections in the agenda itself.
6. Short headings and bullets. No paragraphs. One page.

Reminder email

Paste into “Reminder Email”
Write a short reminder email to a family before the second meeting, sent by the advisor.

Key Instructions:
1. Between 50 and 100 words. Warm, direct, unhurried. Professional but human.
2. Say what the meeting is for in one sentence: the advisor will walk them through a written plan built from what they said at the first meeting, and find out whether it is right.
3. Say that they will take the plan home afterwards, and that nothing is signed or moved at this meeting.
4. Ask them to bring any statements or documents they said they would send, if they have not already.
5. Say the meeting will take about an hour.
6. Mention no products, performance, markets or fees.
7. Plain paragraphs only. No bold, no italics, no bullet points.

Meeting notes

Paste into “Meeting Notes”
You are recording the second of five meetings in an advisory process for a family with substantial wealth. A written investment plan was presented. Capture how the client responded to it.

Key Instructions:
1. Use these headings in this order: Corrections to the profile, Response to the plan, Questions and objections, Fees, Commitment.
2. Corrections to the profile: anything the client changed, added or denied when their own words were read back to them. This is the most valuable content in the meeting — record it precisely, including anything they said was wrong.
3. Response to the plan: go section by section through what was presented and record what the client said about each. Note enthusiasm and hesitation with equal care.
4. Questions and objections: every question asked and every concern raised, in the client's own words. Mark which were answered in the room and which were promised a written answer.
5. Fees: anything said about cost, in the client's words and the advisor's.
6. Commitment: whether the client agreed to move forward, in what terms, and anything they said they need before deciding.
7. Add an html table of any numeric financial data discussed.
8. If a heading was not discussed, write "Not discussed" under it. Never invent or infer content.

---

Now add a section headed "Open items and next steps".

Key Instructions:
1. A markdown table with columns Task, Owner, Due date. Include every commitment either party made.
2. List separately, under the heading "Needs a written answer", any concern the advisor promised to answer in writing, with the concern stated in the client's own words.
3. Record the date agreed for the commitment meeting, if one was agreed.
4. Keep this section under 150 words.

Follow-up email

Paste into “Follow-Up Email”
You are an expert financial communication assistant. Write a follow-up email to a family after the second meeting, sent by the advisor.

Key Instructions:
1. Between 150 and 250 words. Professional but warm, unhurried, and entirely free of sales language.
2. Open by thanking them for their time and naming one specific correction or question they raised — this shows the meeting changed something.
3. Confirm in one short paragraph what was agreed, and repeat plainly that they should take the plan home and think it over before anything is signed.
4. Answer, or say when the advisor will answer, every concern they raised. If an answer is promised, give a date.
5. State the date and time of the commitment meeting, and list what will be signed there so nothing is a surprise.
6. Discuss no products, performance, markets or projections.
7. Use no bold and no italics anywhere except the line "At our next meeting we will sign:".
8. Sign off simply, with no marketing footer.
Why nothing is signed today

A family that signs in the room has agreed to a meeting, not to a plan. Sending them home with the document and meeting again a week later is what turns a decision into a considered one — and it is the single most common step advisors skip.

Done when

Investment Plan appears in your meeting type list, and the four prompts are the ones above rather than the generic defaults.

Step 12 8 minutes

Build the Mutual Commitment meeting type

The meeting where the engagement is actually defined — scope, fees, conflicts — captured in writing every time, including the ask most advisors forget to make.

Do this

  1. Same as before: Add Meeting Type → name it Mutual Commitment → Save.
  2. Edit it and replace all four prompts with the blocks below.
  3. Key phrases: commitment, onboarding, paperwork. Then Save and Regenerate.

Agenda

Paste into “Agenda”
You are preparing a financial advisor for the third of five meetings with a family with substantial wealth. One week ago a written investment plan was presented and the family took it home. At this meeting they become a client: documents are executed, the engagement is scoped in writing, fees and conflicts are stated plainly, and the advisor asks for introductions for the first time.

Prepare a one-page agenda for 45 to 60 minutes, in this order:
1. Collect every question that came up during the week, listed before any is answered
2. Confirm the family is ready to proceed
3. Walk the engagement letter: who the client is, what is in and out of scope, how the advisor is paid, what may be shared and with whom
4. State the fee in dollars and as a rate, in the context of what it buys, and note that this conversation repeats annually
5. Disclose every conflict on the register and how each is managed, and obtain acknowledgement
6. Execute the documents, explaining each before it is signed
7. Congratulate the client, in the words they used at the first meeting about what matters to them
8. Coach for the first weeks: second thoughts are normal, financial media is entertainment, gains arrive unevenly
9. Ask for introductions
10. Schedule the follow-up meeting about six weeks out and ask them to keep every piece of paperwork

Key Instructions:
1. Draw on the previous two meetings, CRM, email history and any connected custodial data.
2. List every concern raised at the previous meeting and whether it was answered, so nothing carries into signing unresolved.
3. List every account, entity and trust that will be opened or transferred, so the advisor can check the package against it.
4. Quote what the client said at the first meeting about what matters to them, for use at the congratulation.
5. Include no market commentary or performance projections.
6. Short headings and bullets. No paragraphs. One page.

Reminder email

Paste into “Reminder Email”
Write a short reminder email to a family before the commitment meeting, sent by the advisor.

Key Instructions:
1. Between 50 and 100 words. Warm, direct, unhurried. Professional but human.
2. Say what the meeting is for in one sentence: to answer anything that came up during the week, and to put the plan in motion together.
3. Say that documents will be signed at this meeting, and list in plain words what they are, so nothing is a surprise.
4. Ask them to bring photo identification and any account details the advisor has requested.
5. Invite them to bring every question they have thought of since the last meeting.
6. Say the meeting will take about an hour.
7. Mention no products, performance, markets or projections.
8. Plain paragraphs only. No bold, no italics, no bullet points.

Meeting notes

Paste into “Meeting Notes”
You are recording the third of five meetings in an advisory process for a family with substantial wealth. At this meeting the family became a client. Capture what was agreed and what was executed.

Key Instructions:
1. Use these headings in this order: Questions raised, Scope agreed, Fees stated, Conflicts disclosed, Documents executed, Coaching given, Introductions.
2. Questions raised: everything the client asked before signing, and how each was answered.
3. Scope agreed: who the named client is, what the engagement covers, what it explicitly does not cover, and what may be shared with whom. Record this in the terms actually used in the room.
4. Fees stated: record the fee as it was said aloud, in both dollars and as a rate, and anything the client said in response. Note that the advisor said this conversation repeats annually, if that was said.
5. Conflicts disclosed: list every conflict, affiliate relationship or fee-sharing arrangement read out, and record whether the client acknowledged each.
6. Documents executed: list every document signed and every account, entity or trust it relates to.
7. Coaching given: note whether the advisor covered second thoughts, the financial media, and the uneven arrival of gains.
8. Introductions: record the ask, every name offered, and anything the client said about how to approach them. If no names were offered, say so.
9. Add an html table of any numeric financial data discussed.
10. If a heading was not discussed, write "Not discussed" under it. Never invent or infer content.

---

Now add a section headed "Compliance record".

Key Instructions:
1. Record whether the client was told the conversation was being recorded, and whether consent was given, refused or not addressed. Quote what was said.
2. Record any statement made about fees, conflicts, affiliate relationships, or what the advisor will and will not do — in the words used, not paraphrased.
3. Record any promise made about future service, returns or outcomes. If none was made, write "None made".
4. Do not interpret, assess or advise on any of this. Record only what was said.

Now add a section headed "Next steps".

Key Instructions:
1. A markdown table with columns Task, Owner, Due date, covering every commitment either party made.
2. Record the date agreed for the follow-up meeting.
3. Note anything the client was asked to keep or send.
4. Keep this section under 120 words.

Follow-up email

Paste into “Follow-Up Email”
You are an expert financial communication assistant. Write a follow-up email to a family who have just become clients, sent by the advisor.

Key Instructions:
1. Between 150 and 250 words. Warm, calm and free of celebration language. This is a considered step, not a win.
2. Open by acknowledging the decision they have made, referring to what they said matters to them rather than to the firm.
3. Confirm in one short paragraph what was signed and what happens next, with dates: when transfers begin, what they will receive from custodians, and what they should do with it.
4. Repeat briefly that some second thoughts are normal, and that the plan works over years rather than weeks.
5. State the date of the follow-up meeting and ask them to keep every piece of paperwork that arrives between now and then.
6. If they offered an introduction, thank them for it in one sentence without pressing.
7. Discuss no products, performance, markets or projections.
8. Use no bold and no italics anywhere except the line "Between now and then:".
9. Sign off simply, with no marketing footer.
Check this one against your own rule

Your written procedure says not to record the commitment meeting — the associate takes decision notes instead. If you keep that rule, this meeting type still earns its place for the agenda, the reminder and the follow-up email; paste your own notes into Hazel afterwards to generate the notes section. If you do record it, the compliance block above captures what was said, which is exactly what you want on file for a meeting where fees and conflicts are stated aloud.

Done when

Mutual Commitment is in the list, and you have decided — and written down — whether you record this meeting.

Step 13 8 minutes

Build the 45-Day Follow-up meeting type

The meeting that turns a stack of confirmations into a record the client understands — and defuses the first performance number before it teaches them the wrong lesson.

Do this

  1. Same as before: Add Meeting Type → name it 45-Day Follow-up → Save.
  2. Edit it and replace all four prompts with the blocks below.
  3. Key phrases: 45-day, forty-five day, follow-up meeting. Then Save and Regenerate.

Agenda

Paste into “Agenda”
You are preparing a financial advisor for the fourth of five meetings with a family with substantial wealth. About six weeks ago they became a client and transfers began, generating confirmations, statements and notices. At this meeting the advisor organises all of it into a record the family can use for years, teaches them how to read it, puts the first weeks of performance in a lifetime perspective, and asks for introductions a second time.

Prepare a one-page agenda for 45 to 60 minutes, in this order:
1. Gather every question the paperwork has raised, listed before any is answered
2. Explain the document set: what each section is for and how it grows over the years
3. Organise the documents together, explaining how to read a statement, a confirmation and a tax notice
4. Review the plain-language summary of any new trust, entity or beneficiary designation, and who the family will tell about it
5. Put the first weeks of performance in perspective before any annualised figure is read
6. Ask for introductions
7. Confirm the contact rhythm they chose at the first meeting and schedule the first progress meeting

Key Instructions:
1. Draw on the previous three meetings, CRM, email history and any connected custodial data.
2. List every account, entity and trust opened or transferred since the commitment meeting, and flag any transfer still outstanding.
3. List every new structure created, so the advisor can check a plain-language summary exists for each.
4. State the contact rhythm and channel the client asked for at the first meeting, quoting them, so the advisor can confirm it still holds.
5. Do not include a performance figure in the agenda. The advisor introduces the context before the number.
6. Short headings and bullets. No paragraphs. One page.

Reminder email

Paste into “Reminder Email”
Write a short reminder email to a client before the follow-up meeting, sent by the advisor.

Key Instructions:
1. Between 50 and 100 words. Warm, direct, unhurried. Professional but human.
2. Say what the meeting is for in one sentence: to get every document from the last few weeks organised and explained, so they always know where things are.
3. Ask them to bring or send everything that has arrived since they became a client, even the pieces they think are junk mail.
4. Say the meeting will take about an hour.
5. Reassure them that nothing needs sorting beforehand — bringing the pile as it is, is exactly right.
6. Mention no products, performance, markets or projections.
7. Plain paragraphs only. No bold, no italics, no bullet points.

Meeting notes

Paste into “Meeting Notes”
You are recording the fourth of five meetings in an advisory process for a family with substantial wealth. The purpose was to organise and explain the paperwork produced by implementation. Capture what was organised and what the client now understands.

Key Instructions:
1. Use these headings in this order: Questions raised, What was organised, What was explained, Structures reviewed, Perspective on performance, Introductions, Contact rhythm.
2. Questions raised: everything the client asked about documents, statements or notices, and how each was answered.
3. What was organised: which documents were filed, into which sections, and anything still missing or outstanding.
4. What was explained: which document types the advisor taught the client to read, and anything the client said they still find confusing. Record confusion plainly — it sets the next meeting's agenda.
5. Structures reviewed: each new trust, entity or beneficiary designation discussed, and what the client decided about who to tell and when.
6. Perspective on performance: record that the long-term framing was given, and anything the client said about the numbers. Do not restate performance figures as findings.
7. Introductions: record the ask, every name offered, and anything said about how to approach them. If no names were offered, say so.
8. Contact rhythm: the cadence and channel the client confirmed, quoted precisely.
9. Add an html table of any numeric financial data discussed.
10. If a heading was not discussed, write "Not discussed" under it. Never invent or infer content.

---

Now add a section headed "Next steps".

Key Instructions:
1. A markdown table with columns Task, Owner, Due date, covering every commitment either party made.
2. Record the date agreed for the first progress meeting.
3. List anything the client is still to send, and anything the advisor owes them.
4. Keep this section under 120 words.

Follow-up email

Paste into “Follow-Up Email”
You are an expert financial communication assistant. Write a follow-up email to a client after the follow-up meeting, sent by the advisor.

Key Instructions:
1. Between 150 and 250 words. Professional but warm, plain, and free of sales language.
2. Open by confirming that everything is now filed and where it lives, naming the sections so they can find things without asking.
3. In one short paragraph, restate what to do with documents that arrive in future: send them in, and keep nothing they cannot find again.
4. Repeat in one sentence that short periods say nothing about a lifetime plan. Do not restate any performance figure.
5. Confirm the contact rhythm they chose and the date of the first progress meeting, and say that meeting will cover tax, transfer, protection and charitable planning.
6. If they offered an introduction, thank them in one sentence without pressing.
7. Discuss no products, performance, markets or projections.
8. Use no bold and no italics anywhere except the line "Still to come:".
9. Sign off simply, with no marketing footer.
Deliver the reframe either way

A good six-week number teaches the wrong lesson just as efficiently as a bad one. The instruction above tells Hazel to record that the framing was given and to keep the figure out of the summary — so the client's written record is about the plan, not about a fortnight of noise.

Done when

All five client meeting types exist: Discovery, Investment Plan, Mutual Commitment, 45-Day Follow-up and Progress Meeting.
