# 25 — Annual client satisfaction survey

Once a year the practice asks every client, formally, how it is doing — and only after it has first checked itself against its own standards and fixed what it found. The survey gives clients permission to say what they would not say in a meeting, tells them we want to hear it, and produces an action list that we report back to them. The instrument is ours, built from the seven factors that actually separate satisfied clients from dissatisfied ones.

## Purpose

- Hear the complaints clients would otherwise keep, before they act on them.
- Measure the practice on the factors that drive loyalty — reliability, no surprises, timely information, warmth, listening, understanding needs, providing alternatives — not on the competence clients already assume.
- Close the loop: team session, action items, and a specific "what will change" message to clients.
- Feed the lost-client interviews and the mystery-shopper results into the same annual review.

## Trigger

- Calendar: Q2 (T15) — the WOW self-audit in May, the survey in May–June, results and the client message in July.
- A new client's first survey is sent at the first Q2 after CCM4, never earlier than six months into the relationship.

## Roles

Owner: LA · Does: CSA (self-audit logistics; survey distribution and collection; response tracking), AA (tabulation and distillation) · Consulted: whole team for the results session; FCT contact in Mode B for the coordination check.

## Timing

| When | Step |
|---|---|
| Early May | WOW self-audit (T12 a) completed by the LA with the team |
| Mid May | Gaps assigned and closed or scheduled before the survey goes out |
| Late May | Survey (T12 b) sent to every client household per the contact plan |
| June | Responses collected; one reminder at two weeks; LA personally calls non-responders among top relationships |
| Early July | Tabulation; team results session; action list |
| Mid July | "What will change" letter to every client; individual follow-up calls where a response warrants it |
| Ongoing | Action items tracked at the monthly team meeting; reviewed at next year's self-audit |

## Inputs

- The WOW self-audit results (T12 a) and the gap list.
- The service-failure log for the year (T12 c) and any lost-client interviews (T12 d).
- The mystery-shopper results (SOP 23).
- The survey instrument (T12 b), personalized only with the client's name and the team members they work with.
- The contact plan and T05: who in each household receives the survey; the Mode B coordination check recipient.

## Prep checklist

- [ ] Self-audit completed and gaps closed or scheduled — the survey is not sent while known gaps sit open.
- [ ] Survey list reconciled: every client household; both spouses invited to answer separately where the contact plan names both.
- [ ] Delivery channel per contact plan (secure link via [Document vault]; paper for clients who prefer it).
- [ ] Anonymity decision recorded: responses are attributed unless the client asks otherwise; the cover note says so and offers an anonymous option.
- [ ] [Firm Compliance] has seen the instrument and the cover note.

## Procedure

1. **Audit first.** The LA and team score the practice against the self-audit (T12 a): met / partly / not for every item. Assign every "not" and "partly" an owner and a date. Close what can be closed in two weeks; schedule the rest and tell the team.
2. **Send the survey** with a short personal note from the LA: why we ask, that we read every answer, that criticism is what we most want, and how long it takes (ten minutes).

    !!! script "Say something like"
        "Once a year I ask every family we serve to tell us plainly what we should do better. Your candid answers shape how we work next year."

3. **Collect.** CSA tracks responses; one reminder after two weeks. For the top relationships and any client with a recent service failure, the LA calls personally to ask for the response or to take it by phone.
4. **Tabulate and distill.** AA scores by factor, lists every open-ended comment verbatim (with the client's name unless anonymous), and flags any score of 2 or below on any item for individual follow-up.
5. **Follow up individually first.** Before any team session, the LA calls every client whose responses flag a problem: thank them, ask what happened, and say what we will do. This is a service-recovery moment (SOP 23), not a data point.
6. **Hold the team results session.** The whole team, including borrowed staff who touch clients regularly: results by factor, themes from comments, the failure log and lost-client findings for the year, the mystery-shopper results. Everyone contributes to the action list; the people who do the work usually know the fix.
7. **Write the action list.** Each item: the change, the SOP it amends (SOP 60), the owner, the date. Distinguish what changes for everyone from what changes for one client.
8. **Tell clients what will change.** A letter to every client within two weeks of the session: what we heard, in their words where possible; the specific changes we are making; thanks. Clients who asked for something particular hear it personally from the LA.
9. **Track** the action list at the monthly team meeting (SOP 74) until every item is closed; carry the results into next year's self-audit as the baseline.
10. **Run the lost-client interviews** for anyone who left during the year, if not already done (SOP 23), and include the findings in the results session.

## Follow-up

- Within two weeks of the results session: the "what will change" letter is out; individual calls made.
- Monthly: action items reviewed; closed items reported to the team.
- Next May: last year's actions verified in the self-audit before the new survey.

## Do / Don't

**Do**

- Fix known gaps before asking clients to point them out.
- Read every comment aloud in the team session, including the uncomfortable ones.
- Tell clients specifically what changed; generic thanks devalue their effort.
- Treat a low score as a call to make today, not a statistic for July.

**Don't**

- Don't survey a client mid-crisis or within weeks of a service failure that is still open; call them instead.
- Don't survey a new client before six months.
- Don't let the action list die after the letter goes out.
- Don't ask questions about investment performance as if it were the point; ask about the relationship.

## Mode A / Mode B

### Mode A — whole family

- Each household receives its own survey; both spouses may answer separately. Family-level questions (family meetings, communication across the family, the family-level plan) are added for families with an active family layer.
- Results are shared with the family contact or council chair only in aggregate and only under the T14 consent; individual households' answers stay with those households.

### Mode B — individual within a larger team

- Survey the individual only; questions about family-level matters are omitted unless within the mandate.
- Optional coordination check (T12 e): three questions to the FCT's designated contact about how the working relationship is functioning — responsiveness, information flow, surprises — sent with the client's knowledge.
- Findings that concern the FCT's own service are raised through the SOP 33 channel, never in the client's letter.

## Metrics

- Response rate by household (target ≥ 70%; 100% among top relationships, by phone if necessary).
- Share of action items closed within 90 days (target ≥ 80%).
- Year-on-year change in the average score on the three lowest factors (target: improving).
