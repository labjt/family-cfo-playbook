# 23 — Service failure recovery

Failures will happen: a transfer stalls, a report is wrong, a promise is missed, a call is not returned. Clients forgive the failure; they do not forgive learning about it late, being blamed for it, or seeing it happen again. This procedure fixes the failure fast, tells the client first, repairs the process, and learns from the clients who leave.

## Purpose

- Resolve every service failure the same day where possible and never later than the time promised.
- Make sure the client hears the problem and the fix from us, together, before they discover the problem themselves.
- Convert each failure into a change in a procedure so it does not recur.
- Learn systematically from lost clients and from an occasional outside test of our own service.

## Trigger

- Any team member notices or is told of an error, a missed deadline, an unreturned message, a wrong document, a mishandled request, or a client expressing dissatisfaction.
- A red flag in SOP 22 turns out to be our mistake.
- A client gives notice, moves assets away, or cancels the relationship.

## Roles

Owner: LA · Does: CSA (intake and log; fixes administrative failures; tracks to closure), SA/AA (fixes analytical or reporting failures; drafts the explanation) · Consulted: [Firm Compliance] where money, documents, or regulatory items are involved; [Custodian] and ISB where the failure sits with them; FCT contact in Mode B through the agreed channel.

## Timing

| Step | Clock |
|---|---|
| Intake and log | Within 1 hour of discovery |
| LA informed | Same hour for anything the client can see; same day otherwise |
| Client told (problem + fix + timing) | Same day, by the LA, by phone |
| Resolution | Same day where possible; otherwise by the time promised, with a progress call if it slips |
| Root-cause note and SOP change | Within 5 business days |
| Lost-client interview | Within 2 weeks of notice |

## Inputs

- The service-failure log (T12, part c).
- The contact plan and personality note (SOP 21, SOP 04) — how this client wants to be reached and how they take bad news.
- The relevant SOP and tracker (T07 for transfers, T10 for planning items).
- T05 and the FCT protocol in Mode B.

## Prep checklist

- [ ] Every team member knows where the log is and that logging is never punished; hiding a failure is.
- [ ] The apology-call rule is understood: the LA makes it personally, whoever caused the failure.
- [ ] Standing arrangement with [Custodian] and ISB partners for escalation contacts and turnaround times.
- [ ] The mystery-shopper check scheduled once a year (a trusted outsider tests a call, an email, and a document request).

## Procedure

1. **Log it.** The person who discovers the failure records it in the log within the hour: what happened, who is affected, when it was promised, what the client knows. No judgment yet.
2. **Contain.** Stop any further harm: pause a transfer, correct a document, hold a report. If money or documents are involved, tell [Firm Compliance] now.
3. **Diagnose fast.** Establish what actually happened and what it will take to fix, in enough detail to give the client a date. Do this before the call; do not call with "we are looking into it" unless the client will otherwise find out first.
4. **Call the client — the LA, personally, the same day.** Problem, cause in one sentence, the fix, the date, and what we are changing so it does not recur. Apologize once, clearly, without excuses.

    !!! script "Say something like"
        "I am calling because we made a mistake on your account and I want you to hear it from me, together with what we have already done to fix it and when it will be complete."

5. **Never blame.** Do not attribute the failure to the custodian, a sister company, the client's other advisors, or the client, in front of the client. The practice owns the client experience whoever caused the problem; escalation and cost recovery happen behind the scenes through the agreed channel.
6. **Fix it** by the date promised. If the date slips, the client hears before the date, not after. Track to closure in the log and the relevant tracker.
7. **Confirm closure in writing** the day the fix lands, and check at the next contact that the client considers it closed.
8. **Root cause.** Within five business days, the LA and the person closest to the failure write a two-line root-cause note: the step in the SOP that failed or was missing, and the change. Amend the SOP (SOP 60). If the same failure recurs, treat it as a system failure, not a person's.
9. **Turn the protector's question on ourselves.** Before assigning cause elsewhere, ask what we did or did not do that contributed. Most "custodian problems" have a missing check on our side.
10. **Review the log monthly** at the team meeting (SOP 74): patterns, open items, changes made. Feed themes into the semi-annual WOW self-audit (T12) and the survey action list (SOP 25).
11. **Lost-client interview.** When a client gives notice or moves assets away, the LA asks for a thirty-minute conversation within two weeks, using the interview guide in T12: what changed, what we missed, what they would tell a friend about us, and whether anything could bring them back. Thank them for the time regardless of the answer. Record the findings; act on them.
12. **Mystery-shopper check.** Once a year, a trusted outsider tests the practice — a call to the main line, an email request, a document request — against the response-time standard. Results go into the self-audit.

## Follow-up

- Same day: log entry, client called, fix under way.
- Within 5 business days: root-cause note; SOP amended; team told what changed.
- Monthly: log reviewed; open items closed or re-dated with the client informed.
- Annually: lost-client findings and mystery-shopper results reviewed with the satisfaction survey.

## Do / Don't

**Do**

- Tell the client before they find out; the surprise hurts more than the error.
- Give a date and keep it; if you cannot, call before the date.
- Fix the process, then the person's understanding of it — in that order.
- Ask a departing client for the interview even when the reason is obvious.

**Don't**

- Don't send the apology by email; the LA calls.
- Don't explain the failure by naming who else was involved.
- Don't let a failure sit unlogged because it was small; small failures are the pattern.
- Don't overreact to a single failure with a new rule that slows every other client; respond, don't react.

## Mode A / Mode B

### Mode A — whole family

- If a failure touches more than one household, each affected household lead is called separately; the family contact is told only what the consent matrix allows.
- A failure in a trust or entity account is reported to the trustee and the beneficiary as the trust's communication protocol requires (SOP 46).

### Mode B — individual within a larger team

- The Advisor owns recovery for his client even when the failure sits with the FCT, the family's custodian, or a sister company; the client hears one accountable voice.
- Escalation goes through the agreed channel in SOP 33; the FCT contact is told what happened and what was done, never that they caused it, in front of the client.
- If the failure reveals a gap in the mandate (something nobody owned), the LA raises it with the FCT and re-scopes T05.

## Metrics

- Failures resolved same day or by the promised date (target ≥ 95%).
- Repeat failures of the same root cause within 12 months (target zero).
- Lost-client interviews completed within 2 weeks of notice (target 100%).
