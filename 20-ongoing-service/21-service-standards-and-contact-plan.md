# 21 — Service standards and the contact plan

Every client told us in Discovery how they want to be worked with. This procedure turns that answer into a written contact plan, sets the response-time and communication standards the whole team is held to, and defines what "service" means for each scope of engagement so that staffing, pricing and the client's expectations all describe the same thing.

## Purpose

- Honor each client's stated process preference for the life of the relationship, in writing, so it survives staff changes.
- Fix the practice's service standards — response times, agendas, ownership of requests, confidentiality posture — as rules, not habits.
- Define service by scope (what a B-narrow mandate receives vs. a Mode A family) so the team can plan capacity and the client knows what to expect.
- Make the practice's contact rhythm deliberately about the client's life, not only the portfolio.

## Trigger

- A new client signs at CCM3 (the contact plan is drafted from the Discovery notes and confirmed at CCM4).
- An existing client's Rediscovery meeting updates their process preference.
- Annually, at the first progress meeting of the year (T15), and whenever a life event (SOP 22) or a service failure (SOP 23) reveals the plan is wrong.
- A new team member joins and must be written into "who you call for what."

## Roles

Owner: LA · Does: CSA (drafts and maintains the contact plan in [CRM]; sends the written version with the CCM4 confirmation; monitors the touch log and response-time standard), SA/AA (execute assigned touches and deliverables) · Consulted: FCT contact in Mode B for the communication protocol.

## Inputs

- Discovery notes on process preference: preferred channel, frequency, meeting cadence, who in the household is the primary contact, what they never want (SOP 10, T03).
- T05 — mode, scope rung, who may instruct, consent for who may receive what.
- The personality note (SOP 04) — how the client likes to be paced and addressed.
- Family map and communication map for Mode A families (T14).
- The annual service calendar (T15) and the segment/scope service definition below.

## Prep checklist

- [ ] Process-preference notes transcribed from the Discovery recording into [CRM] fields (channel, frequency, cadence, primary contact, "never").
- [ ] T05 consent matrix checked so the plan does not promise to send anything to anyone not authorized.
- [ ] Household members who may speak directly with the Advisor listed (default: both spouses; adult children only with consent).
- [ ] "Who you call for what" table filled with the current team, including borrowed specialists by name for active matters.
- [ ] Scope-based service definition selected (table in step 5) and any customization priced or declined.

## Procedure

1. **Write the contact plan.** One page per client in [CRM] and in the notebook: preferred channel; how often they want to hear from us and about what; meeting cadence and format (in person / video); primary and secondary contacts in the household; who on our team they call for what; what they have asked us never to do (for example, never call at the office, never send unsolicited market commentary).
2. **Confirm it with the client at CCM4** and read it back. Change anything they want changed. The plan is theirs, not ours.

    !!! script "Say something like"
        "You told me in our first meeting how you like to work with an advisor; here it is in writing so that everyone on our team follows it. Tell me what to change."

3. **Set the rhythm of contact.** Plan the year's touches from the client's preference and the calendar (T15). Wealth managers who keep their best clients loyal reach them roughly fifteen times a year, most of those touches being about the client's life, family and interests rather than the portfolio. Treat that as a design target for the plan, never as a quota: a client who asked for quarterly contact gets quarterly contact, done well.
4. **Apply the response-time standard.** Every message from a client is acknowledged the same business day by the person who received it, with a time by which the answer will come. The answer arrives by that time or the client hears before the deadline that it will be late and why. Whoever received the request owns it to completion — nobody in this practice says "let me put you through to someone else."
5. **Apply the service definition for the client's scope.** Service is a product, not a word. The default definition by scope:

    | Scope | Meetings per year | Planning depth | Reporting | Held-away assets | Family layer |
    |---|---|---|---|---|---|
    | Mode A — whole family | Quarterly household RPMs (client may choose semi-annual); annual family meeting | Full wealth management plan across all four areas; family-level plan | Consolidated across entities and trusts; custom family statement | Reviewed every RPM; consolidation offered | SOPs 40–48 in full |
    | Mode B — B-full | Quarterly or semi-annual per preference | Full plan for the individual's own assets; family-structure items referred to the FCT | Individual consolidated; compatible with family reporting | Personal held-away only | On invitation |
    | Mode B — B-partial | Semi-annual | Investment plan plus liaison into the FCT's planning | Individual accounts; data feed to the FCT if mandated | Not solicited | On invitation |
    | Mode B — B-narrow | Annual, plus trustee meetings as mandated | Investment plan for the mandate only | Mandate accounts only | Not solicited | None |

    Anything the client wants beyond the definition — custom reporting formats, manually tracked outside assets, exception custodians, one-off analyses — is either priced into the fee (SOP 14) or declined, never absorbed silently.
6. **Put an agenda in front of every meeting** (SOP 05, T02), including calls the client thinks of as quick. A written agenda is the visible sign the practice runs on process.
7. **Write "who you call for what."** A short table in the contact plan and in every confirmation letter (T01): the LA for advice and decisions; the SA for the plan and routine reviews; the CSA for paperwork, money movement and scheduling; a named specialist for an active matter. Update it within one business day of any team change.
8. **Make sure both spouses can reach us.** Each partner in a couple may speak directly with the Advisor and should be able to; the plan names both as contacts unless the client instructs otherwise in writing. This protects the household if one partner is suddenly unable to act and it gives each partner a first-hand relationship rather than a relayed one.
9. **Hold the confidentiality posture.** Very wealthy families attract attention; the practice's job includes not adding to it. Never acknowledge a client relationship in public or to another client. Never confirm to a third party that the family is a client without written consent on T05. Do not discuss one family in front of another, even anonymized, where the details would identify them. Discretion is a value the team is hired for.
10. **Design the experience around the seven satisfaction factors.** Clients assume competence; it does not differentiate. What does: reliability (promises kept on time); no surprises (problems and their fixes communicated first, and complaints actively invited); first-to-know (clients hear about their affairs, the markets, and the firm from us before anyone else); warmth (genuine, not merely polite); listening (patience while explaining, attention while hearing); client-centered work (their specific needs, with real alternatives). Every standard in this SOP maps to one of these.
11. **Audit twice a year against the WOW self-audit (T12)** and fix the gaps before the satisfaction survey goes out (SOP 25).
12. **Record everything in [CRM].** The contact plan, every touch, every request and its resolution time, every change of preference. The red-flag methodology in SOP 22 runs off these records.

## Follow-up

- Within 24 hours of CCM4: the written contact plan is in the notebook and in [CRM]; "who you call for what" is in the confirmation letter.
- Monthly: the CSA reports touch-plan adherence and response-time misses to the LA; misses go to the service-failure log (T12).
- Annually: the plan is reviewed with the client; changes are re-issued in writing within a week.

## Do / Don't

**Do**

- Read the contact plan before every call with a client you do not speak with weekly.
- Reach clients about their lives — a note after a child's graduation, an article on something they collect — more often than about their money.
- Tell a client before the meeting when something material changed; the meeting is for decisions, not surprises.
- Keep the same standards for a B-narrow trust mandate as for a Mode A family; the definition changes, the quality does not.

**Don't**

- Don't let the touch target become a quota that produces hollow contact; a client who asked for quiet gets quiet.
- Don't send anything to anyone the consent matrix on T05 does not authorize.
- Don't absorb custom work silently; price it or decline it.
- Don't use possessive language about clients in front of them or among the team.

## Mode A / Mode B

### Mode A — whole family

- The contact plan is written per household and rolled into a family communication map (T14): who is the primary contact per household, who receives family-level communications, and who may not be copied on what.
- The annual family meeting (SOP 43) and family-level reporting are part of the service definition; the plan states which members attend which meetings.
- Where a family council exists, the council's chair or the family's designated contact is named for family-level matters; individual households keep their own contacts.

### Mode B — individual within a larger team

- Response-time commitments extend to the FCT's designated contact for the matters within the mandate; the standard is the same as for the client.
- A written communication protocol (SOP 33) sits beside the individual's preference: who contacts whom, what is copied to the FCT under the T05 consent, single-thread communication for anything touching family structures.
- "Who you call for what" includes the FCT's contact for family-level matters so the client is never bounced between teams.
- Service definition follows the scope rung; nothing outside the mandate is offered without re-scoping T05.

## Metrics

- Response-time adherence: share of client requests acknowledged same business day and resolved within the promised time (target ≥ 95%).
- Contact-plan adherence: share of planned touches completed per client per quarter (target ≥ 90%).
- Preference changes recorded within one business day (target 100%).
