# 22 — Event-driven contact

The calendar covers the year we can plan. This procedure covers everything we cannot: the signals in the data that something has changed, the market and the world doing what they do, changes inside the firm, and the events in a family's life that decide whether the relationship deepens or ends. Each trigger has an owner, a clock, a message, and a record.

## Purpose

- Make sure the client hears about anything that touches their affairs from us first, fast, and in a consistent voice.
- Turn red flags in the data into contact before they become problems.
- Route life events into the right planning work — the prenuptial timeline, a family meeting, a preamble revision — instead of leaving them to the next scheduled meeting.
- Give the team a checklist for the hardest moment, the death or incapacity of a principal.

## Trigger

Any item in the trigger table below is observed by any team member, reported by the client or the family, or surfaced by the monthly red-flag review.

## Roles

Owner: LA · Does: CSA (monthly red-flag review; first acknowledgment; logging), SA/AA (analysis behind the message; tracker updates) · Consulted: [Firm Compliance] for death, incapacity, and any account restriction; FCT contact in Mode B; ISB ([Trust Co.]) where a trust is involved; EEN counsel for prenuptial, addiction, and estate items.

## Timing

| Trigger class | First contact | Follow-through |
|---|---|---|
| Red flag in the data | Same business day the flag is seen | Resolved or explained within 3 business days |
| Market shock | Same day, top relationships first; all clients within 2 business days | Follow-up note within the week; agenda item at the next RPM |
| External disaster affecting the client | Immediately; then daily until stable | Log; adjust the contact plan if needed |
| Firm or staff change | Before the change is visible to anyone outside the firm | Written note within 24 hours of the call |
| Life event reported | Same day acknowledgment by the LA personally | Planning task opened within 5 business days |
| Death or incapacity of a principal | Same day | Checklist runs over 30–90 days; closing ritual within a year |

## Inputs

- Monthly red-flag review of [CRM] and [Custodian] activity (SOP 21 records).
- The contact plan and personality note for the client (SOP 21, SOP 04).
- T05 — consent for what may be shared with whom; the FCT protocol in Mode B.
- The family map and communication map (T14) for Mode A families.
- The advanced-planning tracker (T10) and any letters of wishes or preambles on file (SOP 46).

## Prep checklist

- [ ] Red-flag rules configured in [CRM] and reviewed monthly by the CSA: large cash movement, statement anomaly, missed or cancelled meeting, change of address or marital status, death notification, beneficiary or trustee request, unusual login or contact from an unknown third party.
- [ ] Market-shock message template drafted and approved by [Firm Compliance] in advance, with a blank for the day's facts.
- [ ] Death/incapacity checklist (below) on file with the named contacts per client: executor, trustees, counsel, CPA, family contact, FCT in Mode B.
- [ ] Every team member knows the rule: the LA personally makes the first call on any life event or shock; the CSA never delivers difficult news alone.

## Procedure

1. **Detect.** The CSA runs the red-flag review monthly and watches daily activity; any team member who hears of an event tells the LA the same day.
2. **Classify** using the trigger table and open a task in [CRM] with the trigger, owner, and clock.

    | Trigger | Owner | Message | Planning task opened |
    |---|---|---|---|
    | Large or unusual cash movement | CSA → LA | Confirm the client authorized it; verify by callback on a known number, never by reply to the request | Fraud check; update T07 if a transfer |
    | Statement anomaly (missing account, unexpected position, fee error) | CSA | Explain what happened and what we did about it, before the client notices | Service-failure log if ours (SOP 23) |
    | Missed or cancelled meeting, repeated | CSA → LA | LA calls: "Is everything all right?" — not a rescheduling email | Contact-plan review |
    | Change of address, employment, or marital status seen in data | LA | Acknowledge personally; ask what it means for them | Life-event branch below |
    | Beneficiary or trustee request | LA | Acknowledge same day; route to SOP 46 | Distribution-request process (T23) |
    | Third party asking about the client | CSA → LA | Nothing confirmed; client told the same day | Consent check on T05 |

3. **Market shock.** On the day, the LA calls the top relationships personally (Mode A principals and household leads; Mode B individuals) with one consistent message: what happened, what it means for their plan, what we are doing, and when they will hear from us next. The CSA sends the approved note to every other client within two business days. Nobody on the team sends their own market commentary.

    !!! script "Say something like"
        "I wanted you to hear from me today rather than from the news: here is what happened, here is what it does and does not mean for your plan, and here is what we are doing about it."

    !!! modeB "Mode B"
        Align the message with the FCT before sending. Two teams telling the same family different stories on the same day destroys confidence in both; when they differ, the LA and the FCT contact agree the wording, or the LA sends only what is within the mandate.

4. **External disaster** (a storm, fire, civil emergency, a public event affecting the client's community or business). Contact immediately to ask whether they and their family are safe and whether we can do anything — money movement, documents, a hotel. Stay in touch daily until stable. This is the moment relationships are made or lost.
5. **Firm or staff change.** Clients hear about a departure, a merger, a new custodian, or a new team member from the LA before it is visible anywhere else. Sequence: team told first; then a call to each affected client; then a written note within 24 hours; then the change. Never let a client learn of a personnel change when they call in need or from a competitor's outreach.
6. **Life events — the planning branch.** Acknowledge personally the same day. Then open the planning task:

    | Event | What it triggers |
    |---|---|
    | Birth or adoption | Beneficiary designations and trust provisions reviewed; a note to the family; rising-generation entry on the family map (T14) |
    | Engagement | The prenuptial timeline starts now: the conversation with the couple and the parents months before the wedding, never in the final weeks; disclosure of trust interests to both partners, with the rule that disclosure is not access; the family's spouse policy applied if one exists (SOP 45); counsel selected to fit the couple |
    | Marriage | Contact plan updated (both spouses may speak directly with the Advisor); consent matrix reviewed; welcome as a new member (SOP 42) |
    | Divorce or separation | Confidentiality between the parties enforced from the first call; [Firm Compliance] consulted; conflicts assessed; the Advisor never mediates a marriage |
    | Job or career change; new business | Compensation and equity terms reviewed; plan updated; tracker items opened |
    | Sale of a business, succession, retirement of a principal | A family meeting convened (SOP 43): what the liquidity event means for the family, not just the balance sheet; expert-network case brief (T09); [ESOP Co.] consulted where an employee-ownership path is live |
    | New life stage or health change of a trustee, beneficiary, or principal | Preambles, letters of wishes, and beneficiary designations revisited (SOP 46); incapacity documents checked |
    | Signs of addiction or crisis | The LA raises it privately with the appropriate family member; the leverage-model conversation with counsel about protective trust provisions and treatment funding; never a moral judgment, always a plan (SOP 46, SOP 30) |
    | Death of a family member | Checklist below |

7. **Death or incapacity of a principal — run the checklist.**
    - [ ] Day 1: LA calls the surviving spouse or the designated family contact; condolences first, logistics only when asked. Nothing else that day.
    - [ ] Day 1–3: [Firm Compliance] and [Custodian] notified; accounts restricted as required; recurring transfers paused; authorized signers confirmed from the file, never from a phone call.
    - [ ] Week 1: executor, trustees, counsel, and CPA identified and contacted; documents located (will, trusts, powers of attorney, letters of wishes, legacy letter); date-of-death valuations ordered.
    - [ ] Week 1–2: the household's immediate cash needs covered; a single point of contact on our team named for the family.
    - [ ] Month 1–3: estate and trust administration coordinated with counsel and [Trust Co.]; investor-allocation and trust-funding items reviewed; the family map updated; the contact plan rewritten with the survivors.
    - [ ] Within the year: a family meeting that includes remembrance (SOP 43); the closing ritual for any trust that terminates (SOP 48); the deceased's story captured for the family archive.
    - [ ] Incapacity: the same sequence with the agent under the power of attorney and the successor trustee; the annual protector check-in brought forward (SOP 46).

    !!! modeB "Mode B"
        The FCT leads estate administration for the family; the Advisor supports the individuals he serves, coordinates through the single thread in SOP 33, and shares nothing beyond the T05 consent.

8. **Record.** Every trigger, message, and task is logged in [CRM] with dates; life events update the Total Client Profile (SOP 11) and the family map.

## Follow-up

- Within 24 hours: written note where a call was made; task opened with owner and date.
- Within one week: planning branch confirmed with the client; trackers updated; the next RPM agenda amended.
- At the next RPM: the event reviewed and the plan changes presented.

## Do / Don't

**Do**

- Make the first call yourself on anything that hurts; delegate the paperwork, never the empathy.
- Say what you know, what you do not know, and when they will hear from you again.
- Start the prenuptial conversation the day you hear of an engagement.
- Treat a beneficiary's request as a relationship moment, not an administrative one.

**Don't**

- Don't send market commentary that has not been approved and aligned.
- Don't verify a money-movement request by replying to the message that made it.
- Don't let a client learn of a staff change from anyone but the LA.
- Don't attempt to mediate between spouses; enforce confidentiality and bring in the right professional.

## Mode A / Mode B

### Mode A — whole family

- Family-wide events (a death, a business sale) are communicated through the communication map (T14): the LA calls household leads; the family contact or council chair coordinates family-level messages.
- Family meetings are the vehicle for transitions; convene within weeks of a sale, succession, or death, not at the next annual meeting.
- Confidentiality between members governs what each household hears about another's event.

### Mode B — individual within a larger team

- Market and shock messages aligned with the FCT; the LA sends only what is within the mandate if agreement is not reached in time.
- Life events shared with the FCT only under the T05 consent; the individual decides what the family learns and when.
- On a principal's death or incapacity, the FCT leads; the Advisor's checklist runs only for the accounts and individuals in his mandate.
- Firm changes affecting the mandate are told to the client first, then to the FCT contact under the protocol.

## Metrics

- Red flags resolved or explained within 3 business days (target ≥ 95%).
- Market-shock outreach to top relationships completed same day (target 100%).
- Life events with a planning task opened within 5 business days (target 100%).
