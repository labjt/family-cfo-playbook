# 40 — Family engagement model

When the whole family is under the Advisor's umbrella (Mode A), the unit of engagement is no longer a household but a multi-household system with entities, trusts, generations, and in-laws. This spec fixes what the practice must know and agree about that system before it runs the consultative sequence on it, and how the Advisor conducts himself inside it.

## Purpose

- Define **who the client is** when the client is a family.
- Standardize the **family map** and the four matrices (decision rights, confidentiality, communication, transitions) that every family-layer procedure reads from and writes to (T14).
- State the Advisor's **stance** — the conduct that makes a family willing to let one outside professional see the whole picture.
- Draw the boundary between what the practice runs and what it refers.

## Contents

### 1. Who is the client

- **One relationship, several clients.** The family is served as one relationship, but the *clients* are named people and named entities. Every household gets its own engagement letter; every entity and trust has a named decision-maker recorded on T14. "The family" never signs anything.
- **Three questions, answered in writing** on T05 and reviewed annually: who pays the fee; who is the client; who is advocating for the rising generation. If the first two answers are the senior generation and the third is "no one," the Advisor raises it with the principals before the plan is built — the rising generation's interests must have a voice, and if no one else holds it, the Advisor says so.
- **When governance exists** (a family council, an assembly, a family office), the council is the Advisor's institutional counterparty for family-level matters. Individual households remain clients in their own right, with their own confidentiality.
- **A couple** is one client unit with two people who may each speak directly with the Advisor. Either spouse may hold something in confidence from the other only where the engagement letter says so and [Firm Compliance] has approved.

### 2. Family of affinity — the working definition

The practice defines the family the way the family defines itself: descendants of a named ancestor *and* the people the family has chosen to include — spouses and partners, adopted members, and, where the family says so, long-serving trusted advisers. The test is shared purpose and a felt sense of belonging, not bloodline alone. Practical consequences:

- Both surnames appear at the head of a couple's file and agenda.
- In-laws are included in learning and in meetings by default (see SOP 43); the family's own policy on spouses, set in advance, governs exceptions.
- A member who has formally left (divorce, estrangement, a written exit) is removed from the map and the matrices at the next review; an ex-spouse with an ongoing interest through children or a trust is recorded as a counterparty, not a member.

### 3. The family map

The map (T14, Part 1) lists every member with: generation, household, roles held, entities and trusts in which they have an interest or a power, and contact preference. Roles are assigned per person, and one person usually holds several:

| Role | Meaning in this practice |
|---|---|
| Principal | A wealth creator or senior holder whose decisions set the family's direction |
| Household lead | The member who speaks for a household's day-to-day financial matters |
| Decision-maker | The person empowered to instruct on a named entity or account |
| Trustee / protector / committee member | A fiduciary or advisory role in a trust or family entity |
| Beneficiary | A current, contingent, or remainder interest in a trust |
| Elder | Recognized by the family for mediation, memory, and ritual (not simply the oldest) |
| Council member | Elected or appointed to a family governing body |
| Rising generation | Any member still forming an identity and calling apart from the wealth — not defined by age |

The entity and trust map (T14, Part 2) draws the **trustscape**: each entity and trust, its purpose, trustee, beneficiaries, protector, advisors, and next review date. Together the two maps are the family-level layer of the Total Client Profile (SOP 11).

### 4. Decision rights

A matrix (T14, Part 3) states, for every recurring matter, who decides and who is consulted: investment policy for each pool; distributions from each trust; sale or financing of a family asset; a new family loan; hiring or dismissing a family advisor; who attends family meetings; what the rising generation is told and when. Where the family has never decided this, the Advisor records the *de facto* answer, labels it as such, and raises it at the next family meeting. Principle: members are bound by rules they helped make; the Advisor never invents decision rights on the family's behalf.

### 5. Confidentiality matrix

- **Default: private per adult.** Nothing an adult tells the Advisor is shared upward, sideways, or with a trustee or a family office without that adult's written, specific consent recorded on T14 (Part 4).
- **Opt-in sharing.** Consent states what may be shared (existence of the relationship; plan; values; recaps; personal circumstances), with whom, and for what purpose. Summary-only is a valid setting.
- **Structural knowledge is family knowledge.** The existence and terms of trusts, entities, and family policies are shared with every adult member entitled to know them under the family's own rules; the Advisor encourages staged disclosure rather than secrecy, and never becomes the instrument by which one branch keeps another uninformed.
- **Inside [Firm].** Where a sister company serves a trust or another branch, the same wall applies between the Advisor's team and that company's staff; the scope memo (T25) states it.

### 6. Communication map

For each household: the primary contact, the process preference of each member (channel, frequency, meeting cadence — honored for the life of the relationship), who is copied on what, and **who you call for what** inside the practice (LA, SA, CSA). For the family as a whole: who convenes, who circulates minutes, and how the Advisor's team is told about births, deaths, marriages, illnesses, and moves. The map lives in T14 (Part 5) and is mirrored in each household's contact plan (SOP 21).

### 7. Conflict rule

Family members' interests diverge — senior and rising generations, trustee and beneficiary, one branch and another, a spouse and a family of origin. The rule:

1. **Name it.** The moment the Advisor sees that serving one member well could disadvantage another, he says so to both, in general terms that respect confidentiality.
2. **Hold the whole.** The Advisor's fidelity is to the family relationship and to each client's stated interests, not to whoever pays the largest fee or speaks loudest.
3. **Separate when needed.** If the conflict cannot be held by one professional — a divorce, a contested distribution, a business buy-out between siblings — the Advisor arranges separate representation for the member(s) affected and continues for the rest only with everyone's informed agreement, documented and cleared with [Firm Compliance].
4. **Never mediate a marriage.** The relationship between spouses is not the Advisor's to mediate; he refers to a qualified professional and steps back.

### 8. The Advisor's stance

The role the family needs is closer to a trusted counsellor than a product provider. The practice says which it is selling — knowledge and products, or the courage and judgment that help a family act on what it already knows — and never markets one while delivering the other. Conduct that follows:

- **Do nothing, most of the time.** Many family problems resolve with time and new information; the Advisor's first instinct is to wait, ask, and let the family work, intervening only when waiting would do harm.
- **Small joint successes first.** Before the family faces its hardest question, engineer a few decisions it can make together and win — a shared giving decision, a modest family loan, a meeting that ends on time with minutes.
- **Confidentiality as a security measure.** The Advisor does not acknowledge a client family in public, does not discuss one family with another, and tells families at the outset that discretion protects their privacy and their safety.
- **External-elder functions only when asked.** A family may ask the Advisor to hold a story, chair a difficult conversation, or remind the family of its own rules. He does so only when invited, and hands the function back to a family elder as soon as one emerges.
- **Transitions, not transactions.** Every recommendation is judged by how it fits the family's long transitions (generational, ownership, leadership), not by this year's tax saving alone.
- **Who advocates for the rising generation.** If the answer is "no one," the Advisor makes sure the question stays on the table until someone does.

!!! warning "Caution"
    The Advisor is never the family's judge, therapist, or referee. When a matter needs mediation, counselling, or adjudication, he refers (SOP 31) and supports the process from the side.

### 9. Out of scope — refer via the network

Drafting a family constitution; designing or chairing a family council or assembly; mediation of disputes; therapy or addiction treatment; legal documents of any kind. The practice helps the family decide whether it is ready for these (SOP 45) and brings the right specialist (SOP 31); it does not deliver them.

### 10. Scoping procedure (Mode A, brief)

1. At handoff, the CSA opens T05 and a blank T14 with everything known.
2. After the household Discovery (SOP 10) and the family discovery (SOP 41), the LA completes T14 Parts 1–2 (map and trustscape) with the principals.
3. Before the first family meeting (SOP 43), the LA drafts Parts 3–5 (decision rights, confidentiality, communication) from what the family already does, labels every *de facto* entry, and asks each adult to sign the confidentiality row that concerns them.
4. At the family meeting, the family confirms or changes decision rights and communication; the LA records the result.
5. The engagement letters (SOP 14) reference T14; the plan (SOP 30) is scoped from it.

!!! extension "Beyond Bowen"
    Ask the principals early whether any asset has ever been formally set aside as *the family's* rather than someone's personal property. Until that separation is made and written down, there is no family money to govern — only individuals' money that others hope to inherit.

## Standards

- Every adult on the map has a signed confidentiality row before the Advisor discusses that adult with anyone else.
- Every entity and trust has a named decision-maker and a next-review date.
- Every *de facto* decision right is labeled as such until the family confirms it.
- No family-level document (plan, executive summary, meeting minutes) is circulated beyond the recipients the communication map allows.
- The stance in §8 is reviewed against the service-failure log at the annual SOP review (SOP 60).

## Refresh cadence

- T14 reviewed at every annual family meeting and on any life event in SOP 22 (birth, death, marriage, divorce, incapacity, sale of a business, a member joining or leaving the enterprise).
- The three questions in §1 re-asked annually at the first progress meeting of the year.

## Owner

LA owns the model and the map; CSA maintains T14 in the [Document vault] and [CRM]; AA keeps the trustscape current from trust documents received under SOP 46.

## Mode A / Mode B

### Mode A — whole family

- This spec applies in full; T14 is a required record before SOP 41 concludes.
- Where the family has a council, the council chair is the counterparty for Parts 3–5; households sign their own confidentiality rows regardless.

### Mode B — individual within a larger team

- The Advisor does not build a family map for a family that has its own central team. He records on T05 the individual's position in the family, the FCT's members and functions, and the individual's consent about what the FCT may be told.
- If the family later invites him into its meetings or its executive summary, the stance in §8 applies unchanged; the map remains the family's, not his.
