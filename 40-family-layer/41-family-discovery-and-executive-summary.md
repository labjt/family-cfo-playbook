# 41 — Family discovery and the family executive summary

Bowen's Discovery Meeting profiles one household in ninety minutes. A family of several households, entities and generations cannot be profiled that way: no single interview reveals the system, and no single member is entitled to speak for the others. This procedure is the Mode A extension — confidential interviews with every adult and key stakeholder, one qualitative instrument to every adult, and a written executive summary the family then discusses together. It produces the family-level layer of the Total Client Profile and the raw material for the wealth management plan.

## Purpose

- Understand the family as a system — its strengths, pressures, decision habits and transitions — from every member's point of view, not only the principals'.
- Give every adult a confidential voice before the plan is built, so the plan is the family's, not the patriarch's.
- Produce a document the family can discuss at its first meeting, and the inputs for SOP 11, SOP 30 and SOP 43.

## Trigger

- A Mode A engagement is confirmed on T05, after the principals' household Discovery (SOP 10).
- An existing Mode A family that has never had a family-wide discovery (run it as the family's rediscovery).
- A trigger in SOP 22 that changes the system: a death, a business sale, a governance decision, a new generation reaching adulthood.

## Roles

Owner: LA (kickoff, every interview, synthesis, presentation) · Does: AA (interview scheduling support, notes typing from recordings, first draft of the summary tables, T18 scoring), CSA (invitations, consent forms, logistics, [Document vault]) · Consulted: an external facilitator (EEN) where the family prefers a non-family, non-advisor interviewer, or where the family is in conflict; [Firm Compliance] on recording and consent.

## Timing

| Step | When |
|---|---|
| Kickoff with the principals | Within 2 weeks of the household Discovery |
| Invitations and consent | Week 1 after kickoff |
| Interviews (60–90 minutes each) | Weeks 2–6; no more than three per day for the LA |
| T18 instrument out and back | Sent with the invitation; due before each interview |
| Synthesis and draft summary | Weeks 6–8 |
| Leader review | Week 8–9 |
| Presentation at a family meeting (SOP 43) | Weeks 10–14, at the family's convenience |
| Feed SOP 11 and SOP 30 | Immediately after the family meeting |

## Inputs

- T05 (mode, scope, who is the client, the three questions) and the household Total Client Profiles already built (SOP 11).
- A first-draft family map and trustscape (T14 Parts 1–2) from what the principals disclosed.
- Trust instruments, entity documents and any existing family documents (mission statements, constitutions, meeting minutes) the principals will release.
- T17 (kickoff checklist, interview guide, notes form, report outline) and T18 (the instrument).
- The two-way confidentiality rule from SOP 40: interview content is confidential to the interviewee; the summary reports themes, never who said what.

## Prep checklist

- [ ] Principals have agreed the goals of the process, the interviewee list, and the timeline (T17 Part 1).
- [ ] Every interviewee has received the invitation letter (T01), the confidentiality statement, the interview topics, and T18.
- [ ] Recording consent obtained for each interview or notes-only agreed; consents filed.
- [ ] Interpreter or facilitator arranged where needed; video links tested for remote members.
- [ ] The LA has reviewed each household's profile and the trustscape before that household's interviews.
- [ ] Discovery overlay menu (below) selected for this family — no more than five overlays per interview.

## Procedure

1. **Kickoff with the family leaders.** Meet the principal(s) (and the council chair if one exists). Agree: why the family is doing this; the outputs; the steps and dates; who will be interviewed — every adult member, adult in-laws by default, and the key non-family stakeholders (family counsel, the CPA, a family-office executive, a long-serving trustee); who will see the summary and in what form. Record the answers on T17 Part 1.

    !!! script "Say something like"
        "Before I can advise the family well, I need to hear from everyone in it — privately, and on the same questions. What comes back to you is what the family thinks as a whole, never who said what."

2. **Build the interviewee list and the invitations.** CSA sends each person a letter: the purpose, the confidentiality promise, the five topics, the T18 instrument with a return date, and a choice of interview times. Rising-generation members receive the same letter as everyone else.

3. **Send the instrument.** Every adult completes T18 (the family balance sheet) before their interview. The AA scores returns as they arrive; scores are not shared until the summary.

4. **Interview each person for 60–90 minutes**, using T17 Part 3. Open with the two questions that reveal a person fastest — what they are passionate about, and who they learn from. Then work the five topics:

    | Topic | What we are listening for |
    |---|---|
    | A. What's working | The family's strengths; what this person would protect at any cost |
    | B. What's coming | Pressures they see in the next two years and the next twenty |
    | C. What they'd do | How they think the family should respond, and what they would need |
    | D. What they'd learn | What they hope to understand about themselves and the family from this |
    | E. What they'd give | What they want out of the process, and what they are willing to contribute |

    Add the household-level Bowen areas (values, goals, relationships, assets, advisors, process, interests — T03) for any adult who has not had a household Discovery. Close by asking what else you should have asked.

5. **Apply the discovery overlays** the LA selected in prep — as conversation, not as a questionnaire:

    | Overlay | Use it to surface |
    |---|---|
    | Essential-questions menu (our wording): does the family decide together; is each member growing; is each generation finding its own dream; does stewardship come after calling; does the family attract or obligate; are elders being cultivated; is the long view alive | The family's self-understanding as a system |
    | The two opening questions — passion, mentor | Whether each member has a calling and the means to pursue it |
    | Generational position — G1 household, G2 siblings, G3 clans, G4+ tribe | What governance the family will soon need |
    | Three-circle attention check — family / ownership / management | Which circle is starved |
    | Qualitative-vs-financial budget comparison | What the family actually spends on people vs on money |
    | "Has any asset been formally set aside as the family's?" | Whether family money exists yet |

    !!! extension "Beyond Bowen"
        Two further overlays, both from Bonner: the **seven-pitfalls probe** (do heirs get advance notice of what is coming; does every heir have a role; is sibling competition capped; do the parents send one message; is ownership passing in time; is spouse fit discussed; is succession a shared vision or a decree) and the **culture inventory** (values, business, hobbies, education, marriage, philanthropy, politics, history, motto, traditions, reputation, vacations, properties, investments — which of these does the family actually share?). Use the **three Ds** — division, dissatisfaction, distance — as a temperature check at the end of each interview.

6. **Record and secure.** Notes typed within 48 hours from the recording or the LA's notes (T17 Part 4); recordings and notes stored in a restricted folder of the [Document vault]; nothing attributable to a person leaves that folder.

7. **Synthesize.** The LA (with AA support on tables) writes the four-section summary on T17 Part 5: **themes** (what most people said, including contradictions between generations or branches); **strengths**; **challenges and opportunities**; **recommendations and next steps**. Attach the T18 results — per capital and family averages only, with the range shown, never per person unless that person agreed. Add the draft family map and trustscape (T14) and the transitions inventory.

8. **Review with the leaders.** Walk the principals (and council chair) through the draft. Correct facts; do not soften themes. Agree how the summary is shared: full document to all adults (preferred), full document to a working group, or a presentation only — and record the choice and the reason.

9. **Present at a family meeting** (SOP 43): the LA presents themes and strengths first, challenges second, recommendations last; the family discusses; the meeting adopts one to three next steps and owners.

10. **Feed the profile and the plan.** Update SOP 11's family-level layer and T14 Parts 1–6; carry the recommendations into the advanced-planning tracker (T10) and the wealth management plan (SOP 30); open learning plans (T19) where the summary named them.

11. **Close the loop with every interviewee.** A short note from the LA: thank you, what the family decided, what happens next.

## Follow-up

- Within 48 hours of each interview: notes typed and filed; T18 score entered.
- Within one week of the family meeting: minutes (T16) and the adopted next steps circulated per the communication map; T14 updated.
- Within one month: the plan (SOP 30) reflects the recommendations; the annual meeting date is in every calendar.

## Do / Don't

**Do**

- Interview in-laws and rising-generation members with the same seriousness as principals.
- Keep every interview to the topics; the LA listens far more than he talks.
- Report contradictions between generations as themes — they are usually the most valuable finding.
- Offer an outside facilitator where the family is in conflict or where members would not be candid with the Advisor.

**Don't**

- Don't let the principals edit themes out of the summary; facts yes, themes no.
- Don't attribute a view to a person, in writing or in the room.
- Don't rank members or households on the T18 results.
- Don't run the family meeting before every interview is done — a missing voice will be noticed.

## Mode A / Mode B

### Mode A — whole family

- Required before the wealth management plan is presented to a family; the household Discoveries alone are not sufficient.
- For an existing Mode A family that has never had one, run it as the family's rediscovery in the first year.

### Mode B — individual within a larger team

- The Advisor does not run a family executive summary for a family that has its own central team.
- If the FCT invites him to contribute, he provides input on his individual's behalf with that individual's consent, and may be one interviewee among many. If asked to conduct interviews for the family, treat it as a separate, scoped engagement on T05 with its own confidentiality terms.

## Metrics

- Share of adult members and in-laws interviewed (target 100%).
- Weeks from kickoff to family-meeting presentation (target ≤ 14).
- Recommendations adopted at the family meeting that appear in T10 within one month (target 100%).
