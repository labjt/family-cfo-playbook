# 44 — Family capitals assessment

A family tracks its financial capital monthly and its other capitals never. This spec defines the practice's annual qualitative statements for a Mode A family: a **family balance sheet** that rates the four qualitative capitals, and a **family income statement** that records whether the family's people and their knowledge grew or shrank this year. Together they sit beside the financial reporting (SOP 24) so the family's picture of itself is complete.

## Purpose

- Measure the capitals the family cannot see on a custodial statement, in a form that can be trended over years and generations.
- Give the annual family meeting (SOP 43) a factual basis for the "is each of us thriving" conversation.
- Surface human-capital liabilities early enough to fund the remedy.

## Contents

### 1. The five capitals — working definitions

| Capital | What we mean | Evidence we look for |
|---|---|---|
| Human | The members themselves: health, self-knowledge, the ability to find meaningful work and to pursue their own happiness | Work that fits; recovery from setbacks; an identity separate from the wealth; freedom to leave |
| Intellectual | What members know and can do, and whether it moves between them | Financial literacy suited to each role; mentors; learning funded; knowledge shared across households |
| Social | The quality of members' relationships with each other and with the world outside | Voluntary time together; disputes settled; newcomers included; giving; cousins who know each other |
| Spiritual | The family's shared sense of purpose beyond money — humility, gratitude, a long view | A stated purpose; stories told; decisions weighed for grandchildren; rituals kept |
| Financial | The property the family owns — the tool that funds the other four | Financial statements (SOP 24); the plan (SOP 30) |

!!! extension "Beyond Bowen"
    One source counts a fourth capital differently — "organizational" capital, the family's institutions and processes — and drops the social and spiritual capitals. The practice keeps the five above and records governance maturity separately (SOP 45), so nothing is lost by the choice.

### 2. The family balance sheet — instrument design (T18)

- **Form.** Four sections, one per qualitative capital, each with about eight plain statements written by the practice for a family of substantial wealth; every adult rates agreement 1–5. A one-line definition heads each section.
- **Scoring.** Average per capital per member; per capital across the family; the family's overall profile. Show the range as well as the mean — a capital on which the generations disagree by two points is the finding.
- **Assets and liabilities.** The statements describe assets. A separate liabilities page lists, per household and without attribution in the family version, the conditions that drain human and intellectual capital — addiction, illness, dependency on distributions, a member without work that fits, unspoken conflicts — each with a status (being addressed / not yet) and an owner.
- **Confidentiality.** Individual ratings are seen by the LA and the AA only. The family sees averages and ranges. A member's own scores are returned to them privately with the family's averages for comparison.
- **First use.** In the family discovery (SOP 41), before the executive summary. Thereafter with the annual meeting pre-work.

### 3. The family income statement — the annual change

Compiled each year from four short documents each adult supplies before the annual meeting (forms in T18):

| Input | What it records | Who supplies it |
|---|---|---|
| Personal résumé update | What a good friend would know: work, passions, current mentors, what was learned this year | Every adult |
| Personal mission statement | One paragraph: what I am for; revised or reaffirmed | Every adult |
| Beneficiary self-report | For each trust: did I meet my responsibilities as a beneficiary this year (understood the trust; met the trustee; kept financial literacy growing; gave the trustee my assessment)? | Each beneficiary |
| Trustee report | For each trust: did I meet mine (purposes honored; annual meeting held; beneficiary education in place; policies implemented)? | Each trustee the family controls; [Trust Co.] where applicable |

The AA summarizes: members who took on new roles, skills or responsibilities; mentoring relationships formed; learning funded; trusts whose relationships improved or deteriorated; the human-capital liabilities resolved or newly recognized. The LA writes the one-page narrative — *did the family's people and knowledge grow this year?* — with the year's financial summary on the facing page.

### 4. Human-capital inventory

A confidential register (T14 Part 1 extended): each member's passions, current mentors, skills, roles held inside and outside the family, and the role the family could offer them. Used to fill roles (a committee seat, a property to manage, a foundation task), to find internal mentors, and to spot the member who has no role at all.

### 5. Human-capital liabilities as unfunded liabilities

!!! extension "Beyond Bowen"
    Treat a member's addiction, illness, dependency or unresolved conflict as an unfunded liability on the family balance sheet: it will come due. The family either funds the remedy — the best available treatment, a coach, a role, a loan that builds independence — or insulates the wealth from the liability through trust design (SOP 46, SOP 48). Doing neither is the choice the balance sheet makes visible.

### 6. The philanthropy share

One line: the share of the family's financial capital, and of its members' time, devoted to giving this year. Recorded whether or not the family gives through a structure; feeds the charitable section of SOP 30.

### 7. Transitions inventory

The four transitions — family (generation to generation), ownership (who holds what, and in trust or outright), board or council, management (of the business or the money) — each with a stage, a leader, and the decisions it will require (T14 Part 6). Several usually run at once; the inventory is the reason the plan's sequencing exists.

### 8. Rising-generation learning plans

For each rising-generation member (any member still forming an identity and calling apart from the wealth): the age-staged learning map (T19), this year's steps, who mentors, what the family funds, and how progress is seen — never as a test tied to money. Reviewed in the separate rising-generation conversation at the annual meeting (SOP 43), not in the money session.

### 9. The qualitative-vs-financial budget comparison

Once a year, two numbers side by side: what the family spent on preserving and growing financial capital (management, advisory, legal, accounting, custody fees) and what it spent proactively on the four qualitative capitals (learning, coaching, treatment, family meetings, mentoring, travel to gather). The ratio is discussed, not judged; the family decides whether it wants a qualitative budget at all.

## Standards

- Every adult member is invited to complete the instrument; participation below three-quarters is reported as a finding.
- Individual scores never appear in a family document; ranges and averages only.
- Statements are reviewed by the LA every three years for wording that has dated, and any change is noted so trends remain comparable.
- The liabilities page always carries an owner and a status per item; a liability without an owner is escalated to the principals.
- The income-statement narrative is one page; the financial summary faces it.
- The assessment is presented at the family meeting by the LA, never mailed cold.

## Refresh cadence

- Balance sheet: at family discovery, then every one to three years as the family chooses (annually while the family is in transition).
- Income statement inputs: annually, due two weeks before the annual family meeting.
- Human-capital inventory and transitions inventory: at every annual meeting and on any event in SOP 22.
- Learning plans: annually, and at each rising-generation member's milestone.

## Owner

LA owns the design, the narrative and the presentation; AA scores, compiles and maintains the register; CSA distributes and collects the forms and files them in the restricted folder of the [Document vault]. Where [Trust Co.] is trustee, its trust officer supplies the trustee report through SOP 46.
