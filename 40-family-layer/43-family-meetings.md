# 43 — Family meetings

A family meeting is owned by the family: it is about the whole of the family's wealth, not only the money, and the family — not the Advisor — sets its agenda. The Advisor designs, prepares and (until the family can) facilitates. This SOP holds three procedures: the standing rules every family-attended meeting runs on; the first meeting and the mission/values session; and the annual family meeting, which is the family-level counterpart of the Regular Progress Meeting.

## Purpose

- Give the family a safe, disciplined way to stay connected, learn, and decide together.
- Turn the executive summary (SOP 41) and the capitals assessment (SOP 44) into shared decisions.
- Establish the rhythm — rules, roles, minutes, cadence — that lets the family govern itself later without the Advisor in the room.

## Trigger

- A family executive summary is ready to be presented (first meeting).
- The family's annual meeting month (annual meeting).
- A transition or crisis in SOP 22 — a business sale, a leadership change, a death or incapacity — where the family needs to meet within weeks.

## Roles

Owner: LA (design, preparation, facilitation until handed over) · Does: CSA (invitations, venue, materials, childcare arrangements, minutes circulation), AA (packet: T18 results, T10 family-level tracker, reporting extracts) · Consulted: external facilitator (EEN) for the first meetings, for conflict, and for annual tune-ups; specialists (ISB/EEN) attend only their portion.

## Timing

| Meeting | When | Length |
|---|---|---|
| First meeting | 10–14 weeks after the family-discovery kickoff | Two half-days (about 3 hours each) with a free afternoon and evening between, or one full day with long breaks |
| Mission/values session | Same retreat as the first meeting, or within six months | 4–6 hours |
| Subsequent meetings while the practice forms | Every six months for two or three cycles | Half-day to one day |
| Annual family meeting | Same month each year, fixed in the family calendar (T15) | One to two days |
| Preparation | Agenda out 4 weeks ahead; pre-work due 2 weeks ahead; packet 1 week ahead | |
| Minutes | Draft within 5 days; final within 2 weeks | |

## Inputs

- The family executive summary (T17 Part 5) and T18 results (first meeting); the annual capitals refresh (SOP 44) and the family-level advanced-planning tracker (T10) thereafter.
- T14 (map, decision rights, confidentiality, communication) — attendance and what may be discussed in front of whom.
- Pre-work from every attendee (T16 Part 3): one-page member profile; ten-year statement.
- The family's mission statement, values statement, and governance documents if any exist.
- T16 for the design frame, rules sheet, run-sheets, agendas, exercise menu, story prompts, rituals calendar, minutes template.

## Prep checklist

- [ ] Design frame completed (T16 Part 1): why this meeting, what it must produce, who attends, when, how (format and learning styles), where.
- [ ] Attendee list agreed with the leaders against T14; spouses invited unless the adult children asked otherwise; members under fourteen provided with childcare or a parallel program; specialists booked for their portion only.
- [ ] Agenda drafted with the family's items first; circulated 4 weeks ahead with pre-work forms.
- [ ] Packet assembled 1 week ahead in the formats members learn from (numbers on paper for some, one-page visuals for others); heavy content split across two sessions.
- [ ] Venue meets the rules below; audio works; breaks scheduled every 75–90 minutes.
- [ ] Facilitator briefed (or the LA has rehearsed the run-sheet); chair and secretary for this meeting nominated from the family and briefed.
- [ ] Any weighty disclosure the leaders intend (an estate plan, a sale, a new trust) has been discussed with the LA beforehand, with time reserved for members to react.
- [ ] Ground rules sheet printed for signature (first meeting) or for reading aloud (later meetings).

## Procedure

### Part A — Standing rules and the conflict protocol (every family-attended meeting)

1. **Open with the rules.** At the first meeting the family discusses and adopts its own ground rules from the draft on T16 Part 2 and signs the sheet; at later meetings the chair reads them aloud. The practice's draft, in our words:

    | Rule | In practice |
    |---|---|
    | Be here | Devices away; on time; pre-work done |
    | One voice at a time | Let people finish; no finishing sentences for them |
    | Play it back before you push back | Show you understood before you answer |
    | Say "I" | Own your view; no "everyone thinks" |
    | Hold your outcome loosely | Come to decide together, not to win |
    | Drop the "but" | Begin a reply with what you heard, not with the objection |
    | Candor with care | Say the hard thing so it can be heard |
    | Park, don't drop | Not everything is settled today; parked items get an owner and a date |
    | What's said here stays here | Unless the family agrees otherwise |
    | The rules bind everyone | Including the people paying for the meeting |

2. **Appoint a chair and a secretary from the family for this meeting**, after the agenda is set so that a member with a major report is not also chairing. Roles rotate. The chair facilitates as a peer; the secretary keeps minutes (T16 Part 10) and circulates a draft within five days.
3. **Speaking order.** Where a decision affects the young most, offer the family the practice of hearing the youngest adults first and the eldest last.
4. **Enforce the rules.** The chair (or the LA, until the family can) names a breach the moment it happens, kindly and without exception. Rules enforced on everyone counteract entitlement; rules ignored teach that the meeting is theater.
5. **Run the conflict protocol when disagreement turns destructive.**
    1. Ask whether the disagreement is producing better discussion or stopping it.
    2. Understand first: ask clarifying questions only, until each side can state the other's position.
    3. Separate the people from the problem; name the source of the conflict (a misunderstanding, different needs, different values, a structure that isn't working) and ask the group to confirm the name.
    4. Invoke the relevant ground rule.
    5. Look for common ground and legitimate difference — differing values are allowed.
    6. Map allies, neutrals and opponents privately before the next vote; there are no enemies inside a family.
    7. Call a break when stuck. If the matter cannot be settled in the room, hand it to the family's elders or, failing them, to an outside mediator (SOP 31). The Advisor never adjudicates.

    !!! warning "Caution"
        A disagreement between spouses is not for this room. Pause it, and refer privately (SOP 40 §7).

### Part B — The first meeting and the mission/values session

6. **Pre-work.** Every attendee sends, two weeks ahead: a one-page profile (what a good friend would know about you — passions, current mentors, what you are working on) and a ten-year statement (where you want to be, and what the family could do to help). The CSA compiles a profile book for the meeting.
7. **First meeting — presenting the executive summary.** Chair opens; rules adopted (step 1); profile round (each person, one minute); the LA presents themes and strengths, then challenges, then recommendations; discussion in the family's own order; the family adopts one to three next steps with owners; takeaway round; close.
8. **A family standing up its own family office or council** uses the timed agenda instead (T16 Part 5): about four hours in eight blocks — open and celebrate recent accomplishments; roll call and profiles; the family office idea and the family balance sheet; break; the family-office budgets (support, property, travel to family events); constituting an investment committee and the investor-allocation idea (SOP 47); break; how decisions get made — decision rights and confidentiality (T14); close with takeaways and the next date.
9. **The mission/values session** (T16 Part 4), in our version of the classic sequence, with clock times for a family of up to about twenty (larger families break into groups that report back):

    | Step | Minutes | What happens |
    |---|---|---|
    | 1. Rules and roles | 10 | Read the rules; chair and secretary confirmed |
    | 2. Profiles | 15 | Each person: passions and mentors from their profile |
    | 3. Values lists | 25 | Each person writes the handful of values the family's long-term success depends on; posted on the wall; clustered into a shared list |
    | 4. The long view | 20 | Each person writes what they would want the family to remember mattered most, looking back from very old age; shared in small groups |
    | 5. Twenty years out | 30 | Each person describes the family two decades from now; read aloud |
    | 6. Family history | 45–60 | A small committee's draft of the family's story — crises, opportunities, memorable ancestors, past decisions good and bad — reviewed and corrected |
    | 7. How we will decide | 30 | The forms of family government, briefly; the family chooses how it will decide together and who represents whom |

    Add either or both quick exercises where energy allows: *what matters most to me and why* (a round the table, then aggregated into the values list); *founding values vs present values* (the whole family names the founders' values, each generation separately names today's, and the two are compared). A drafting committee of two or three, spanning generations, writes the first draft of the mission/values statement from the wall; the family treats it as a first draft and revisits it annually. Keep the whole session enjoyable — food, breaks, a walk.

10. **Follow the first meetings with a short cycle.** Meet again in six months, then six months after that, before moving to an annual rhythm; the facilitator attends the first two or three and then returns for annual tune-ups or new skills.

### Part C — The annual family meeting

11. **Design the agenda in three tracks** (T16 Part 6): **individual** — each member's update on life, work, plans; **generational** — each generation caucuses on what it wants to share with and learn from the others, then reports; **family-wide** — the business of the family enterprise.
12. **Run the family-wide track as a progress meeting.**
    1. Chair opens; rules read; agenda additions.
    2. A rising-generation member reads the mission/values statement aloud.
    3. **The four health questions**, in our words: Is each person here thriving? Do the leaders of each generation give their peers reason to stay and to listen? Does everyone know how they could leave the family enterprise, so that no one stays because they cannot? Are those who represent the family looking after all of its capitals, and do members feel that they are?
    4. **Capitals review** — the T18 results (SOP 44) year on year: what grew, what shrank, what the family will do about it.
    5. **Family-level advanced-planning tracker** (T10) — status of wealth transfer, protection and giving items; the family confirms drop / defer / do on each open item.
    6. **Money and structures** — the year's family-level reporting (SOP 24 extracts), any change to trusts or entities, the investor-allocation and family-bank report (SOP 47), the family's giving.
    7. **Review of representatives** — council members, trustees the family can influence, the family's advisors including this practice: what worked, what to change; the fee conversation for the practice is held here or in the principals' progress meeting, never skipped.
    8. **Decisions requested** — each with a proposer, the options, and a recommendation; decided by the family's own rules (consensus preferred; vote where the family has agreed to).
    9. **The rising generation** — a short, separate conversation, never tied to money decisions in the same session: what is your dream, and how can the family invest in it? Learning plans (T19) updated.
    10. **Next steps with owners and dates**; the next meeting date fixed; takeaway round.
13. **Stories and rituals in every annual meeting.** Reserve time for one or two stories from a designated storyteller who received the prompts in advance (T16 Part 8); keep the grandparent–grandchild lunch from which parents are absent; mark the year's passages — a birth, a coming of age, a marriage, a new elder, a death, the welcome of a new advisor — with the family's own rituals (T16 Part 9); close with a gratitude round in which each person names someone they will thank, and how.
14. **Attendance rules.** Spouses attend by default — the organizing generation asks the adult children rather than deciding for them, and spouses may join for the general and learning sessions before executive ones. Members under fourteen have childcare or a parallel program (money skills, a service project). Elders are always invited. Experts attend only their portion. A member who wants to opt out of family meetings is met with a conversation, not a shrug.
15. **Facilitation.** Use an outside facilitator for the first meetings, for any meeting following a crisis, and once a year thereafter for a tune-up; between those, the family chairs its own meetings and the LA attends as the family's advisor.
16. **Venue.** Not a parent's home or office. Somewhere with room for both work and play, convenient for everyone expected, quiet enough for older ears, with food and rest for those who traveled. The cost is a statement that the family invests in itself.

## Follow-up

- Within 5 days: draft minutes (T16 Part 10) from the family secretary, reviewed by the LA for accuracy only, circulated per the communication map.
- Within 2 weeks: final minutes; decisions entered in T10 and T14; learning plans (T19) updated; the next meeting in every calendar.
- Within 1 month: every owner of a next step has been contacted once by the CSA.
- After the first two meetings: the LA and the family's chair review what worked and adjust the design frame.

## Do / Don't

**Do**

- Put the family's items first and the Advisor's last.
- Split heavy content across two half-days; leave the evening between free.
- Give the young something real to do — reading the statement, presenting a grant, keeping the minutes.
- Build in silence after a weighty disclosure; people need time before they can respond.

**Don't**

- Don't let a performance review of the portfolio masquerade as a family meeting.
- Don't adopt rules and then let a senior member break them.
- Don't decide anything the family has not had time to discuss; park it with an owner.
- Don't let the Advisor's agenda replace the family's; if the family owns nothing on the agenda, the meeting has failed before it starts.

## Mode A / Mode B

### Mode A — whole family

- The practice designs and facilitates until the family can; every family has at least one meeting a year and a fixed month for it.
- Where the family has a council, the council plans the meeting with the LA and the council chair chairs it.

### Mode B — individual within a larger team

- The Advisor does not convene meetings for a family with its own central team. When invited to present, he attends his portion only, presents what his individual has consented to, and follows the family's rules and chair.
- If his individual asks for help preparing for the family's meeting, he prepares them privately — their update, their questions, their position on decisions.

## Metrics

- Annual meeting held in the fixed month with minutes issued within two weeks (target 100% of Mode A families).
- Share of adults (including in-laws) attending the annual meeting (target ≥ 85%).
- Next steps from the previous meeting completed or formally re-decided by the next one (target ≥ 80%).
