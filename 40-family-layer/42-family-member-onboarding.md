# 42 — Family member onboarding

When an individual member of a family the practice already serves — a rising-generation adult, a new spouse, a sibling's household, a branch — becomes a client in their own right, the consultative sequence runs again for that person, inside the family's existing profile and confidentiality rules. This procedure scales the sequence to the member, adds the family-specific steps (welcome, map, consent, learning plan), and protects the member's own identity and agency throughout.

## Purpose

- Give each member the same disciplined experience the principals received, sized to their situation.
- Bring the member into the family map, the confidentiality matrix, and the contact plan without breaching anyone else's confidences.
- Onboard in a way that strengthens the member's own path (individuation) rather than binding them to the wealth.
- Make sure no member ever learns of a trust, gift, or inheritance by accident.

## Trigger

Any of: a principal asks the Advisor to "bring in" a child, in-law, or branch; a member asks for their own relationship; a trust in which the member is a beneficiary becomes distributive or the member reaches an age of notice; a marriage, divorce, or new household changes who is a decision-maker; a member is added to a family bank, investment committee, or council role.

## Roles

Owner: LA · Does: CSA (scheduling, T05 for the member, [CRM] and [Document vault] set-up, welcome logistics), AA (member-level profile draft, plan draft) · Consulted: the principals (only within their own consent), the trustee where a trust is involved, the family's elder or council chair for the welcome ritual.

## Timing

| Step | Relative timing |
|---|---|
| Sponsor conversation and advance-notice plan | Before any contact with the member |
| Member Discovery (CCM1) | Within 30 days of the member's agreement |
| Investment plan (CCM2) | +2 weeks — or combined with Discovery on the compressed path |
| Mutual commitment (CCM3) | +1 week |
| Follow-up (CCM4) | +45 days |
| First progress meeting | +90 days, then at the member's stated cadence |
| Welcome ritual | At the next family gathering after CCM3 |

## Inputs

- Family-level Total Client Profile (SOP 11) and the family map, decision rights, confidentiality matrix, and communication map (T14).
- What the member is entitled to know: trust instruments naming them, gifts planned, any structures already in place (checked with counsel and the trustee).
- The parents' or principals' intentions for disclosure and the one-page structure summaries already prepared (T21).
- The discovery interview guide (T03) and the member-level profile outline (T04).
- The rising-generation learning map (T19) if the member is under about 30 or new to financial responsibility.

## Prep checklist

- [ ] Sponsor conversation held with the principal(s): why now, what they hope for, what they are willing to disclose, what they want kept private. Recorded on T14.
- [ ] Advance-notice plan agreed: who tells the member what, in what setting, before the Advisor's first meeting (SOP 15 and T21 for any structure involved).
- [ ] Confidentiality check: nothing from another member's profile appears in this member's packet unless that member consented on T14.
- [ ] T05 opened for the member as a client in their own right — named client, who pays, who may instruct, who advocates for them.
- [ ] Trustee informed and invited where the member is a beneficiary and the trust is relevant to the conversation (SOP 46).
- [ ] Pre-meeting letter (T01) written to the member directly, not via the parents, with the agenda and an invitation to add items.
- [ ] Personality and process notes: how this person prefers to be contacted; whether a spouse or partner attends.

## Procedure

1. **Hold the sponsor conversation.** Meet the principal(s) alone. Establish that the member becomes a client in their own right, with their own confidences, and that the Advisor will not report on the member to the parents without the member's consent. If the principals cannot accept that, do not proceed; refer the member to another advisor.
2. **Plan the disclosure before the meeting.** With the principals (and counsel if a trust is involved) decide the content, tone, and process of what the member will be told: the existence of structures first, then their workings, then figures as the member's questions warrant. Prepare or update the one-page summary (T21). The parents deliver the news; the Advisor explains the mechanics afterward.

    !!! warning "Caution"
        Never let a trustee's letter or a statement in the mail be the first a member hears of a trust. The advance-notice rule is absolute.

3. **Invite the member directly.** The CSA sends T01 to the member. The letter says who asked for the introduction, that the member's relationship is their own, what will be covered, and what to bring.
4. **Run Discovery (SOP 10) for the member.** Use T03 in full. Add the two opening questions in the family overlay — what they are passionate about and who they learn from — and the member's own view of the family's values and of the structures that touch them. Record the member's process preference and honor it for the life of the relationship.
5. **Choose the path.**
    - *Standard path* — the member has meaningful assets, entities, or a trust with distributions: CCM1 → CCM2 (+2 weeks) → CCM3 (+1 week) → CCM4 (+45 days) → progress meetings.
    - *Compressed path* — a rising-generation member with modest assets and no current trust distributions: Discovery and a short investment plan in one 90-minute meeting; commitment by signed letter within a week; a 45-day call in place of a meeting; the first progress meeting at 90 days. Say in the room that the pace will slow down as their situation grows.
6. **Build the member's profile (T04) inside the family profile.** Household level for the member; link to the family level only for the nodes the member is entitled to see. Flag every node with its confidentiality level.
7. **Draft the plan with the member's own ends.** The investment plan (SOP 13) states what belongs to the member, what is held for them in trust, and who decides what. For beneficiaries, the plan names the trustee and the distribution process (SOP 46) as inputs, not as the member's money.
8. **Offer, never impose, the development conversation.** If the member wants it, work through the "setting off" questions over several meetings: whether they feel free or dependent; whether they have found work that matters to them; whether they are a good friend and have good friends; whether they can be compassionate with themselves, express gratitude, and enjoy themselves; whether they take part in the wider community; where they want to grow. Record only what the member chooses to record.

    !!! script "Say something like"
        "Some members find it useful to talk about the life they want before we talk about the money that will support it. We can do that, or not — it's your call."

9. **Set the norms that protect individuation.** Discuss, with the principals' agreement, the family's norms: working outside the family enterprise for a period before joining it; building an identity and a livelihood that would stand without the wealth; avoiding public prominence about the family's affairs. Frame them as the family's choices, not the Advisor's rules.
10. **Choose an engagement channel.** Offer the six channels a member can take up in the family enterprise — a role in the business, in the family philanthropy, a seat on the investment committee, management of a family property, an administrative role in the family office function, or a funded venture through the family bank (SOP 47). Record the choice on T14 and T19.
11. **Consider a managed slice.** With the principals and any trustee, offer the member a small allocation — roughly half to one percent of family financial capital, or a fixed sum — to manage personally with the Advisor's coaching, so mistakes are affordable and real. Record it on T19.
12. **Run CCM3 for the member (SOP 14).** The engagement letter names the member as client, states the mode and scope, and records consent (T05). The fee basis is explained in the member's own terms. The introductions ask is reframed: "Who else in the family should we be serving?"
13. **Add the member to the family map, matrices, and contact plan (T14, SOP 21).** Update decision rights, the communication map ("who calls whom for what"), and the annual calendar (T15). Both spouses of a new household are told they may speak directly with the Advisor and are given enough understanding to do so.
14. **Arrange the welcome ritual.** Ask the family's elder or council chair to mark the member's entry at the next gathering — a few words, a story, an introduction of the member's own dreams — and mark the practice's side with a note from the LA and the member's own notebook (T08).
15. **Run CCM4 and the first progress meeting (SOPs 16 and 20).** Include one small joint decision the member and the principals can make together early (a charitable grant, a property question, a family-bank policy) so the first shared experience is a success.
16. **Build the learning plan (T19).** For members under about 30 or new to financial responsibility, agree the next twelve months of learning: what they will read, which meetings they will attend, what they will manage, who mentors them, and how progress is reviewed at the annual family meeting.

## Follow-up

- Within 24 hours of each meeting: recap to the member only (unless consent says otherwise); [CRM] updated; T14 and T19 updated.
- Within one week of Discovery: the one-week call (SOP 05) — new questions always surface once a young member has slept on it.
- Within 30 days of CCM3: welcome ritual scheduled; contact plan live; the member's notebook set up.
- Annually: the member's process preference, consent, and learning plan reviewed at their first progress meeting of the year.

## Do / Don't

**Do**

- Treat the member as a client with their own confidences from the first minute.
- Ask what the member already knows and believes about the family's wealth before telling them anything.
- Let the parents deliver news; let the Advisor explain mechanics.
- Keep the first shared decision small and winnable.

**Don't**

- Don't diagnose. The "Black Hole" patterns in SOP 04 are awareness cues for the Advisor, never labels used with the member.
- Don't give the member only the "small" parts of the family's affairs to learn on; the large, complex structures are where the teaching value is.
- Don't report a member's spending, choices, or requests to the parents — not even as reassurance.
- Don't let a welcome become a performance review.

## Mode A / Mode B

### Mode A — whole family

- The full procedure applies. The family map, matrices, and calendar are the practice's own records and are updated here.
- Where the family has a council, inform it of the member's entry as the constitution provides; the member's affairs remain their own.

### Mode B — individual within a larger team

- When another member of a large family asks to work with the Advisor, inform the FCT as agreed on the existing client's T05, then open a **separate mandate** with its own T05, its own scope rung, and its own consent — nothing flows between the two mandates without both members' consent.
- The family's own welcome and governance practices are theirs; the Advisor takes part only when invited.
- The learning plan is offered only where the mandate includes the member's own affairs (B-full or B-partial).

## Metrics

- Members onboarded in the year and share on the compressed path.
- Share of member Discoveries preceded by a documented advance-notice plan (target 100%).
- Learning plans reviewed at the annual family meeting (target 100% of plans).
